# Procter & Gamble (PG) Fundamental Analysis — FY2026 Q4 Results

## Revenue and Earnings Trend

For fiscal year 2026 (ended June 30, 2026), P&G reported net sales of $87.0 billion, up 3% year-over-year, with organic sales growth of 1%. GAAP diluted EPS came in at $6.62 (+2%), and Core EPS was $6.89 (+1%). These full-year figures show modest but positive growth across both the top and bottom line.

However, the fourth quarter tells a markedly different story. Q4 FY2026 net sales were $21.2 billion (+2%), but GAAP diluted EPS fell sharply to $1.26, down 15% year-over-year, while Core EPS declined 3% to $1.43. The data explicitly presents these figures side by side without reconciling the divergence — a full year of GAAP EPS growth (+2%) alongside a fourth quarter GAAP EPS decline of 15% implies that the year's earlier quarters must have carried disproportionately strong growth to offset the Q4 drop, or that a specific one-time item affected Q4. The provided data does not identify the cause of the Q4 GAAP EPS decline, so I cannot characterize whether it reflects a one-off charge or an emerging trend.

## Guidance and Implications

For FY2027, P&G guided to:
- Net sales growth of +1% to +3% (both all-in and organic)
- Core EPS growth of 0% to 3%, off the FY2026 Core EPS base of $6.89, implying a range of $6.89 to $7.11

The guidance explicitly flags a headwind: approximately $1 billion in after-tax cost pressure from commodities, energy, and transportation expected in FY2027. The data also states that management has indicated Q1 FY2027 EPS could decline more than 5% year-over-year.

Taken together, this guidance implies a company expecting continued low-single-digit top-line growth but facing near-term margin/cost pressure substantial enough to keep earnings growth flat to low-single-digit at best, with a particularly weak start to the new fiscal year. The combination of decelerating Q4 GAAP earnings, a stated near-term EPS headwind for Q1 FY2027, and a full-year Core EPS guidance range whose low end (0%) implies no growth at all, suggests a company navigating cost inflation more than one of strong momentum.
