**Facts both cases share without dispute:** PG has a 136-year unbroken dividend payment streak and 70 consecutive years of increases (Dividend King status). Q4 FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS fell 15% YoY to $1.26, while full-year FY2026 net sales grew 3% to $87.0B and Core EPS grew 1% to $6.89. FY2027 guidance calls for net sales growth of +1% to +3% and Core EPS growth of 0% to 3%, with Q1 FY2027 EPS guided to potentially decline more than 5% YoY. A ~$1B after-tax FY2027 headwind from commodities, energy, and transportation is disclosed. A restructuring is underway cutting ~15% of non-manufacturing headcount (~7,000 people), with $1.0B–$1.6B in pre-tax non-recurring charges, roughly half complete as of June 2026. Mid-single-digit price increases across 25% of the U.S. portfolio began in August 2026. CEO Shailesh Jejurikar took office January 1, 2026. Technically, as of August 3, 2026, PG closed at $144.49, below both its 20-day ($148.06) and 50-day ($147.33) moving averages, with RSI at 42.4 (neutral) and buy/sell signals split 2:2. Price-to-FCF is 29.0x versus Unilever's 16.9x — a large, internally consistent gap. Analyst ratings uniformly point "Buy" with no Hold/Sell reported, though price targets range widely ($145–$186).

**Where the two cases genuinely conflict on facts:** They don't, in the strict sense — neither one contests a hard number the other cites. The apparent conflicts are actually disagreements over which *unresolved* data point to trust or emphasize where the source material itself is contradictory: forward P/E (21.1x vs. 25.4x), tariff cost estimates ($600M vs. $1B), revenue ($87.0B vs. $85.26B), and average price target/upside figures that don't reconcile internally. Both cases flag these same contradictions; the bear case explicitly states it took the less favorable reading where inconsistent, while the bull case leans on the more favorable reading (or falls back to margin/dividend durability instead of valuation to avoid the issue).

**Where they interpret the same facts differently:** This is the real substance of the disagreement. (1) The Q4 GAAP EPS decline and weak Q1 guidance — the bull treats this as a "front-loaded" transition consistent with restructuring costs landing early and savings ramping later in the year; the bear treats it as evidence of a deteriorating trajectory that management itself is confirming will worsen before it improves. (2) The ~$1B cost headwind — the bull reads PG's margin cushion (26% operating margin) as capacity to absorb it without full earnings pass-through; the bear reads the *unresolved* question of whether guidance already embeds this cost as a reason FY2027 EPS could land below the guided range. (3) Restructuring and pricing actions — the bull frames these as decisive, proactive management; the bear frames the same actions as a defensive scramble carrying unquantified demand-elasticity risk, executed by an unproven CEO on his first cost cycle. (4) The Price-to-FCF premium (29.0x vs. Unilever's 16.9x) — the bull asserts the margin advantage (~7-9 points) justifies it; the bear notes this justification is asserted, not quantified, and a 7-9 point margin edge is not obviously sufficient to support a ~12-point valuation premium. (5) Technical positioning — the bull calls the current range a "base-building" pattern near a reasonable entry; the bear calls the same price action (below both moving averages, underperforming JNJ/KO on this measure) confirmation of present weakness.

**Open questions that would most change the conclusion:**
1. Whether the ~$1B commodity/energy/transportation headwind (and restructuring charges) are already embedded in the FY2027 Core EPS guidance range or additive to it — this single disclosure gap determines whether guidance is trustworthy or optimistic.
2. Whether the Q4 GAAP EPS decline reflects one-time/restructuring-related items or an underlying deceleration that will persist into FY2027.
3. Price elasticity / actual volume response to the August 2026 price increases across 25% of the U.S. portfolio — no data on this exists in either case, yet it directly determines whether the margin-defense strategy succeeds or erodes share to branded and private-label competitors.
4. Which forward P/E and price-target figures are accurate, since the valuation debate (premium vs. justified premium) cannot be resolved while the inputs themselves conflict by source.
5. Whether the 63.77% payout ratio reflects a period capturing the Q4 EPS decline, which bears on how much confidence to place in dividend coverage headed into a guided-down quarter.
