# BEAR CASE: Procter & Gamble (PG)

## 1. The most recent quarter shows earnings deteriorating sharply — and management guided for it to get worse before it gets better

Full-year FY2026 headline numbers (net sales +3%, GAAP EPS +2%, Core EPS +1%) mask what actually happened in the most recent quarter. Q4 FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS fell **-15% YoY to $1.26**, and Core EPS fell -3% to $1.43, even as net sales growth decelerated to +2%. The trajectory into the new fiscal year is deteriorating, not improving — and management itself is telling investors it will get worse first: **Q1 FY2027 EPS is guided to potentially decline more than 5% YoY**. A company guiding down for its next reported quarter, on top of a quarter that just posted a double-digit GAAP EPS decline, is not a picture of business momentum. Full-year guidance of only 0-3% Core EPS growth for FY2027 is itself anemic for a company trading at a premium multiple (see #3).

Underlying organic growth is also weak: FY2026 organic sales growth was only **+1%**, meaning the +3% reported net sales figure was substantially inflated by factors outside core volume/price/mix. FY2027 guidance of +1-3% organic growth is only marginally better than what the company just delivered, offering no clear evidence of a growth re-acceleration.

## 2. A real, quantified ~$1B cost headwind is landing on top of restructuring costs whose treatment in guidance is unexplained

P&G disclosed a **~$1B after-tax headwind in FY2027 from commodities, energy, and transportation costs** — this is a concrete, company-disclosed figure, not a data artifact. Layered on top of that is an active restructuring program cutting **~15% of non-manufacturing headcount (~7,000 people)** over two years, with **$1.0B–$1.6B in pre-tax non-recurring charges** expected. Critically, the news analysis flags that it is **not disclosed whether these costs are already embedded in the FY2027 Core EPS guidance range ($6.89–$7.11) or are additive to it**. If additive, actual EPS could land meaningfully below the guided range. Even under the generous read (fully embedded), a guided range of 0-3% growth absorbing a $1B headwind implies the underlying business is doing little more than treading water.

Separately, tariff cost estimates conflict between sources ($600M vs. $1B) — whichever figure is correct, it represents another cost pressure stacked on the commodity/energy/transportation headwind, and P&G's own guidance commentary anticipates a rough Q1 as a direct result.

## 3. The company is priced for more than this operating picture supports

Wherever the valuation data is internally *consistent* (i.e., not one of the contradictory metrics), PG screens expensive:
- **Price-to-FCF of 29.0x vs. Unilever's 16.9x** — a large, unconflicted gap. The bull argument that PG's superior operating margin justifies this is asserted qualitatively in the data but never quantified; a margin advantage of roughly 7-9 points (24-26.3% vs. ~17%) is not obviously sufficient to justify a ~12-point Price-to-FCF premium.
- Against the two peers with single, unconflicting Forward P/E figures — **Unilever (17.3x) and Kimberly-Clark (17.7x)** — PG trades at a premium under *either* of its own reported Forward P/E figures (21.1x or 25.4x). There is no version of the clean data that shows PG as cheap.
- Analyst price targets place the current share price **near the low end of a wide $145–$186 range**, and even the more optimistic average target ($178.63) implies an upside that is not overwhelming set against the earnings deceleration described above. The average 1-year upside figure (+11.67%) cannot be reconciled with which average target it derives from, meaning even the "upside" bulls would cite is not a solid number.
- No DCF or intrinsic-value estimate exists anywhere in this data to anchor a "fairly valued" argument — every valuation claim here is relative-multiple-based, and the relative multiples that are trustworthy point toward a premium, not a discount.

Paying a premium multiple for a business that just posted a -15% GAAP EPS quarter, is guiding for a >5% EPS decline next quarter, and faces a ~$1B disclosed cost headwind is a weak setup on valuation grounds alone.

## 4. Technical structure confirms the market is not rewarding the stock right now

As of the most recent technical read (2026-08-03), PG closed at $144.49, **below both its 20-day ($148.06) and 50-day ($147.33) moving averages** — a short- and medium-term bearish structure. This stands in direct contrast to peers JNJ and KO in the same dataset, which were reported trading above all their moving averages. Whatever the fundamental debate, price action is currently confirming weakness relative to peers, not strength. The stock sits closer to its support level ($138) than its resistance ($150) is far, and closer to resistance than one might like for a name being pitched as undervalued — the RSI (42.4) and mixed buy/sell signal count (2:2) offer no offsetting bullish momentum signal to counter the moving-average picture.

## 5. Price increases to defend margins carry real demand risk that the data cannot rule out

To offset tariff costs, P&G is implementing mid-single-digit price increases on **25% of its U.S. product portfolio starting August 2026**. This is a defensive margin action, not a growth initiative, and it is being executed in categories where the industry analysis explicitly notes competition from branded rivals (e.g., Colgate-Palmolive) and private-label alternatives. The news analysis is explicit that **no data on P&G's price elasticity or historical volume response to similar increases is provided**, meaning the risk that this action triggers volume/share loss cannot be dismissed — it is an open, unquantified risk sitting directly against a company that already reported only +1% organic growth in the base year.

## 6. Leadership transition adds execution risk at a delicate moment

The restructuring, tariff response, and pricing actions are all being executed under a CEO (Shailesh Jejurikar) who took office **only as of 2026-01-01** — meaning a major headcount reduction, a pricing offensive, and a cost-inflation fight are all being run by unproven new leadership simultaneously. The data provides no track record or strategic differentiation for this CEO to offset that concern.

## 7. Dividend quality — durable, but coverage cannot currently be verified as comfortable

While P&G's 136-year payment streak and 70-year raise streak (Dividend King status) are genuine structural strengths, the **payout ratio of 63.77% cannot be confirmed as based on a period that reflects the recent EPS decline** — the data explicitly does not clarify whether this figure reflects the full year or a period capturing the -15% GAAP EPS quarter. No free-cash-flow coverage ratio is available anywhere in the data to independently verify the safety margin. For a bear thesis, the point is not that the dividend is at risk today, but that **the one data point (payout ratio) offered as reassurance is itself compromised by the same EPS volatility driving the rest of this bear case**, and cannot be relied upon as clean evidence of comfortable coverage headed into a guided-down quarter.

---

## Summary of the bear thesis

PG is a business showing **decelerating, and in the most recent quarter, sharply negative GAAP earnings momentum** (Q4 EPS -15%), guided to **decline further next quarter** (Q1 FY2027 EPS possibly down >5%), facing a **disclosed ~$1B cost headwind** whose interaction with guidance is unexplained, pursuing **margin defense through price hikes with unquantified demand risk**, executing a **large workforce restructuring under new, unproven leadership**, while trading at a **premium valuation** on every internally-consistent multiple available (Price-to-FCF 29.0x vs. peers' high-teens; Forward P/E above Unilever/Kimberly-Clark under either cited figure) and showing **bearish technical structure** (below both key moving averages, underperforming sector peers JNJ/KO on this measure). The dividend's long history is real, but its near-term coverage cannot be independently confirmed as comfortable from the data provided.

**Explicit caveat:** This case is constructed entirely from internally-flagged data limitations — several headline figures used elsewhere to argue the bull case (revenue, margins, forward P/E, price targets, tariff costs) are contradictory across sources in the underlying data and cannot be fully resolved here. Where data was consistent, it was used directly (Q4 EPS decline, guidance figures, restructuring costs, technical levels, Price-to-FCF gap); where inconsistent, this case relied on the reading least favorable to the stock but flagged the contradiction rather than asserting false precision.
