Valuation Analysis: Procter & Gamble

**Headline multiples are internally inconsistent, which limits how much weight any single conclusion can carry.** The data reports P/E (TTM) of 21.66x from one source, but Forward P/E is given as both 25.4x and 21.1x by different citations within the same dataset — a nearly 20% spread on what should be a single, well-defined metric. The provided text itself surfaces this contradiction: one passage states "PG's forward P/E of 21.1x is slightly above the peer average of 20.5x," while another passage cites 25.4x against Unilever's 17.3x. These cannot both be reconciled without knowing whether the two figures use different consensus estimate sets, different fiscal-year bases, or different data-provider methodologies — none of which the source material explains. Any statement about PG being "cheap" or "expensive" relative to peers is therefore contingent on which of these two numbers is used, and the answer could plausibly flip depending on that choice.

**The peer comparison set is also unreliable on its own terms.** Colgate-Palmolive's forward P/E is reported as both 25.9x and 22.1x across sources in the same dataset — a second, independent internal contradiction. This means neither side of the PG-vs-peer comparison is stable: PG's own multiple is uncertain by ~4 points, and the peer benchmark (via Colgate-Palmolive) is uncertain by a similar margin. Only Unilever (17.3x) and Kimberly-Clark (17.7x) are cited with single, unconflicting values, and against those two peers, PG trades at a premium under every version of its own Forward P/E cited here (21.1x or 25.4x).

**Where the data is internally consistent, PG screens expensive.** Price-to-FCF shows PG at 29.0x versus Unilever at 16.9x — a large, unambiguous gap (no conflicting figures given for this metric) that would support a "expensive relative to this peer" read if taken at face value. The stated justification is an operating-margin advantage (~24% per one source, 26.3% per another — a third, smaller internal discrepancy — versus Unilever's ~17%), but the data provided contains no calculation linking the margin gap to the multiple premium; it is asserted qualitatively, not quantified. I cannot verify whether a ~7-9 point margin advantage justifies a ~12-point Price-to-FCF premium from what's given here.

**No DCF-based intrinsic value estimate is present in this data.** Without an independent valuation anchor, this analysis is confined to relative (peer-multiple) comparisons, which — as shown above — are themselves contradictory across sources.

**Conclusion on cheap vs. expensive:** The data does not support a clean, confident judgment. On Price-to-FCF, PG appears expensive relative to Unilever by a wide and internally consistent margin. On Forward P/E, PG appears to be trading at some premium to the peer average under either cited figure (21.1x or 25.4x), but the magnitude of that premium is unknowable given that both PG's own Forward P/E and one key peer's (Colgate-Palmolive) are reported with contradictory values in the source material itself. I'd flag this as a case where the underlying data quality — not the analysis — is the binding constraint: before drawing a firm relative-valuation conclusion, the discrepancy between the 21.1x and 25.4x Forward P/E figures (and between Colgate-Palmolive's 22.1x/25.9x) would need to be resolved against original source methodology, which is not available here.
