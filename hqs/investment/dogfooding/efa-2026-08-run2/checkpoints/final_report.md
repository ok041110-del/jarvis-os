# EFA (iShares MSCI EAFE ETF) — Final Research Report

*Data snapshot as of 2026-08-17 (search-collected, not live/real-time), with EFA fact sheet dated 2026-06-30. Not independently cross-checked against primary source documents.*

## Composition

EFA tracks the **MSCI EAFE Index** (developed markets ex-US/Canada, large/mid-cap, ~700 holdings, blended growth/value). The fund uses **representative sampling**, not full replication, with a minimum 80% of assets in index constituents or "economically substantially identical" instruments — leaving up to 20% potential in non-index assets (e.g., derivatives). Sector allocation detail is entirely absent from the data; only country weights are available.

## Holdings / Exposure

- **Country concentration:** Japan ~24%, UK 15%, France 9%, Switzerland 9%, Germany (5th, exact % not given); top 5 countries exceed 60% combined.
- **Single-stock concentration:** Top holding ASML Holding at 3.22%, followed by HSBC (1.62%), Roche (1.39%), Novartis (1.32%), AstraZeneca (1.18%) — low individual-stock risk. Top-5/top-10 cumulative holding weight not provided.
- **Sector allocation, credit exposure, currency-hedging status:** not in the data — cannot be assessed.
- Note: "~700 holdings" (diversification) and ">60% in 5 countries" (concentration) are not contradictory — they measure different axes (issuer count vs. geographic weight) — but the data doesn't explain the mechanism producing this combination.

## Cost / Tracking

- Expense ratio: **0.32%**.
- Competitor VEA (same index) is described as having a lower expense ratio and "generally" lower tracking error — but VEA's actual expense ratio and tracking-error figures are **not given**, only the qualitative comparison.
- **No EFA-specific tracking-error number exists anywhere in the data.** This is a key gap: the cost/tracking case against EFA rests on an asymmetric comparison (competitor has qualitative advantage cited; EFA has no counter-figure).
- Sampling methodology is a *plausible* mechanical source of tracking difference, but the data does not establish this causally for EFA specifically.

## Macro

- The only macro-adjacent figure available is **industry-wide** US fund flow data (week ending 2026-08-05): $27.28B aggregate net inflow, composed of $43.70B ETF inflows offset by $16.42B mutual fund outflows. This is **not EFA-specific or region-specific** and has no established read-through to international/EAFE fund flows.
- **No data exists** on ECB/BOJ policy rates, Eurozone/Japan growth or inflation, or EUR/USD or JPY/USD conditions — despite these being directly relevant to EFA's underlying exposure. This is flagged as the most significant gap in the dataset; no macro/rate-environment judgment can be supported.

## Bull Case

Strong 1-year return (20.06%) with broad issuer diversification (~700 holdings, largest position only 3.22%); a 25-year track record through a full market cycle; independent third-party validation via Morningstar Silver rating; a meaningful semiannual income stream (3.36% or 2.75%, take either); liquid access to major developed markets in one ticket; a fund structure (80% index-constituent minimum, large sample size) that plausibly bounds tracking drift; and a broader industry backdrop of ETF-wrapper inflows (though not EFA-specific).

## Bear Case

A higher fee (0.32%) than at least one same-index competitor (VEA) with an unfavorable, if only qualitative, tracking-error comparison and no EFA-specific number to rebut it; structural latitude of up to 20% off-index holdings with no tracking-error data to confirm this isn't producing slippage; geographic concentration (>60% in five countries, ~24% Japan) that undercuts the "700 holdings = diversification" framing, compounded by a total absence of sector data; unquantified, unconfirmed currency exposure (JPY/GBP/EUR/CHF); a severe 61% historical max drawdown and 20.8% volatility against only mid-single-digit long-run returns; internally conflicting yield and return figures with no authoritative resolution; and a macro backdrop that is effectively a black box, meaning no data supports a current entry thesis.

## Synthesis

Both cases draw on the **same facts** and do not dispute any figure — they diverge entirely in **interpretation**. The same 700-holdings/60%-country-concentration pairing reads as "diversified" (bull) or "concentration dressed as diversification" (bear); the same 6.55–6.65% return and 20.8%/61% risk profile reads as "durable track record" or "mediocre risk-adjusted payoff." Neither side resolves the two unexplained numeric conflicts (yield: 3.36% vs. 2.75%; return: 6.55% vs. 6.649%) — both flag them as open rather than picking a side.

The single most consequential missing datum is an **EFA-specific tracking-error figure**, which would either validate or neutralize the core cost/tracking bear argument. Close behind: confirmed currency-hedging status, sector allocation, reconciliation of the yield/return discrepancies, the timing of the 61% drawdown, and current European/Japanese macro conditions. None of these are present in the data provided.

**Bottom line:** the data is internally consistent on the facts it does contain but materially incomplete for a risk-adjusted judgment (no Sharpe ratio, no beta, no EFA-specific tracking error, no hedging confirmation, no sector data, no regional macro data). Any conclusion beyond what is stated above would not be supported by the source material.
