# Synthesis: EFA (iShares MSCI EAFE ETF)

## Where the two cases actually agree on facts

Both sides draw on the same underlying data and don't contest any figure directly — the disagreement is entirely about **interpretation**, not **facts**. Shared inputs:

- 1-year total return: 20.06%
- Since-inception (2001) annualized return: 6.55% or 6.649% (unresolved discrepancy)
- Annualized standard deviation: 20.8%; max drawdown since inception: 61.0%
- ~700 holdings; top holding ASML at 3.22%; top 5 countries >60% (Japan ~24%, UK ~15%, France ~9%, Switzerland ~9%)
- Expense ratio 0.32%, higher than VEA (same index), with VEA "generally" described as having lower tracking error — no absolute tracking-error number for either fund
- 80% minimum index/economically-identical-asset requirement (implying up to 20% latitude)
- Morningstar Silver rating, Foreign Large Blend category (645 funds), as of 2026-07-31 — no star rating or in-category numeric rank given
- Yield: 3.36% or 2.75% (unresolved discrepancy)
- Currency-hedging status: not stated anywhere in the data
- No sector-allocation data available
- ICI fund-flow data (week of 2026-08-05) is industry-wide, not EFA-specific

## Where they diverge — interpretation, not fact

| Same fact | Bull reading | Bear reading |
|---|---|---|
| ~700 holdings, top 5 countries >60% | Broad diversification limiting single-stock risk | Geographic concentration dressed up as diversification; ~24% single-country (Japan) bet |
| 6.55–6.65% since-inception return | 25-year live track record demonstrating durability | Mediocre payoff given 20.8% vol and 61% drawdown |
| 20.8% vol / 61% drawdown | Acknowledged as real risk, but framed as the cost of the 20% 1-year return | Framed as disqualifying relative to the return earned |
| 0.32% expense ratio vs. VEA | Cost of a Silver-rated, sampling-based fund | Unjustified premium with no EFA tracking-error data to defend it |
| 80% index-constituent minimum | Structural limit that keeps drift bounded | Structural latitude (up to 20%) that could produce unmeasured slippage |
| Morningstar Silver | Independent third-party quality signal | Limited endorsement — no star rating, no numeric rank |
| Unhedged/unconfirmed FX exposure | Noted as a live, unquantified factor | Escalated to a standalone risk given "principal risk factor" framing elsewhere in the data |

Neither side actually contradicts a number the other cites — both list the same 6.55%/6.649% and 3.36%/2.75% conflicts and flag them as unresolved rather than picking a winner. The real split is in **framing**: the bull treats absent data (hedging status, EFA-specific tracking error, sector breakdown) as caveats layered on an otherwise constructive case; the bear treats those same absences as themselves evidence against the fund, on the theory that a fee-bearing, sampling-based product should have to demonstrate what it's doing.

## What would most change the conclusion

1. **An EFA-specific tracking-error figure.** This is the single most consequential missing number — it would either validate the "you're paying more for the same thing, worse" bear argument or neutralize it entirely. Right now the comparison to VEA is asymmetric by construction (VEA has a number, EFA doesn't), which the bear leans on structurally rather than substantively.
2. **Confirmed hedging status.** Both cases treat this as unresolved, but it directly determines how much of the 20.8% volatility / 61% drawdown history is equity risk versus currency risk — and how much of that risk an investor is actually taking today versus historically.
3. **Reconciliation of the yield and return discrepancies**, and their sourcing/dates. Until these are reconciled, neither the income case nor the long-run-return case (bull) nor the "mediocre risk-adjusted return" case (bear) can be stated with precision.
4. **Sector allocation data**, currently absent entirely — needed to know whether the country concentration compounds into a sector concentration.
5. **Timing/context of the 61% max drawdown** (no date range given) and current macro conditions in Japan/UK/Eurozone (rates, inflation, growth, FX), which the bear notes are a "black box" in this dataset. Without these, there's no way to assess whether the historical drawdown is a relevant analog to present conditions or a stale artifact of a specific past crisis (e.g., 2008).

## Bottom line on the data itself

The provided data is **internally consistent on facts but incomplete on the inputs needed for a risk-adjusted judgment**: it lacks a Sharpe ratio, beta, EFA-specific tracking error, hedging confirmation, sector breakdown, and region-specific macro conditions. Both cases are legitimate readings of the same limited data; the choice between them currently rests less on what the data shows than on how much weight an investor assigns to unresolved gaps versus the fund's demonstrated 25-year track record and Silver rating. This synthesis does not constitute and should not be read as a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold EFA.
