# Apple Inc. Fundamental Analysis — Q3 FY2026 (June Quarter, Reported 2026-07-30)

**Revenue and earnings trend.** Apple reported revenue of $109.4B for the June quarter, up 16% year-over-year and above the consensus estimate of $108.79B — described in the sourcing as a June-quarter record. Diluted EPS came in at $2.02, up 29% year-over-year and ahead of the $1.88 consensus. Note that $0.11 of that EPS figure is attributed to a tariff refund, which is a non-recurring item — the data does not specify what EPS would have been excluding it, so the underlying earnings growth rate is somewhat overstated by the headline number as given. Operating income rose to $35.7B from $28.2B in the prior-year quarter, a year-over-year increase of roughly 27%, broadly consistent with the strength in revenue and EPS.

**Segment trend.** The revenue beat was driven by hardware, not services. iPhone revenue was $54.3B, up 22% year-over-year and itself a June-quarter record — a notably strong growth rate for Apple's largest product line. Mac revenue of $10.35B also beat expectations ($8.74B). Services revenue, by contrast, came in at $30.74B, which missed the $31.22B estimate. Since Services is generally Apple's higher-margin segment, a miss there alongside hardware outperformance has mixed implications for margin mix — the provided data does not include a gross margin or segment-margin breakdown, so I cannot characterize the margin trend beyond this qualitative observation about mix.

**Margin trend.** The data provided does not include gross margin, Services margin, or hardware margin figures — only total revenue and operating income. Operating margin can be roughly inferred (about 32.6% of revenue in the June quarter versus a computed ~29.9%-equivalent share of the prior year's smaller revenue base, based on the operating income figures given), suggesting some operating leverage, but this is a derived approximation rather than a reported figure, and the data does not break out what drove it (cost of goods, opex, or the tariff refund embedded in EPS). I'd flag this as an area where the provided data is insufficient for a confident margin-trend judgment.

**Guidance and implications.** For the September quarter, Apple guided to revenue growth of 9–11% year-over-year, a deceleration from the 16% just reported. The guidance explicitly cites two headwinds: an FX drag of 2.5 percentage points, and supply constraints affecting iPhone, Mac, and iPad. This combination implies that part of the September-quarter slowdown is external/transitory (currency) while part reflects a company-specific operational constraint (supply) rather than a demand problem — though the data provided doesn't indicate the expected duration or cause of the supply constraints, so it's unclear whether this is a one-quarter issue or a longer pattern.

**Data limitations (explicit).** This analysis is built solely from a summarized set of figures sourced from web-search results (MacRumors, CNBC, Yahoo Finance, MacObserver) rather than primary filings (10-Q or earnings call transcript), and the underlying source material was not independently verified. No gross margin, Services-segment margin, cash flow, balance sheet, iPad-specific, or Wearables-specific figures were provided, which limits how completely the margin trend and overall segment picture can be assessed. The figures represent a snapshot collected around the report date rather than confirmed final/restated numbers. As instructed, I have not filled these gaps with assumed or estimated data beyond the operating-margin approximation flagged above, and I am not offering a buy/sell view, as that is outside this analysis's scope.
