# BULL CASE: Procter & Gamble (PG)

## 1. An unmatched dividend fortress, even on cautious reading

PG has paid a dividend without interruption for 136 years and raised it for 70 consecutive years — a record spanning two world wars, the Depression, 2008, and the pandemic. That is direct evidence that dividend continuity sits above short-term earnings noise in this company's capital-allocation priorities, not a promise about the future. The 10-year dividend CAGR of ~5% matches KO's 3–5% range, and the payout ratio (63.77%) sits comfortably below KO's 77–80%, in the middle of the peer set. Whatever uncertainty exists about the *exact* payout-ratio period, no data point in any section suggests the streak itself is at risk — the 70-year track record is the single most consistent, cross-verified fact in the entire dataset.

## 2. Growth and EPS are still positive — and guidance points to stabilization or improvement

FY2026 revenue grew +3% to $87.0B and full-year GAAP EPS grew +2% ($6.62), Core EPS +1% ($6.89). Even the weaker Q4 print was still a growth quarter on revenue (+2%). Management's own FY2027 guidance calls for continued growth: revenue +1–3% and Core EPS +0–3% ($6.89–$7.11) — meaning the *low end* of next year's Core EPS guide is simply flat-to-current, not a decline, and the high end represents real acceleration. A company navigating ~$1B in commodity/energy/freight headwinds while still guiding to positive EPS growth is signaling confidence in its ability to offset cost pressure.

## 3. Management is actively attacking the controllable variables

Two concrete, already-in-motion actions directly target the headwinds the bear case would cite:
- **Cost structure**: A restructuring program cutting ~15% (≈7,000) of non-manufacturing roles is already 50% executed on schedule (3,500+ of 7,000 by mid-2026, one year into a two-year plan) — this is on-pace execution, not a stalled initiative.
- **Tariff offset**: Starting August 2026, PG implemented mid-single-digit price increases across 25% of its U.S. product portfolio — a direct, already-executing lever against the tariff cost estimate (whether $600M or $1B), consistent with the standard, proven playbook consumer staples companies use to defend margins.

Fresh leadership (CEO transition effective January 2026) coincides with this restructuring push, consistent with a narrative of active portfolio and cost reshaping rather than passive drift.

## 4. Analyst consensus is directionally unanimous: Buy

Despite wide dispersion in the exact target-price figures across sources, every source agrees on direction — consensus is "Buy"-weighted, with an average one-year upside estimate of +11.67% cited in the sentiment data. Even the most conservative reported average target ($163.3) sits above the technical section's reference price levels. This directional agreement, sourced independently across multiple providers, is a more robust signal than any single disputed number.

## 5. Technical picture is neutral, not broken — and offers a defined risk/reward box

The stock is not in a confirmed downtrend by the data's own account: RSI at 42.4 is explicitly described as neutral (neither overbought nor oversold), and the composite signal count is balanced (2 buy / 2 sell = Neutral) rather than bearish. The reported trading range ($138 support / $150 resistance) with price near the midpoint ($144.49) describes a stock that has not broken down — it offers a definable entry zone with identified support before any deterioration is confirmed.

## 6. Premium valuation is a byproduct of a genuine profitability edge

Even the most bearish-leaning valuation reads still put PG's operating margin (24–26.3%) meaufsAribly above Unilever's (~17%), and gross margin at 51.2% — a real, cross-referenced profitability gap, not merely an assertion. A premium multiple (on P/E and especially Price-to-FCF) attached to a business generating structurally higher returns on sales than its largest named peer is a defensible, if not fully quantifiable, premium — "you get what you pay for" is at least directionally supported by the operating-margin data, even though the exact fair size of that premium can't be pinned down from these sources.

---

**Necessary caveat on this bull case**: several figures underpinning points above are internally inconsistent across the source data (Forward P/E 21.1x vs 25.4x; target price averages $163.3 vs $178.63 on identical claimed sample sizes; tariff cost $600M vs $1B; revenue $87.0B vs $85.26B). The bull case above deliberately leans on the facts that are *directionally consistent across sources* (dividend streak, Buy-consensus direction, positive guidance, active cost/pricing actions, margin gap vs. Unilever) rather than on any single disputed point estimate. No DCF-based intrinsic value or FCF-based dividend coverage ratio was provided in any section, so this case cannot and does not claim PG is undervalued in absolute terms — only that the qualitative and directionally-consistent evidence supports a constructive stance.
