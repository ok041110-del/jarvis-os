**Cost and Tracking Analysis — iShares MSCI EAFE ETF (EFA)**

EFA carries a total expense ratio of 0.32%. This is the only cost figure provided, and no separate figure for EFA's own tracking error is present in the data. That absence is significant for a tracking-quality assessment: expense ratio and tracking error are related but distinct — expense ratio is a known, contractual drag, while tracking error captures the full deviation between fund and benchmark returns, including effects like sampling, cash drag, foreign withholding tax treatment, and trading costs around rebalancing. Without EFA's own tracking-error number, no direct statement can be made about how closely EFA actually follows the MSCI EAFE Index in practice.

The data does include a comparative claim: Vanguard's VEA, which tracks the same underlying index, is reported to charge a lower expense ratio and to generally exhibit lower tracking error than EFA. This is presented as a relative comparison sourced from the material, not as an independently verified figure for either fund, and the underlying VEA tracking-error number itself is not given — only the qualitative claim that it's "generally lower."

On construction method, the data notes that EFA is understood to use a representative sampling (optimization) approach rather than full replication of the index. Representative sampling is a plausible structural source of tracking error in general, since a sampled portfolio will not perfectly mirror every constituent and weight of the benchmark, especially during periods of index reconstitution or market stress. However, the data explicitly does not establish a direct causal link between EFA's sampling methodology and any specific tracking deviation for this fund — this connection is noted as a general possibility, not a documented fact about EFA specifically.

**Explicit data gaps:** No numeric tracking-error figure for EFA is provided. No numeric tracking-error figure for VEA is provided either — only a qualitative "generally lower" comparison. No data on currency hedging status or FX sensitivity, no sector-level breakdown, no fund flow data, and no confirmation that the sampling-methodology point was cross-checked against MSCI's or iShares' own documentation. The source material itself flags unreconciled discrepancies elsewhere (dividend yield figures differing by ~0.6 percentage points and average annual return figures differing slightly across sources), which further limits confidence in treating any single number here as precise. Given these gaps, a firm conclusion on how well EFA tracks its benchmark cannot be supported by the data as provided — only the expense-ratio comparison and the qualitative, source-asserted claim about relative tracking error can be reported.
