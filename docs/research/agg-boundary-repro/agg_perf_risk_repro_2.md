## Performance & Risk Analysis: iShares Core U.S. Aggregate Bond ETF (AGG)

These two figures are not directly reconcilable from the data provided — the YTD figure's cutoff date is explicitly not specified in the source, so it's unclear whether the negative YTD and positive 1-year figures reflect different measurement windows, a rebound within the year, or simply a discrepancy in reporting cutoffs across sources. On the yield side, the fund shows a 30-day SEC yield of 4.51% (as of 2026-06-30) and a yield to maturity of 4.38% — these are broadly consistent with each other, as expected for two related forward-looking yield measures. However, the data's limitations section separately notes that a "dividend yield" figure is reported inconsistently across sources as 3.85%, 4.04%, and 4.07%. This is a real inconsistency worth flagging explicitly: dividend yield (a trailing, distribution-based measure) is conceptually different from SEC yield and YTM (forward-looking, sourced from the fund's official filings), so the discrepancy may partly reflect different metrics being compared rather than pure reporting noise — but the data does not clarify which is the case, and no single reconciled dividend-yield figure can be stated with confidence.

**Interest-Rate Sensitivity (Duration)**

AGG's effective duration is reported at 5.80 years, with a weighted average maturity of 8.11 years. Using the duration-based approximation cited in the source, a 1-percentage-point move in interest rates would be expected to move the fund's price by roughly 5.85% in the opposite direction. This is a meaningful, intermediate-to-long level of rate sensitivity: a rise in rates would be expected to pressure the fund's price, while a decline in rates would tend to support it, all else equal. It's worth noting that this 5.85% figure is described as a duration-based estimate rather than an empirically realized price move, and it is a linear (first-order) approximation — actual price sensitivity can differ at larger rate moves due to convexity, which is not addressed in the data.

**Volatility and Market Correlation**

The fund's 3-year standard deviation is reported at 5.51%, and its 3-year equity beta is 0.23. Taken together, these suggest AGG has historically exhibited moderate volatility in absolute terms but a low degree of co-movement with equity markets — consistent with its identity as an investment-grade bond index fund rather than an equity-correlated instrument. The data explicitly frames this low beta as evidence of low correlation to stocks, which is a reasonable reading of that single statistic, though beta alone doesn't fully characterize the correlation structure (e.g., it doesn't quantify R² or behavior during market stress specifically).

**Data Gaps and Caveats (as stated in the source)**

The provided data explicitly acknowledges several limitations that should be carried into any interpretation:
- The underlying iShares fact sheet PDF and Bloomberg index methodology were not directly cross-checked; the analysis rests on web-search summaries only.
- No tracking-error figure was found.
- The exact as-of date for the -0.47% YTD return is not specified.
- A narrative about "rate hike prospects" in the macro commentary is noted as resembling similar commentary for another fund (SCHD) from a different source/time, and this similarity has not been cross-verified — it may or may not reflect independent confirmation of a shared macro view.
- The data is a search snapshot as of 2026-08-13, while the underlying fact sheet metrics (duration, weighted average maturity) are dated 2026-06-30, so there is a roughly six-week lag between the two.

**Summary**

Within the bounds of the data given, AGG displays a moderate historical volatility profile (5.51% 3-year standard deviation) with low equity-market correlation (0.23 beta), and a clearly quantified interest-rate sensitivity via effective duration (5.80 years), implying meaningful exposure to rate moves (~5.85% price impact per 1pp rate change, per the cited estimate). Its yield metrics (SEC yield 4.51%, YTM 4.38%) are internally consistent, but the return series (YTD vs. 1-year) and the dividend-yield figures are either insufficiently dated or inconsistent across sources, and should not be treated as fully reconciled. No tracking-error data is available, limiting assessment of how closely the fund has tracked its benchmark index. This analysis does not constitute and should not be read as a buy/sell recommendation.
