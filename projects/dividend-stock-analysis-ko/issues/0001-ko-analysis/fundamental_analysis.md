## Fundamental Analysis: The Coca-Cola Company (KO) — Q2 2026

**Revenue and earnings trend**

Coca-Cola reported Q2 2026 net operating revenue of $13.4B, up 7% year-over-year from $12.5B, with organic revenue growth of 6% — meaning the bulk of the top-line increase came from underlying business performance (pricing/mix and volume) rather than from currency translation or acquisitions, though there was some gap between the reported 7% and organic 6% figures suggesting a modest net positive contribution from other factors (e.g., currency or structural items) in the quarter itself. Comparable EPS came in at $0.97, up 11% year-over-year — EPS growth outpacing revenue growth by roughly 4 points, consistent with margin expansion (discussed below) doing some of the work.

Unit case volume grew 5% year-over-year, which the data describes as benefiting from an easier prior-year comparison, favorable weather, and an activation effect tied to the FIFA World Cup. Trademark Coca-Cola volume also grew 5%, characterized in the sourcing as the strongest growth rate in 17 years excluding the COVID recovery period — a notable data point, though the "17-year" framing comes from secondary reporting (Yahoo Finance/TipRanks) rather than the primary release, so it should be read as reported rather than independently verified here. POWERADE grew 8% globally, indicating strength in the sports-drink category specifically.

Because several of the tailwinds cited (easier comps, weather, a one-time sporting event) are cyclical or non-recurring in nature, the data does not allow a clean separation of how much of the volume growth reflects durable demand versus temporary factors — the sourcing flags these drivers explicitly but doesn't quantify their individual contribution to the 5% figure.

**Margin trend**

Comparable gross margin expanded 120 basis points to 60.5%, and comparable operating margin expanded 90 basis points to 31.2%. Both are moving in the same direction, with gross margin expanding more than operating margin — implying that some of the gross-margin gain was reinvested below the gross-profit line (e.g., in opex, marketing, or other costs) rather than flowing through entirely to operating income. The data does not specify the drivers of the margin expansion (pricing, input costs, mix, productivity), so the underlying cause can't be characterized from what's provided.

**Guidance and its implications**

Coca-Cola raised its 2026 guidance to: organic revenue growth of about 5%, comparable currency-neutral EPS growth of 7–8%, and comparable EPS growth of 9–10% (versus a 2025 base of $3.00). The company also guided to a currency effect of +1 point on net revenue and +3 points on EPS for 2026, describing currency as a net favorable factor this year — notable since currency is often a headwind for a multinational like Coca-Cola, so this is a reversal or tailwind relative to what might be a typical assumption. The gap between the currency-neutral EPS growth guide (7–8%) and the reported EPS growth guide (9–10%) is consistent with, and roughly matches the magnitude of, the stated +3 point currency benefit. Full-year 2026 free cash flow guidance is $12.4B.

Taken together, an organic revenue guide of ~5% against Q2's already-delivered 6% organic growth implies either an expectation of deceleration in the back half of the year, or that management is guiding conservatively relative to first-half momentum — the data doesn't clarify which, and no explicit full-year or second-half commentary beyond the raised guidance figures themselves is provided.

**Data limitations relevant to this analysis**

- The 7% reported vs. 6% organic revenue growth gap is not explained in the source data (currency, acquisitions, or other factors are not broken out).
- The specific drivers behind the 120bp gross margin and 90bp operating margin expansion (pricing vs. cost vs. mix) are not provided.
- The "17-year" volume-growth framing is sourced from secondary reporting, not the primary earnings release, and is presented as-reported rather than verified.
- The data does not quantify how much of Q2 volume growth was attributable to each cited driver (comp base, weather, World Cup), nor does it address whether these effects are expected to persist or reverse in coming quarters — which bears directly on whether the "5%" full-year organic guide implies deceleration.
- No commentary is provided in the source data on how the $20B IRS tax dispute or tariff pressures — both flagged elsewhere as open items — factor into the guidance figures.
- This analysis is based on a search-derived snapshot as of 2026-08-14, not primary filings (10-Q) or the full earnings release, and has not been independently cross-checked against those documents.
