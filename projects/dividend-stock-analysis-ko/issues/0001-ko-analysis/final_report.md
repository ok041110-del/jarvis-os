# The Coca-Cola Company (KO) — Integrated Research Report

**Snapshot date:** 2026-08-14 | **Basis:** Web-search-derived data collected at a point in time, not live market data, and not independently cross-checked against primary filings (10-Q/10-K/SEC documents) unless otherwise noted.

---

## 1. Fundamental Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Q2 2026 net operating revenue was $13.4B, up 7% YoY from $12.5B, with organic revenue growth of 6%. The 1-point gap between reported (7%) and organic (6%) growth is not explained in the source data (currency, acquisitions, or other factors are not broken out). Comparable EPS was $0.97, up 11% YoY — outpacing revenue growth by roughly 4 points, consistent with margin expansion.

**Volume.** Unit case volume grew 5% YoY, attributed to an easier prior-year comparison, favorable weather, and a FIFA World Cup activation effect. Trademark Coca-Cola volume also grew 5%, reported (via secondary sources — Yahoo Finance/TipRanks, not the primary release) as the strongest rate in 17 years excluding COVID recovery; this framing should be read as reported, not verified. POWERADE grew 8% globally. The data does not quantify how much of the 5% volume growth came from each driver, nor whether these effects will persist.

**Margins.** Comparable gross margin expanded 120bps to 60.5%; comparable operating margin expanded 90bps to 31.2%. The larger gross-margin gain versus operating-margin gain implies some reinvestment below the gross-profit line, but the specific drivers (pricing, input costs, mix) are not specified in the data.

**Guidance.** 2026 guidance was raised to ~5% organic revenue growth, 7–8% comparable currency-neutral EPS growth, and 9–10% comparable EPS growth (vs. a 2025 base of $3.00). Currency is now guided as a net tailwind: +1 point to revenue, +3 points to EPS — a reversal of the currency headwind typical for a multinational. FY2026 free cash flow guidance is $12.4B. The ~5% full-year organic guide sits below Q2's already-delivered 6%; the data does not clarify whether this reflects conservative guidance practice or an expected deceleration as one-time tailwinds fade.

**Data gaps:** the 7%/6% revenue reconciliation, the drivers of margin expansion, the individual weighting of volume-growth drivers, and any linkage between guidance and the $20B IRS dispute or tariff pressure are all absent from the data.

## 2. Dividend Quality

**Track record.** KO is described as a Dividend King. However, sources disagree on the consecutive-increase streak: 54 years (MacroTrends) vs. "60+ years" elsewhere — a gap too large to be rounding, and unresolved by the data provided. Both figures support the same qualitative conclusion (a long, uninterrupted increase streak), even though the precise number cannot be confirmed.

**Recent growth.** Recent dividend growth is 3–5%, described as consistent with a mature large-cap consumer staple. A claim that this "comfortably outpaces inflation" is asserted but not backed by any inflation figure or real-growth calculation in the data, so it cannot be verified.

**Coverage.** The payout ratio is reported at 77.24%–80.1%, notably higher than the comparator JNJ's 46.19% cited in the same source material; one source classifies >75% as "high." Critically, no free-cash-flow coverage ratio is provided, and it is not specified whether the payout ratio is calculated on GAAP net income, adjusted earnings, or FCF. This is a material gap: it's not possible to separately assess earnings-based versus cash-flow-based dividend safety.

**Unanswered questions:** FCF dividend coverage; the earnings basis of the payout ratio; the quantitative impact of the $20B IRS dispute and tariffs on future dividend capacity; verification of the "outpaces inflation" claim; and the exact consecutive-increase streak length.

## 3. Valuation

**Headline:** KO trades at a premium on every earnings-multiple comparison in the dataset. Trailing P/E (as of 2026-07-23) was 25.45x; forward P/E ranges 24.59x–25.61x across sources (a ~1-point spread, a minor cross-source inconsistency), averaging in the mid-25x range.

Against every benchmark provided:
- **Sector** (Consumer Defensive, 22.53x): +13%
- **Industry** (Beverages–Soft Drinks, 19.33x): ~+30%
- **Broad market** (S&P 500, 20.81x): ~+21%
- **Named peers** (NTM P/E — PepsiCo ~16x, Diageo ~15x, Keurig Dr Pepper ~12x): +60% to +113%, the widest and most consistent gap in the data.

**Justification offered.** LTM gross margin of 61.9% and an unspecified "return on capital advantage" are cited as qualitative reasons KO might deserve a premium. No calculation, regression, or benchmark in the data sizes what premium those figures would justify — this supports the *direction* of an argument, not its *magnitude*.

**Major gap: no DCF.** No discounted-cash-flow or other intrinsic-value estimate exists in this dataset. The valuation conclusion therefore rests entirely on relative multiples, with no independent absolute-value anchor — a genuine limitation, not a minor caveat.

**Other inconsistencies:** price targets range from $80.83 (Barchart) to $95.40 (another source), an ~18% spread; technical indicators are presented inconsistently (see Technical, below); the dividend streak is reported at 54 vs. 60+ years. No 2026 sugar-tax update was found, and the earnings impact of the IRS dispute/tariffs is unquantified.

**Summary judgment:** KO screens as expensive on every relative multiple in the data, most starkly against direct peers. The margin/ROIC rationale for the premium is plausible but unquantified, and the absence of a DCF means the "cheap vs. expensive" conclusion rests on relative multiples alone.

These aren't directly comparable (different timeframes), but the divergence is notable: short-term momentum looks stretched while longer-term momentum looks moderate — consistent with sources' own "bullish but overbought" characterization.

**Critical gap:** no current/last traded price is stated anywhere in the data, so none of the moving averages or RSI readings can be anchored to where price actually sits.

**Conclusion:** The technical picture is directionally suggestive of an uptrend with mixed-to-overbought momentum, but the unreconciled moving-average figures and absence of a current price mean no precise or verified technical read is possible; no buy/sell judgment is offered.

## 5. Industry and Competitive Position

**Scale.** As of 2026-06-10, KO's market cap (~$360B) was ~70% larger than PepsiCo's (~$211B).

**Global category.** KO holds "40%-plus" share of global non-alcoholic beverages vs. PepsiCo's ~30%; the precise upper bound of KO's share is not specified.

**U.S. CSD.** KO holds 47.1% of U.S. carbonated soft drinks vs. a PepsiCo figure of 16% for U.S. "liquid beverage" — the data explicitly flags these as not directly comparable (different category definitions), so the ~31-point gap should not be read as a like-for-like measure of competitive advantage.

**Regional variation.** India: KO portfolio (incl. Thumbs Up, Sprite) ~60% vs. PepsiCo 30–35%. Developed Europe: KO CSD share 45–50% (narrower lead, but still leading).

**Category growth.** The global soft drink market is projected to grow from $283.8B (2026) to $340.8B (2031), a 3.7% CAGR — moderate, mature-industry growth. Health-oriented reformulation, flavor innovation, and functional/prebiotic beverages are flagged as trends, without company-specific performance data.

**Gaps:** no time-series data (so share trajectory — expanding, stable, eroding — cannot be assessed); no competitors beyond PepsiCo are covered (effectively a two-player comparison); no 2026 sugar-tax information; no linkage between the IRS dispute/tariffs and competitive spending or share.

The dispute size is substantial relative to company size. The data contains **no information on whether this liability is reserved for**, how it might affect cash flow, or an expected resolution timeline — a clear information gap distinguishing "size of potential exposure" from "how the company is financially absorbing it."

**Fanta/Sprite "100% natural flavors" class action.** A false-advertising-type suit. No claim amount, litigation stage, or precedent outcome is provided, making financial materiality impossible to assess from this data. (General patterns suggest labeling suits often resolve via label changes or settlements, but this is outside-data context, not evidence about this specific case.)

**Tariff pressure (aluminum/metals).** Packaging-cost pressure was reportedly present in market narrative, yet the stock reportedly remained resilient — suggesting the market may have priced in the risk or offset it with other positives (pricing power, portfolio diversification), but no quantified cost impact or pass-through data is provided.

**Sugar tax.** No 2026 developments were found in the data — an explicit information void on a factor described elsewhere as structurally important to CSD economics.

## 8. Bull Case

1. **Accelerating momentum:** Q2 revenue +7% (organic +6%), EPS +11% — growth outpacing revenue, signaling real operating leverage. Broad-based volume strength (unit case +5%, Trademark Coca-Cola +5%, POWERADE +8%).
2. **Margin expansion, not compression:** gross margin +120bps to 60.5%, operating margin +90bps to 31.2%, occurring alongside volume/pricing strength.
3. **Raised guidance with a currency tailwind:** ~5% organic revenue growth, 9–10% EPS growth, +3-point EPS currency benefit, $12.4B FCF guide — signals of management confidence.
4. **Category-leading scale:** ~70% larger market cap than PepsiCo; #1 position in global share, U.S. CSD, India, and Europe, supporting durable pricing power in a stable (3.7% CAGR) category.
5. **Decisive analyst sentiment:** 84% Buy-equivalent, 0 Sell; even the most conservative price target implies upside.
6. **Technical structure:** both (unreconciled) moving-average datasets show bullish stacking; aggregated "Strong Buy" signal; weekly RSI "healthy," with the overbought daily RSI plausibly a byproduct of strong quarter momentum rather than trend exhaustion.
7. **Dividend legacy:** regardless of the 54- vs. 60+-year discrepancy, KO is a Dividend King by any reading, with $12.4B FCF guidance as a funding base.
8. **Market resilience to flagged risks:** stock reportedly held up despite tariff pressure; none of the three flagged risks ($20B IRS dispute, labeling suit, tariffs) carry a quantified negative impact in the data.
9. **Qualitative premium justification:** high LTM gross margin (61.9%) and cited "return on capital advantage," combined with current-quarter execution, offered as the basis for the valuation premium — though unquantified.

**Bull-case honesty check:** no DCF exists to size the valuation premium; price-target/technical data is internally inconsistent; the 5% full-year guide sits below Q2's 6% delivered organic growth with no data clarifying conservatism vs. deceleration; risk items carry no quantified financial linkage; payout ratio (77–80%) is high versus the JNJ comparator with no FCF-coverage confirmation.

## 9. Bear Case

1. **Valuation priced for perfection:** 13–113% premium across sector/industry/market/peer comparisons, with the peer gap being the "starkest and most consistent signal in the data." The margin/ROIC justification is explicitly unquantified, and **no DCF exists anywhere in the dataset** to confirm the premium is earned rather than a mispricing. Price targets disagree by up to 46%.
2. **Growth quality is largely cyclical/borrowed:** Q2's 5% volume growth is explicitly tied to an easier comp, favorable weather, and a one-time World Cup effect — none recurring. The "17-year" volume framing is secondary-sourced and unverified. Full-year guidance (~5% organic) sits below Q2's delivered 6%, and given the non-recurring nature of Q2's drivers, deceleration is arguably the more parsimonious reading than conservative guidance. Margin-expansion drivers are undisclosed, so repeatability can't be judged.
3. **Dividend quality concerns:** payout ratio near/above 80% (vs. JNJ's 46.19%), decelerating 3–5% dividend growth, an unverified inflation-outpacing claim, and **no FCF coverage data** anywhere in the source material — plus an unresolved 54-vs-60+-year streak discrepancy that undermines confidence in the underlying fact set.
4. **Technical weakness:** daily RSI 76 is unambiguously overbought — the single most concrete, single-timeframe reading available. The two moving-average datasets contradict each other numerically, and with **no current price stated anywhere**, the "uptrend" cannot be verified against reality.
5. **Large, unmapped legal/cost risk:** the $20B IRS dispute remains unresolved with no data on reserve treatment, cash-flow timing, or resolution schedule. Tariff pressure is acknowledged but unquantified beyond "stock remained resilient" — an observation, not proof of full cost pass-through. The Fanta/Sprite suit is unquantifiable. Sugar tax status for 2026 is a total blank despite being called structurally important.
6. **Sentiment reliability issues:** despite 84% Buy-equivalent ratings, price targets disperse by ~46%, and two sources attach the identical "+9.9%" implied upside to two different absolute targets — an internal inconsistency the sentiment analysis itself flags as unresolved.
7. **Industry backdrop:** a mature, 3.7% CAGR category is not typically associated with an expanding premium multiple, and KO's headline U.S. share-advantage statistic (47.1% vs. 16%) is explicitly flagged by the industry data itself as not like-for-like — undermining one of the most commonly cited "dominance" statistics.

**Bear-case core thesis:** KO is priced at a 20–113% premium with no independent valuation check, on a growth print substantially explained by non-recurring factors that guidance itself suggests won't persist, with dividend safety undermined by a high payout ratio and no FCF-coverage data, overbought short-term momentum, and a $20B unresolved liability plus unquantified regulatory risk sitting outside the figures used to justify the premium.

## 10. Synthesis

**A. Data inconsistencies (not interpretive disagreements — genuine source problems):**
- Dividend streak: 54 vs. 60+ years (6-year gap, unexplained).
- Price targets: $80.83–$104 range, including two sources attaching an identical "+9.9%" upside to two different absolute targets.
- Moving averages: two incompatible datasets, with no current share price given anywhere to anchor either.
- Minor: payout ratio (77.24–80.1%) and forward P/E (24.59x–25.61x) ranges are narrow enough not to change conclusions materially.

The one signal that survives this noise intact is the *direction* of analyst sentiment (84% Buy-equivalent, 0 Sell) — both bull and bear cases treat this as real, even while disputing what it's worth in price terms.

**B. Same facts, opposite readings:**

| Shared fact | Bull reading | Bear reading |
|---|---|---|
| Q2 volume +5%, organic rev +6%, EPS +11% | Broad operating leverage, accelerating momentum | Substantially attributable to easy comp, weather, World Cup — non-recurring |
| 2026 guide (~5%) below Q2's 6% | Conservative guidance practice | More parsimonious reading is deceleration |
| Margin expansion (+120bps/+90bps) | Multi-front strength | Driver undisclosed — repeatability unknowable |
| Valuation premium (13–113%) | Justified by scale/margin/ROIC | Justification unquantified; no DCF exists |
| Tariff resilience in stock price | Market judges pricing power sufficient | Price behavior ≠ demonstrated cost pass-through |
| No quantified IRS/litigation/tariff impact | Mild positive (absence of demonstrated damage) | The unquantified silence is itself the risk |

**C. One point where the bear case is better grounded in the data itself:** the industry report explicitly states that KO's 47.1% U.S. CSD share vs. PepsiCo's ~16%/~30% figures are not a like-for-like comparison. This is not an interpretive stance — it's the source material self-flagging a methodological flaw in one of the bull case's headline scale statistics, which the bull case does not directly address.

**D. Open questions that would most change the picture, if answered:**
1. Is there an independent DCF/intrinsic-value estimate? (Largest gap overall.)
2. What specifically drives the 120bps/90bps margin expansion?
3. How much of Q2 volume growth reverses in H2 2026?
4. What is KO's FCF-based dividend coverage (not just payout ratio)?
5. Is the $20B IRS liability reserved for, and on what timeline?
6. What is KO's current share price? (No technical read is verifiable without it.)
7. Is KO's market share expanding, stable, or eroding over time?
8. What is the 2026 status of sugar-tax regulation?

**Bottom line:** The bull and bear cases largely diverge not over disputed facts but over how to interpret *acknowledged gaps* in the data — non-recurring growth drivers read as "momentum" or "borrowed strength"; unquantified risks read as "priced in" or "unpriced overhang"; an unquantified valuation premium read as "earned" or "unfalsifiable without a DCF." Both readings are internally consistent with what's provided; the data itself does not contain enough to adjudicate between them. Given the unreconciled dividend-streak, price-target, and technical figures, any conclusion resting on a precise number (an exact price target, a specific technical level) should be treated as directionally suggestive at best, not reliable.
