## Valuation Analysis: The Coca-Cola Company (KO)

**Headline picture: KO trades at a premium on every earnings-multiple comparison in this dataset.**

On a trailing basis, KO's P/E as of 2026-07-23 was 25.45x. On a forward basis, the figure ranges narrowly across sources: 25.61x, 25.22x, and 24.59x depending on provider — a spread of roughly one point, which is not large in absolute terms but is worth flagging as a minor cross-source inconsistency rather than a single settled number. Averaging these puts KO's forward P/E in the mid-25x range.

Against every comparison set provided, that multiple sits meaningfully above the benchmark:

- **vs. sector**: 13% above the Consumer Defensive sector average (22.53x).
- **vs. industry**: 25.22x forward vs. 19.33x for Zacks' Beverages–Soft Drinks industry average — roughly a 30% premium.
- **vs. broad market**: 25.22x forward vs. 20.81x for the S&P 500 — roughly a 21% premium.
- **vs. direct peers (NTM P/E)**: PepsiCo ~16x, Diageo ~15x, Keurig Dr Pepper ~12x. KO's ~25.6x is not just modestly above these — it is 60–113% higher than each named beverage peer. This is the starkest and most consistent signal in the data: across sector, industry, market, and named-peer comparisons, KO is priced richer on earnings, and the peer gap is the largest of the four.

**The case offered for the premium, and its limits.** The data cites LTM gross margin of 61.9% and an unspecified "return on capital advantage" as qualitative justification for why KO might deserve to trade above its peers and the market. This is a plausible rationale — high, stable margins and returns are often rewarded with a multiple premium — but the material provided does not include a calculation, regression, or benchmark showing what premium those specific margin/return figures would justify. In other words, the data supports the *direction* of an argument (some premium may be warranted) but supplies no basis for judging whether a ~20–30% premium over industry/market, or a 60%+ premium over the cheapest peer (Keurig Dr Pepper), is the *right size* of premium. Treat this as an assertion in the sourcing, not a demonstrated valuation conclusion.

**A significant gap in the data: no DCF cross-check.** The material explicitly notes that, unlike a comparable analysis done for JNJ, no discounted-cash-flow or other intrinsic-value estimate is included here for KO. This means the "multiples say expensive, DCF says X" comparison — a standard way to triangulate whether a premium is a market mispricing or a reflection of fundamentals — cannot be performed with this dataset. Any valuation conclusion here rests on relative (peer/sector/market) multiples alone, with no independent absolute-value anchor. This should be treated as a real limitation, not a minor omission.

**Other inconsistencies and gaps worth naming explicitly, since they bear on confidence in the "expensive" read:**

- **Price targets vary widely** across sources, from $80.83 (Barchart) to $95.40 (another source) — a spread of about 18%. Without knowing each source's methodology, this range can't be reconciled, and it's not clear whether either end is more credible.
- **Technical indicators are internally inconsistent** in presentation (daily RSI of 76 vs. weekly RSI of 61.23; differing moving-average conventions across sources) without documentation of methodology, so they add noise rather than corroboration to the valuation picture and are not reliable enough to weigh here.
- **Dividend increase streak** is reported inconsistently (54 years vs. 60+ years depending on source) — a factual detail, not a valuation multiple, but another sign that the underlying sourcing is not fully reconciled.
- No data is provided on the quantitative earnings impact of the $20B IRS tax dispute or tariff pressure, both of which could affect forward earnings (and hence the forward P/E denominator) but are unquantified here.
- No 2026 update on sugar-tax developments was found, leaving a potential regulatory/margin risk factor unaddressed in this snapshot.

**Summary judgment on the metrics as given:** On every P/E-based comparison in this dataset — sector, industry, broad market, and named peers — KO screens as expensive, with the peer comparison (vs. PepsiCo, Diageo, Keurig Dr Pepper) showing the widest and most consistent gap. The qualitative margin/return-on-capital argument offered as justification is directionally reasonable but not quantified, so it cannot be used to confirm or size the "fair" premium. Because no DCF or intrinsic-value estimate is present in this data, the analysis cannot check whether the multiple premium is corroborated or contradicted by an independent absolute valuation — that is a genuine gap, not just a caveat, and it means the "cheap vs. expensive" conclusion here rests on relative multiples only. Given the additional dispersion in price targets and unreconciled technical/dividend-history figures across sources, the overall data quality supports a directional read (rich on relative multiples) but not a precise or fully corroborated one.
