## Cost and Tracking Analysis: iShares Core U.S. Aggregate Bond ETF (AGG)

**Cost structure.** AGG's expense ratio is 0.03%, which is stated in the data to be lower than both QQQ (0.18%) and SCHD (0.06%). On an all-in cost basis, AGG appears to be one of the cheaper products among those referenced in the source material. The data also notes that AGG is described as having a lower expense ratio than competing aggregate bond funds BND and LAG, but no specific comparative figures for BND or LAG are provided, so the magnitude of that cost advantage cannot be quantified from what's given here.

**Tracking error.** No specific numeric tracking-error figure is provided for AGG in the source data. This is an explicit gap — the same gap noted for QQQ and SCHD in the broader dataset — so nothing here can support a quantitative judgment about how tightly AGG has actually tracked the Bloomberg U.S. Aggregate Bond Index.

**Structural reasons tracking difference may arise.** Although no tracking-error number is given, the data does describe a mechanism that could plausibly produce tracking difference: AGG uses a sampling strategy rather than full replication, and this sampling approach specifically avoids lower-liquidity bonds within the index. The source material frames this as a case where the construction method itself — not just trading costs or fees — can be a source of tracking error, since the fund does not hold every constituent bond in the underlying index. This is characterized in the data as a distinct type of cause compared to the liquidity/bid-ask-spread and securities-lending-income explanations given for QQQ and SCHD, reflecting a bond-ETF-specific limitation: full replication of a broad bond index is often impractical, so sampling is used, and sampling inherently introduces the possibility of deviation from the index's return.

**Data limitations that bear on this analysis.** Several caveats in the source material limit how much confidence can be placed in the above: (1) the underlying material is a summary of web search results and was not cross-checked against the full iShares official fact sheet (PDF) or the Bloomberg index methodology document directly; (2) the fact sheet reference date (2026-06-30) lags the search snapshot date (2026-08-13); (3) a YTD return figure (-0.47%) appears in the broader dataset without a specified as-of date; and (4) the dividend yield figures cited elsewhere in the material (3.85%/4.04%/4.07%) vary by source, though this particular inconsistency pertains to yield rather than cost or tracking figures directly.

**Bottom line on what the data supports.** The data supports a statement that AGG has a very low stated expense ratio (0.03%) relative to the other funds referenced. It does not support any quantitative statement about actual tracking error, since no such figure was found. It does support a qualitative point, explicitly stated in the source material, that AGG's sampling-based construction (as opposed to full replication) is a mechanism that can itself contribute to tracking difference, distinct from the liquidity/spread and lending-income mechanisms cited for other funds. No buy/sell judgment is offered, consistent with the scope of this analysis.
