**AGG (iShares Core U.S. Aggregate Bond ETF) — Concentration and Exposure Profile**

A preliminary note on methodology: single-name concentration, the metric relevant to equity funds (e.g., a top-10-holdings percentage), is not a meaningful concept for AGG. The fund holds roughly 13,224 individual bonds, so no single issuer or security represents a material share of the portfolio. For this fund, the operative exposure lens is sector allocation, credit-quality distribution, and maturity structure, and the analysis below is organized around those three dimensions.

**Sector/Asset-Type Composition.** The portfolio is dominated by U.S. Treasuries at 46.26%, the largest single sector allocation by a wide margin. The second-largest allocation is MBS Pass-Through securities (agency mortgage-backed securities) at 23.43%. Together, Treasuries and agency MBS account for approximately 70% of the fund, meaning roughly seven-tenths of the portfolio sits in government or government-backed instruments. The remaining ~30% is spread across corporate credit and other structured/municipal exposure: Industrial corporate bonds (14.28%), Financial Institutions corporate bonds (7.90%), Utility bonds (2.53%), and CMBS (commercial mortgage-backed securities, 1.39%), with additional smaller allocations not itemized in the data provided. Within this structure, corporate credit exposure (Industrial + Financial + Utility) totals approximately 24.71% of the fund, based on summing the individual figures given.

**Credit-Quality Distribution.** The credit-quality breakdown shows AAA at 2.21%, AA at 73.60%, A at 11.85%, and BBB at 11.63%, with no allocation reported at BB or below. This is a conservative, investment-grade-only profile: the data shows zero high-yield/sub-investment-grade exposure. Notably, the AA category (73.60%) is by far the largest credit bucket — substantially larger than the Treasury sector allocation alone (46.26%), which implies that agency MBS and/or other government-related holdings are likely also carrying AA-equivalent ratings within this classification, though the provided data does not break out the credit-rating composition by sector, so this connection cannot be confirmed directly from what's given. The near-total absence of AAA-rated paper (2.21%) alongside the heavy Treasury weighting also stands out as a potential inconsistency worth flagging rather than resolving, since Treasuries are conventionally treated as AAA/equivalent in many rating frameworks — the data as provided does not explain this discrepancy, and I won't speculate on the underlying rating methodology.

**Maturity Distribution.** The maturity structure shows the largest bucket at 7–10 years (23.23%), followed by 3–5 years (21.11%), 5–7 years (13.93%), and 1–2 years (11.98%). These four buckets sum to approximately 70.25% of the fund; the data provided does not include figures for other maturity bands (e.g., 0–1 year, 10–20 year, 20+ year), so the full maturity distribution cannot be characterized — roughly 30% of the portfolio's maturity allocation is not accounted for in the data given. Based on the buckets provided, the portfolio's duration exposure is weighted toward intermediate maturities (3–10 years covers roughly 58% of the fund among the reported buckets), but a complete duration/maturity picture would require the missing bands.

**Summary.** AGG's exposure profile is that of a broadly diversified, investment-grade-focused core bond fund: heavily weighted to government and agency-backed paper (~70% combined Treasury and MBS), with no single-name concentration risk given its ~13,224-bond portfolio, an investment-grade-only credit profile (no BB-or-below exposure reported), and a maturity structure concentrated in intermediate terms based on the four buckets provided — though roughly 30% of the maturity distribution is not covered by the supplied data. The credit-quality data also contains an unresolved discrepancy (AA at 73.60% versus AAA at just 2.21% despite the ~46% Treasury weighting) that the source data does not clarify.
