## Nestlé S.A. (NESN.SW / NSRGY) — Fundamental Analysis, H1 2026

**Revenue and top-line growth**

Nestlé reported H1 2026 sales of CHF 43.1 billion, with organic growth of +3.6%, split between real internal growth (RIG) of +1.5% and pricing of +2.1%. This means slightly more than half of organic growth came from price increases rather than volume/mix gains. Q2 2026 alone showed organic growth of +3.7% (RIG +1.8%, pricing +1.9%), a marginally better RIG contribution than the H1 average, suggesting a modest sequential improvement in volume-driven growth within the quarter, though this is a single data point and not confirmed as a trend.

Growth was geographically uneven: emerging markets excluding China grew organically +7.1% (RIG +3.9%), more than three times the developed-market organic growth rate of +2.3% (RIG +0.6%). This indicates developed markets are growing overwhelmingly through pricing (RIG of only +0.6% out of +2.3% total) while emerging markets ex-China are seeing genuine volume/mix strength. The data explicitly excludes China from the emerging-market figure, and no standalone China figure is provided, so China's contribution to the overall picture cannot be assessed from this data.

**Earnings trend**

This is the most notable — and most ambiguous — element of the release. Reported (GAAP) net profit was CHF 3.5 billion, down 31.4% from CHF 5.1 billion in H1 2025. However, underlying (adjusted) net profit was CHF 5.7 billion, down only -2.4% in reported currency and actually up +3.4% on a constant-currency basis. These two profit measures move in opposite directions relative to the prior year — one shows a sharp decline, the other shows underlying stability-to-growth. The provided data does not disclose what accounts for the roughly CHF 2.2 billion gap between GAAP and underlying net profit (e.g., no mention of impairments, restructuring charges, disposals, or other one-off items), so I cannot attribute the GAAP decline to any specific cause. Given this, the earnings trend should be read as unresolved: whether Nestlé's underlying profitability is holding up (as the adjusted figure implies) or genuinely deteriorating (as the GAAP figure implies) cannot be determined from the data provided.

**Margin trend**

The data does not include actual H1 2026 margin figures (e.g., no reported UTOP margin percentage, gross margin, or comparison to H1 2025 margin). The only margin-related information is forward-looking guidance that full-year UTOP margin is expected to improve versus 2025. There is therefore insufficient data here to describe an actual realized margin trend for H1 2026 — only a company expectation for the full year, which cannot be verified against reported figures in this dataset.

**Cash flow and cost program**

Free cash flow improved to CHF 3.4 billion in H1 2026 from CHF 2.3 billion in H1 2025, a clear year-on-year improvement. The "Fuel for Growth" cost-savings program has delivered CHF 1.7 billion cumulatively against a 2026 target of CHF 2 billion, implying the program is close to (roughly 85% of) its full-year target at the half-year mark, which — if the pace continues — would suggest the target is achievable, though the data doesn't specify how savings are phased across the year.

**Guidance**

For full-year 2026, Nestlé maintained organic growth guidance at 3–4% but narrowed the range (the data doesn't state the prior, wider range, so the degree of narrowing can't be quantified). Management also guided to UTOP margin improvement versus 2025 and free cash flow above CHF 9 billion. Against H1 FCF of CHF 3.4 billion, achieving >CHF 9 billion for the full year would require a materially stronger second half (roughly CHF 5.6 billion+), which is plausible given normal seasonal cash-flow weighting toward H2 for consumer staples companies, but this pattern isn't confirmed in the provided data.

**What this implies, and where the data falls short**

Taken together, the data depicts a company growing organic sales in the low-to-mid single digits, still price-driven in developed markets, with meaningfully stronger volume growth in emerging markets ex-China. Cash generation and cost-cutting appear to be progressing. The central open question is profitability: the GAAP net income figure suggests a significant year-on-year decline, while the underlying figure suggests near-stability or slight improvement, and the source material gives no explanation for this divergence. Any conclusion about earnings quality or trend direction would require the specific reconciling items between reported and underlying net profit, which are not present in the data provided — I'd flag this as the key gap before drawing further conclusions from the earnings line. Additionally, no actual (non-guidance) margin data exists in this dataset, so the margin trend cannot be independently assessed beyond management's forward statement.
