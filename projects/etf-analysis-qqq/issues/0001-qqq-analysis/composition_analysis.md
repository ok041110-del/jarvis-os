## QQQ Composition & Index Methodology Analysis

Invesco QQQ Trust, Series 1 tracks the **Nasdaq-100 Index**, which comprises the 100 largest non-financial companies listed on the Nasdaq exchange, selected and ranked by market capitalization.

**Weighting methodology.** The index uses a "modified market-capitalization-weighted" approach, administered by Nasdaq, Inc. as index provider. Under this methodology, larger companies generally receive proportionally larger index weights, but the "modified" element imposes caps on individual constituent weights — a mechanism commonly used in such indices to prevent excessive concentration in the largest names and to maintain diversification requirements (e.g., for regulated investment company compliance). The specific numeric weight caps or capping thresholds are not present in the data provided, so I cannot describe the precise concentration limits.

**Recent methodology change.** According to the data, effective May 1, 2026, the index changed how it calculates company rankings for the purpose of index eligibility and composition: rankings now use total market capitalization (including both listed/outstanding and unlisted shares) rather than listed shares alone. Notably, the actual weight calculation for constituents continues to use listed shares only — it is the ranking/selection step that changed, not the weighting step itself. This revised methodology was first fully applied at the June 22, 2026 rebalancing.

**Implication of this change (as stated in the source data):** broadening the ranking basis to total market cap rather than listed-share market cap could, in principle, alter which companies qualify for inclusion or how they rank relative to peers, since companies with large unlisted share counts (e.g., dual-class structures, closely held stakes) would be ranked using a larger capitalization figure than under the old methodology. The data does not specify which, if any, constituents were actually added, removed, or reranked as a result, so I cannot state a concrete compositional outcome — only the mechanical nature of the change.

**Rebalancing cadence.** The index undergoes quarterly rebalancing of weights and an annual reconstitution (full review of eligible constituents), per the data provided.
