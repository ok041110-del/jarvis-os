## QQQ Sector & Geographic Exposure Analysis

**Sector concentration.** All three cited sources agree that the Invesco QQQ Trust (tracking the Nasdaq-100) carries a heavy concentration in the Technology sector, but they report meaningfully different magnitudes: Source A puts Technology at 57.8%, Source B (described as a 2026 Q2 average) puts it at 64.75%, and Source C puts it at 66.9%. That is a spread of roughly nine percentage points between the low and high estimates — large enough that it changes the qualitative picture of "majority tech" versus "supermajority tech." The data provided does not explain this discrepancy; it may stem from differences in the date each figure was measured, differences in sector-classification methodology (e.g., GICS version or vendor-specific reclassification of companies that straddle Technology and Communication Services or Consumer Discretionary), or some combination, but none of that is confirmed in the source material. This should be treated as an open discrepancy rather than resolved.

Source C additionally provides a fuller picture of the rest of the portfolio: after Technology (66.9%), Consumer Discretionary is the next-largest sector at 17.6%, with every remaining sector individually under 4%. Sources A and B do not provide a comparable full breakdown, so it isn't possible to cross-check Source C's non-Technology figures against the other two sources.

**Relative to a broader benchmark.** Source B also states that QQQ's Technology weighting (64.75%) is well above the S&P 500's Technology weighting (43.28%) for the same period, indicating that QQQ is substantially more tech-concentrated than a broad-market index. This comparison is only available from Source B; Sources A and C do not provide an S&P 500 comparison point.

**Performance linkage.** For 2026 Q2, the data indicates Technology-sector returns within QQQ were 45.14%, compared with 29.07% for the Technology sector within the S&P 500, and the source material characterizes QQQ's heavier tech weighting as having had a large effect on the fund's overall performance during that period. No decomposition (e.g., attribution analysis isolating weighting effect from stock-selection effect) is provided, so this should be read as a directional observation rather than a quantified attribution.

**Geographic exposure.** No explicit geographic exposure figures are present in the data provided. The fund tracks Nasdaq-100 listed companies, which would suggest a U.S.-listing-centric composition, but the source material does not include any data that verifies or quantifies this — no country or region weightings, and no discussion of revenue geography (i.e., even U.S.-listed constituents could derive significant revenue internationally, but nothing in the data addresses this). This is a data gap, not an inference that can be supported from what's given.

**Data limitations, stated explicitly:**
- The sector percentages are only web-search-derived summaries (Fool.com, tradingsim.com, and an Invesco quarterly highlights reference) and have not been cross-checked against an Invesco official fact sheet or SEC filing.
- The three sector figures for Technology conflict with each other (57.8% / 64.75% / 66.9%), and the cause of the conflict is not identified in the source data.
- No geographic/regional exposure data is present at all.
- No tracking error figures were found in the source material.
- No quantitative volatility metrics (standard deviation, beta) are present — only qualitative commentary elsewhere in the broader dataset.
- All figures are a snapshot as of the search date (2026-08-13) and reflect the most recent reporting found at that time, not live or real-time data.

Given the unresolved three-way discrepancy in the Technology sector weighting and the complete absence of geographic data, this analysis should be read as indicating a *directionally* tech-heavy, Nasdaq-100-based portfolio, without a single precise, verifiable concentration figure or any regional breakdown.
