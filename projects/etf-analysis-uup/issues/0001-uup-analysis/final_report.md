## 1. Composition

UUP is designed to track the Deutsche Bank Long USD Currency Portfolio Index (Excess Return version). The source data, however, contains two materially different characterizations of what that index actually does, and this report does not resolve the conflict by picking one:

- One source describes the benchmark as a rules-based index composed solely of long positions in USDX (U.S. Dollar Index) futures contracts traded on ICE Futures U.S. — exposure achieved through a single futures contract that itself references a fixed basket.
- A second source describes the fund as replicating dollar strength against a basket of six currencies (euro, yen, pound, Canadian dollar, Swedish krona, Swiss franc) via futures positions taken in those individual currencies — exposure built from multiple separate currency futures rather than one consolidated USDX future.

These are not equivalent mechanisms. A single-instrument USDX-futures replication and a multi-instrument, per-currency futures replication can behave differently with respect to roll timing, per-leg liquidity, and how precisely the fund's return matches the referenced basket's return. Because the data does not clarify which description is accurate — or whether both have applied at different points in the fund's history — this is treated as an open inconsistency in the record, not a resolved fact.

**Structural features, regardless of which description is correct:**

- *Legal/tax structure*: UUP is organized as a commodity pool and, legally, a limited partnership. Holders receive K-1/K-3 tax forms rather than 1099s.
- *Collateral and return composition*: The futures positions are collateralized with Treasury securities, money market funds, and T-Bill ETFs. Total return is therefore a composite of (a) the underlying futures/index exposure and (b) interest income generated by the collateral pool. The fund is not a pure, unlevered proxy for spot dollar movements — the interest-rate environment affecting collateral contributes to total return independently of currency-futures performance. No data is given on the relative weighting or performance contribution of each component.

**Data limitations:** collected via web search on 2026-08-16, not live; the index-description conflict above is unresolved; no figures for currency weightings, collateral allocation, volatility, beta, or tracking error; dividend yield is reported inconsistently (3.26% vs. 3.39%); a YTD figure dated 2026-03-20 sits alongside an undated later report of a multi-year DXY low.

**Not applicable to this structure:** single-name concentration, sector weighting, and credit-rating breakdowns are equity/bond-fund concepts with no analogue here. UUP is not a bond portfolio exposed to individual issuer credit risk; forcing such metrics onto a currency-futures position would be inappropriate.

**Collateral structure — data absent.** Futures-based products typically hold Treasuries, money market funds, and short-term Treasury ETFs as collateral, generating separate interest income. No breakdown of this collateral pool's composition or maturity structure is provided, so its duration, maturity diversification, interest-rate sensitivity, and liquidity profile cannot be assessed.

**Quantitative risk metrics — absent.** No volatility, beta, or tracking-error figures appear in the sources. **Summary.** The direction of UUP's exposure (long USD vs. a six-currency basket) is clear; the concentration within that direction — which currency dominates, how collateral is structured — is not determinable from the data provided. Traditional concentration/credit metrics don't apply to this fund's structure in the first place, and the one metric that would apply (per-currency weighting) is simply absent from this dataset.
