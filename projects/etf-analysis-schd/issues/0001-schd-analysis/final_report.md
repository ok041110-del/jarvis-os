## Composition

SCHD tracks the **Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index**. The index universe starts from the ~2,500-company Dow Jones U.S. Broad Market Index and applies a four-stage screening process, of which only stage one is documented: a minimum **10-year record of consecutive dividend payments**, which excludes any company that has ever cut or suspended its dividend. Stages two through four are undisclosed in the data.

Surviving companies are then ranked annually (in March) on trailing 10-year dividend history, trailing 5-year dividend growth, free cash flow-to-total-debt, ROE, and dividend yield. How these four factors combine into a composite score, and how that score translates into actual position weights (market-cap-based, score-proportional, capped, etc.), is **not explained** in the data — a material methodological gap.

The most recent reconstitution (effective 2026-03-23) added 25 constituents and removed 22. Reporting characterizes the net effect as reduced commodity/energy exposure and increased healthcare/technology exposure, but no ticker-level detail or before/after sector percentages were provided — only this directional characterization.

**Data caveats:** top-holdings figures differ between an earlier undated snapshot and a 2026-07 snapshot without a documented cause; no quantitative tracking-error figure exists, only qualitative commentary on securities lending.

## Cost

SCHD's **expense ratio is 0.06%** — a directly verified, hard figure, though the data provides no peer/category comparison to contextualize it.

**No numeric tracking-error figure exists anywhere in the data.** The only related claim is qualitative: Schwab's securities-lending program (lending healthcare/semiconductor holdings) returns 100% of lending income to NAV and is asserted, unsubstantiated, to "lower effective tracking error." This should be read as unverified commentary, not evidence of low tracking error.

The March 2026 reconstitution is described as "one of the largest constituent turnovers in the fund's history," with no quantitative link to any tracking-error outcome. Separately, the reconstitution reportedly generated an **unexpected distribution of $0.8241/share**, described as a real cost sitting "behind" the low 0.06% expense ratio. The mechanism (realized capital gains, tax treatment) is not explained — this is a discrete, sourced number, not further interpretable from the data.

**Conclusion:** the 0.06% expense ratio is verified; whether SCHD tracks its index closely cannot be assessed from this data.

## Bull Case

1. **Strong, low-risk recent returns:** +26.70% YTD / +31.87% 1yr alongside beta of 0.56 and 11.10% volatility — an apparently favorable risk/return combination, reinforced (qualitatively) by a reported record-margin outperformance of the S&P 500.
2. **Near-zero cost:** 0.06% expense ratio plus a securities-lending mechanism claimed (unverified) to aid tracking.
3. **Quality-screened methodology:** the 10-year consecutive-dividend-payment floor plus FCF/debt, ROE, and growth ranking filters for durability, not just yield — consistent with distribution growth from <$0.05 (2011) to $0.2525 (2026).
4. **Reconstitution as quality upgrade:** the shift from cyclical energy names (Chevron, ConocoPhillips) to healthcare (Abbott, UnitedHealth) is read as the rules-based process reducing commodity-price sensitivity.
5. **Diversification:** 103 holdings, top position only 4.42%, top-10 at ~42% — no dominant single-name risk.
6. **Income plus a possible tailwind:** yields of ~3%, roughly 7x QQQ's 0.42%, potentially aided if the rate-cut narrative (rather than the hike narrative) proves correct, given SCHD's defensive/value sector tilt.

**Data-limited caveats acknowledged by the bull case itself:** no multi-year track record; the rate-cut tailwind is contested, not confirmed; no verified tracking-error statistic; Sharpe ratio methodology undisclosed; top-3 sector overlap in healthcare unexamined at full-portfolio level; the reconstitution's $0.8241/share distribution is a real, unexplained cost.

## Bear Case

1. **Inverse-to-market positioning is a bet, not just a hedge:** ~55% in Healthcare/Staples/Energy vs. 17.53% for the S&P 500, and only ~9.2% Technology vs. 36.32% — a fund built this way should mechanically lag tech-led rallies over a full cycle (unconfirmed by any multi-year series in the data).
2. **Top-10 concentration is sector-clustered:** the top 3 holdings (Abbott, UnitedHealth, Merck) are all healthcare, exposing the fund's most influential names to shared regulatory/pricing/litigation risk that the "103 holdings" headline obscures.
3. **Methodology is a partial black box:** screening stages 2–4 and the weighting formula are undisclosed, meaning the fund's forward risk profile can't be fully audited from available data.
4. **The reconstitution had a real, quantified cost:** the $0.8241/share distribution demonstrates true cost of ownership exceeds the 0.06% expense ratio, and this is a recurring (annual) feature of the methodology, not a one-off.
5. **Sector character is unstable:** the swing from energy-heavy to healthcare-heavy top holdings shows the fund's profile can change materially from one reconstitution to the next.
6. **Possible yield compression:** the most recent, forward yield (2.95%, dated 2026-08-11) falls below the cited 5-year range floor (3.02%), and TTM (3.30%) sits in the lower half of that range — a bear reading available in, though not confirmed by, the data.
7. **Performance is a single un-contextualized snapshot:** no multi-year data exists to confirm durability, the Sharpe ratio lacks disclosed methodology, and the "record outperformance" claim is an unquantified headline.
8. **Macro downside is equally plausible:** if the hawkish narrative (rather than the cuts narrative) is more current, SCHD's defensive tilt has no demonstrated cushion in this data.
9. **No verified tracking-error data exists** anywhere across any of the seven underlying analyses — investors are asked to take index fidelity on faith, supported only by an unsubstantiated securities-lending claim.
10. **The underlying source data is itself inconsistent** (holdings snapshots differ, single-source AUM/holdings-count figures, unreconciled yield methodologies, contradictory macro commentary) — an independent due-diligence risk.

## Synthesis

Critically, **the bull and bear cases do not dispute any underlying number.** Every hard figure — expense ratio (0.06%), returns (+26.70%/+31.87%), beta (0.56), volatility (11.10%), Sharpe (2.89), holdings count (103), top holding (4.42%), top-10 weight (~42%), reconstitution sector shifts (Energy −8pts, Healthcare +4pts, Technology +3pts), turnover (25 added/22 removed), the special distribution ($0.8241/share), and the three yield readings (3.06%/2.95%/3.30%) — appears identically in both cases. What differs is **interpretation**, not fact: e.g., low beta read as favorable risk-adjusted profile (bull) vs. mechanical tech-lag risk (bear); the reconstitution read as a quality upgrade (bull) vs. evidence of instability (bear); the $0.8241/share distribution acknowledged by both sides as a real cost, differently weighted.

Both cases independently converge on the same **data-quality gaps**, which are shared limitations rather than points of disagreement:
- No multi-year (3/5/10-yr or since-inception) return series
- No disclosed Sharpe ratio methodology
- No quantitative tracking-error figure anywhere in the data
- Screening stages 2–4 and the weight-derivation formula undisclosed
- Unconfirmed whether the 2026-07 snapshot reflects the March 2026 reconstitution
- Unreconciled, contradictory macro narratives (rate cuts vs. hikes)

The open questions that would most change the conclusion, if resolved: (1) whether a longer track record shows the same pattern as this single strong year; (2) which macro narrative is actually current; (3) whether the 2026-07 snapshot is pre- or post-reconstitution; (4) whether any real tracking-error figure exists; (5) how the three divergent yield measures reconcile. None of these can be resolved from the data provided.

**Bottom line:** the data supports a genuinely two-sided read of SCHD, but because the two sides are not actually in factual conflict, further scrutiny of this dataset would not "pick a winner" — the data itself is insufficient to say which interpretation better predicts what happens next. Any conclusion beyond that would be filling gaps the data does not support.
