## Composition & Index Analysis — Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD)

**Index tracked.** SCHD tracks the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index, calculated by S&P Dow Jones Indices. This is the totality of what the provided data specifies about the benchmark itself; no data on index inception date, index provider governance, or licensing terms was supplied.

**Universe and selection methodology.** The index construction starts from the Dow Jones U.S. Broad Market Index, described as comprising roughly 2,500 companies. From this universe, the data describes a four-stage sequential screening process, but only the first stage is detailed: companies must have a minimum 10-year record of consecutive dividend payments. This stage mechanically excludes any company that has ever suspended or cut its dividend, as well as newer dividend payers and non-dividend-paying growth stocks. The data does not specify what stages two through four screen for — only that four stages exist and stage one is dividend-history length. This is a gap in the provided material, not an inference I can fill.

**Ranking and reconstitution mechanics.** Separately, the data describes an annual process (occurring in March) in which surviving companies are evaluated on: trailing 10-year dividend history, trailing 5-year dividend growth rate, and current financial ratios — specifically free cash flow to total debt, return on equity (ROE), dividend yield, and 5-year dividend growth. These metrics are used to produce a relative ranking, and the index is reconstituted on this basis. However, the data does not explain how these four ranking metrics are combined into a single composite score, nor how that score translates into the actual portfolio weight assigned to each constituent (e.g., whether weighting is market-cap-based, score-proportional, or capped in some way). This is a second methodological gap in the supplied data — I can describe the inputs to the ranking but not the weighting formula itself.

**Recent reconstitution (effective 2026-03-23).** The 2026 annual reconstitution added 25 new constituents and removed 22, according to the cited sources. The reporting characterizes the net sector effect as a reduction in commodity/energy exposure and an increase in healthcare and technology exposure. The data does not provide the specific tickers added or removed, the resulting sector weight percentages before and after, or the magnitude of the shift — only the directional characterization from the cited reports (techtimes.com, S&P Global, topdividendetfs.com, schdtools.com).

**Explicit data limitations (carried forward as provided):**
- This summary is based on web-search snapshots, not a direct review of Schwab's official fact sheet or the full S&P Dow Jones Indices methodology document, so the screening-stage and weighting gaps noted above may simply reflect what those secondary sources chose to report rather than omissions in the actual methodology.
- Top-holdings figures were reported with different constituents and weights at two different points in time (an earlier snapshot vs. one dated 2026-07), and the data does not establish when or why the change occurred — this inconsistency is noted rather than resolved.
- No quantitative tracking-error figure was found; only a qualitative reference to securities-lending revenue as a contributing factor.
- All figures reflect a search snapshot as of 2026-08-13, not real-time index or fund data.

I have not made a buy/sell judgment, consistent with the scope of this analysis; the two structural gaps (screening stages 2–4, and the exact weighting formula) mean a complete picture of index construction cannot be confirmed from the data provided.
