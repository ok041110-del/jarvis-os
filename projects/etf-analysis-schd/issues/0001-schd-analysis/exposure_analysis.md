## SCHD (Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF) — Sector and Geographic Exposure Analysis

**Sector concentration.** The data collected (as of the 2026-07 snapshot, per kavout.com and topdividendetfs.com) shows SCHD's exposure concentrated in three sectors: Healthcare and Consumer Staples combined at 41.1%, Energy at 14.1%, and Information Technology at only 9.2%. A second source set (ts2.tech, seekingalpha.com) reports these same three sectors — Healthcare, Consumer Staples, and Energy — summing to 55.17%, with IT at a nearly identical 9.23%. Notably, when the first source's Healthcare+Staples figure (41.1%) is added to its separately-reported Energy figure (14.1%), the sum is 55.2% — which matches the second source's combined 55.17% almost exactly. So while the two source sets present the numbers differently (one broken out by sector, one pre-aggregated), they are numerically consistent with each other rather than contradictory. Given this convergence, the data supports a fund profile heavily weighted toward defensive/value sectors (Healthcare, Consumer Staples, Energy together account for roughly 55% of the portfolio) and correspondingly light in technology (~9%).

The provided comparison to the S&P 500 sharpens this picture: the same three sectors (Healthcare + Staples + Energy) make up only 17.53% of the S&P 500, versus SCHD's 55.17%, while IT is 36.32% of the S&P 500 versus SCHD's 9.23%. Based strictly on this data, SCHD's sector composition is close to the inverse of the broad market's — substantially overweight defensive/value sectors and substantially underweight technology.

**Reconstitution-driven shift.** One source attributes a March 2026 index reconstitution to sector-level changes: Energy weight reduced by roughly 8 percentage points, while Healthcare and Technology increased by about 4 and 3 percentage points, respectively. However, the data does not explicitly state the "before" and "after" dates this change is measured against, nor does it confirm whether the 2026-07 figures cited above already fully reflect this reconstitution. I can't determine from the provided data whether the 41.1%/14.1%/9.2% figures are pre- or post-reconstitution, or whether the reconstitution is the explanation for any difference between the two source sets — this is a gap in the data, not a fact I can supply.

**Geographic exposure.** No geographic breakdown figures were provided in the source data. The only basis for a geographic characterization is the fund's name and index ("U.S. Dividend" / Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index), which implies a U.S.-domiciled/U.S.-listed equity focus, but this is an inference from the index name rather than a verified data point — no country- or region-level exposure percentages were present in any source.

**Other limitations noted in the source data itself.** The underlying research is described as WebSearch summaries, not a direct read of Schwab's official fact sheet or S&P Dow Jones Indices' methodology documentation. Top-holdings figures reportedly differ across two points in time without a clear record of exactly when the change occurred. No tracking-error figure was found (only qualitative commentary on securities-lending income). And the broader source set contains contradictory macro commentary (rate-cut expectations vs. a shift toward considering rate hikes) without clear dating to distinguish which applies when. All figures reflect a search snapshot as of 2026-08-13, not live data.

**Summary of what the data does and doesn't support:** The data supports characterizing SCHD as concentrated in defensive/value sectors (Healthcare, Consumer Staples, Energy ≈ 55% combined) and light in Technology (~9%), consistent across both source sets and consistent with its stated inverse-to-S&P-500 profile. It does not support any specific geographic breakdown, nor a confident statement about whether current sector weights already reflect the cited March 2026 reconstitution.
