## Cost and Tracking Analysis: Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD)

**Expense ratio.** The data indicates SCHD's expense ratio is 0.06%. This is a directly stated, quantitative figure and is the only hard cost number provided. On its face, this is a low headline cost for holding the fund, but the data set contains no comparison points (e.g., category average, peer dividend-ETF fees) to contextualize how low 0.06% is relative to alternatives — so no relative cost judgment can be made from this data alone.

**Tracking error.** No numeric tracking-error figure is present in the provided material. What exists instead is a qualitative claim, attributed to the sources, that Schwab's securities-lending program — lending out healthcare and semiconductor blue-chip holdings — returns 100% of lending income to the fund's NAV, and that this is reported to be a factor that "lowers effective tracking error." This claim should be treated strictly as an unverified, qualitative assertion: no basis-point figure, time period, or methodology is given to substantiate it, and it is presented as reported commentary rather than a measured statistic. It should not be read as evidence that SCHD's actual tracking error is low, only that a mechanism exists which is asserted (by the source, not demonstrated in the data) to help tracking.

**March 2026 reconstitution.** The data reports that the index reconstitution in March 2026 was "one of the largest constituent turnovers in the fund's history." This establishes, as a matter of reported fact, that large-scale trading activity occurred. However, the data provides no quantitative link between this turnover event and any measured tracking-error outcome — there is no before/after tracking-error figure, no attribution of slippage or deviation to the reconstitution. Any inference that this turnover degraded (or improved) tracking would be speculation not supported by the material given.

**Reconstitution-driven distribution.** A separate reported item states that the reconstitution generated an unexpected distribution of $0.8241 per share, described in a headline as being "behind" the low 0.06% expense ratio — i.e., suggesting that reconstitution-related capital gains distributions represent a real cost/tax effect distinct from the stated expense ratio. This is a specific, seemingly quantitative figure ($0.8241/share), but the data does not explain the underlying mechanism (e.g., whether this reflects realized capital gains passed through to shareholders, and under what tax treatment), nor does it connect this distribution figure to any measured effect on total cost of ownership or tracking performance. It should be reported as a discrete, source-attributed data point rather than interpreted further.

**Overall assessment.** Based strictly on what is provided: SCHD's stated expense ratio (0.06%) is a verified quantitative figure, but there is no verified tracking-error statistic in this data set — only a qualitative, unsubstantiated claim about securities lending helping tracking, and an unquantified reconstitution event. The $0.8241/share distribution figure is a real number but its cost/tax implications are not explained in the source material. Consequently, this data does not support a judgment on how well SCHD "likely tracks its benchmark" in any measured sense — that would require actual tracking-error or tracking-difference statistics, which are absent. Any statement to the contrary would be filling a gap the data does not support.

**Note on data limitations (as recorded in the source material):** the underlying summary was not cross-checked against Schwab's official fact sheet or S&P Dow Jones Indices methodology documents; holdings figures are reported inconsistently across two undated snapshots; no tracking-error number could be found in the search; and the snapshot reflects reporting as of 2026-08-13, not real-time data.
