No position-limit or look-through exposure policy was stated, so there is no defined ceiling to test this against — the concentration exists as a fact, but its acceptability can't be checked against any stated threshold, only against judgment.

2. **Correlation risk between the two holdings.** Both PG and JNJ are large-cap, long-dividend-history "quality compounder" names with overlapping investor bases (dividend-aristocrat / bond-proxy demand). A shock that hits one channel (e.g., a rate move, a rotation out of low-growth dividend payers) plausibly hits both simultaneously.

This is a distinct risk from ordinary volatility: the portfolio is sitting on two large weights where the *basis* for holding is explicitly "insufficient information to differentiate," not "confirmed stable."

4. **Bounded observation.** Only these two positions were disclosed as portfolio state. There is no information here about the remainder of the portfolio, so nothing can be said about overall (portfolio-wide) concentration — only about the relationship between these two named positions relative to each other.

**Tolerability judgment:**

The risk level here is moderate, not severe, but not clearly "acceptable" either, for a specific reason: there is no stated policy against which to benchmark the 18% combined weight or the correlation between the two names, and the size is carried in two positions that are both, on their own terms, undecided rather than confirmed-sound.
