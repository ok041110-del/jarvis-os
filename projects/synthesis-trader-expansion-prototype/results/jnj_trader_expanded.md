## REPORT

**Where the two cases agree on facts.** Both sides work from the identical underlying data points and rarely dispute the numbers themselves: Q2 2026 revenue $25.3B (+6.6% YoY) and adjusted EPS $2.90 beating consensus; FY2026 guidance raised to $101.1B revenue and $11.68 adjusted EPS; GAAP EPS of $2.27 versus adjusted $2.90 (~22% gap); a payout ratio move from 84% to 46.19%; 64 consecutive years of dividend increases with a 3% raise to $5.36/share; a P/E range spanning 16.9x–29.7x across sources; a $5.5B talc settlement proposal covering ~70,000 claims with $3B deferred to 2027; the bankruptcy-channel strategy rejected and not appealed; a $45M Illinois verdict affirmed on appeal; price at $259.24 above EMA20/50/200; a negative MACD histogram alongside rising price; two divergent RSI readings (64.566 and 55.45); and an analyst base of 13 Buy / 11 Hold / 1 Sell. Neither side invents numbers the other omits — the disagreement is almost entirely about how to weight and interpret this shared data set, not about what the data says.

**Where interpretation genuinely diverges on the same facts.**
- *Valuation:* Both note the P/E dispersion (16.9x vs. 27.6x–29.7x) and the DCF-vs-multiples "tug of war." The bull leans on the DCF signal and the low P/E as forward-looking and cash-flow-anchored; the bear treats the low P/E as the uncorroborated outlier and weights the two mutually consistent higher readings against a 17.1x sector average, concluding JNJ screens expensive on two of three data points.
- *Price versus analyst targets:* Both agree $259.24 sits above the disclosed target range ($155–$240), median ($210.02), and mean ($238.2). The bull treats this as a footnote that "tempers" the sentiment read; the bear treats it as the single most concrete, quantitatively solid data point in the entire set.
- *The GAAP/adjusted EPS gap and "margin under pressure" headline:* Both flag it as unexplained. The bull treats the guidance raise as valid because it's built on the adjusted metric; the bear treats reliance on adjusted EPS as building the raise on precisely the metric where pressure was flagged, and floats (without confirming) a link to unprovisioned talc costs.
- *Payout ratio improvement:* Both flag the cause as undisclosed. The bull reads it as evidence of "stronger cash generation"; the bear raises the alternative, unconfirmed possibility that a prior-year litigation charge distorts the comparison.
- *Talc litigation trajectory:* Both agree the bankruptcy channel was rejected and not appealed. The bull reads this as management redirecting toward a converging, bounded settlement; the bear reads the same rejection as the loss of JNJ's preferred capped-resolution mechanism, pushing exposure back toward less predictable settlement-plus-individual-litigation, reinforced by the affirmed $45M verdict.
- *Technicals:* Both cite the same MA/RSI/MACD data. The bull calls the moving-average picture (12 buy / 0 sell signals) unambiguously bullish trend; the bear treats the negative MACD histogram and RSI disagreement as momentum diverging from price, with an additional flagged inconsistency between two sources' moving-average levels.
- *Competitive positioning:* Both note AbbVie in immunology and MedTech's slower 4.5% growth versus Pharma's 7.8%. The bull frames JNJ as resourced and active across every major franchise; the bear reads the same language (AbbVie called "strong" specifically against JNJ, JNJ only "pursuing" oncology leadership) as evidence the diversified story is currently carried by one segment.

**Genuine data inconsistencies (not just differing interpretation), as both sides flag them:** plaintiff counts differ across sources (68,914 vs. "over 63,000"); one source's moving-average levels sit $19–30 below the reported close, an unexplained gap; a stated "+6.05% upside vs. average target" claim is arithmetically inconsistent with the $238.2 average cited in the same report; one source states JNJ trades above the pharma industry average P/E (17.1x) while another cites 16.9x as below a 21.2x industry average — these are contradictions within the source data itself, not resolved by either case.

**Open questions that would most change the conclusion, in order of likely impact:**
1. What explains the ~22% GAAP/adjusted EPS gap and the "net margin under pressure" headline — is it talc-related, and is it structural or one-time? This bears directly on whether the beat-and-raise and the payout-ratio improvement reflect durable operating strength or an accounting/litigation artifact.
2. Is the $5.5B settlement (and the $3B deferred to 2027) already reserved, or does it represent an incremental future charge against the ~$21B FCF figure the dividend framework depends on? This determines whether the litigation overhang is genuinely "bounded" or still an open liability.
3. Why does current price exceed every disclosed analyst target, and is more recent target data available that the sourced snapshot (through 2026-08-07/08-13) does not capture? This is the most concrete valuation-anchor question and the one most sensitive to new information.
4. Which P/E reading (16.9x vs. 27.6x–29.7x) is methodologically correct, and against which peer basis? This single resolution would settle the "cheap vs. expensive" dispute that both cases otherwise leave open.

## DECISION

- Direction: HOLD
- Rationale: The two cases work from the same facts but reach opposite emphases on valuation, margin quality, and litigation risk, and several of the load-bearing figures (P/E, GAAP/adjusted gap cause, settlement accounting treatment, analyst target currency) are internally inconsistent or unresolved in the data itself. A directional call requires resolving at least one of those open questions in one side's favor, which the given data does not allow.
- Reassess when: Whether the $5.5B talc settlement (and its $3B tranche deferred to 2027) is already reserved for versus representing an incremental future charge against JNJ's ~$21B free cash flow — this bears most directly on both the "bounded liability" bull read and the "unverified dividend safety" bear read, and would most likely tip the balance in either direction.
