## REPORT

**Facts both cases agree on (no dispute):** Q2 2026 revenue grew 24% YoY to $20.543B; adjusted EPS grew 73% YoY ($4.72 → $8.17); adjusted operating margin expanded ~430bps (17.6% → 21.9%); segment growth was broad-based (Construction +35%, Power & Energy +17%, Resource Industries +20%, Financial Products +10%); backlog reached $72B, up ~92% YoY; the 2026 tariff headwind is estimated at $2.2–2.4B, versus Deere's $1.2B; CAT's ROE (48.21%) is well above Komatsu (10.83%) and Terex (13.43%); of 28 covering institutions, 15 rate CAT a buy, 11 hold, 2 sell; and several data points — forward P/E, market cap, price targets, and full-year revenue guidance language — are internally inconsistent across sources within the dataset itself.

**Where the two cases conflict on interpretation of the same facts, not on the facts themselves:**

- *Analyst consensus (15/11/2):* Both sides cite the identical numbers. The bull frames it as "buy outnumbers hold+sell combined." The bear frames it as "only just over half are bullish, and nearly half aren't." Neither is factually wrong — this is a framing dispute over the same 28-institution distribution, sharpened by a genuinely wide $575–$1,218 price-target range that both sides acknowledge is too inconsistent to use as valuation support on its own.
- *Peer growth/margin comparison (CAT trailing Terex on growth, Deere on margin):* Both cases see this same table. The bull dismisses it as unreconciled with CAT's own Q2 actuals and therefore unreliable; the bear treats it as a data point that exists in the sourcing and stands as a caution even under an acknowledged different measurement basis. This is a genuine methodological disagreement about how much weight to put on an unreconciled comparison, not a disagreement about what the table says.
- *Valuation premium vs. ROE gap:* Both agree CAT trades at roughly 2.6–3x Komatsu/Terex's forward P/E while posting a ~4–5x ROE advantage. The bull treats the ROE gap as directional support for a premium multiple; the bear notes there is no quantitative link (no PEG, no regression) in the data connecting the two, so the premium is asserted rather than demonstrated. This is an interpretive dispute over how much a return-on-equity edge should map onto an earnings-multiple premium, not a factual one.
- *Technical picture:* Both sides work from the same self-contradictory source (neutral 6/6 and 3/3 moving-average splits alongside a "strongly bearish" label; RSI 40.55 mislabeled "oversold"). The bull concludes the signal is too internally inconsistent to carry weight either way. The bear extracts the "strongly bearish" EMA characterization and the sub-50 RSI as standalone cautionary reads. Same source, opposite selective emphasis.
- *Guidance raise amid tariff cost:* Both note guidance moved up even as a $2.2–2.4B tariff headwind was flagged. The bull reads this as evidence demand is strong enough to absorb the cost; the bear reads the same sequence as a live risk, since neither source confirms the tariff cost is actually netted into the raised figure.

**Where the data itself is genuinely unresolved (not an interpretation dispute — an actual data gap):** the guidance language ("mid to high teens" vs. "low double-digit"); forward P/E (38.51x vs. 43.7x); market cap ($392.49B vs. $409B); price-target averages (three different figures from three different institution counts); whether the tariff headwind is already reflected in guidance; and the backlog's conversion timeline and segment composition. Neither case resolves these — both explicitly flag them as unreconciled and, to their credit, avoid leaning on the weaker figures as load-bearing evidence.

**Open questions most likely to change the conclusion, in rough order of leverage:**

1. Is the $2.2–2.4B tariff headwind already netted into the raised guidance? This is the single largest swing factor — it determines whether the "guidance raised despite tariffs" bull read or the "guidance may not survive tariff realization" bear read is correct.
2. Which guidance figure is current — "mid to high teens" or "low double-digit"? This changes the magnitude of the forward growth story materially.
3. What is the backlog's conversion timeline and segment mix? Without this, the $72B figure cannot be translated into a near-term revenue trajectory with any confidence.
4. Is the peer growth/margin comparison (CAT trailing Terex/Deere) measured on the same basis as CAT's own Q2 actuals? Resolving this determines whether "CAT lags peers" is a real signal or a data artifact.

## DECISION

- **Direction:** HOLD
- **Rationale:** The reported Q2 results (24% revenue growth, 73% EPS growth, 430bps margin expansion, broad segment strength, record backlog) are genuinely strong and undisputed between the two cases, which argues against a bearish stance. But the forward-looking case for paying CAT's current premium rests on several unresolved data conflicts — which guidance figure is operative, whether tariff costs are already priced into it, and how much weight the unreconciled peer-comparison and technical readings deserve — none of which this dataset resolves in either direction. Given no portfolio, cost-basis, or sizing context was provided (and none should be assumed), the balance of confirmed strength against unresolved forward-looking risk supports neither a conviction BUY nor a SELL.
- **Reassess when:** Whether the $2.2–2.4B tariff headwind is already reflected in the raised 2026 guidance — this is the open question most likely to flip the stance, since it directly determines whether the guidance raise reflects genuine demand strength absorbing a known cost (supporting BUY) or an unresolved risk to bottom-line delivery later in the year (supporting SELL).
