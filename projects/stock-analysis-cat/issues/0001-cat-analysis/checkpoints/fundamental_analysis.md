## Caterpillar Inc. (CAT) — Fundamental Analysis: Q2 2026

**Revenue and earnings trend**

Caterpillar reported second-quarter 2026 revenue of $20.543 billion, up 24% year-over-year from $16.569 billion — the first time quarterly revenue has crossed the $20 billion threshold. Earnings per share rose to $7.77 from $4.62 a year earlier, and adjusted EPS reached $8.17 versus $4.72, a 73% year-over-year increase. The gap between headline revenue growth (24%) and adjusted EPS growth (73%) indicates earnings expanded well ahead of the top line, consistent with the margin expansion discussed below rather than one-time items (none are specified in the data provided).

Growth was broad-based across segments: Construction Industries revenue grew 35%, Power & Energy grew 17%, Resource Industries grew 20% (to $4.648 billion, with segment profit of $693 million, up 23%), and Financial Products revenue grew 10% (to $1.145 billion, with segment profit of $328 million, up 32%). The fact that segment profit grew faster than segment revenue in both Resource Industries and Financial Products points to profitability gains within those units specifically, not just volume-driven growth.

**Margin trend**

Operating margin improved to 20.9% from 17.3% a year earlier, and adjusted operating margin rose to 21.9% from 17.6%, a 430-basis-point improvement. This is a substantial year-over-year expansion and, combined with the disproportionate EPS growth relative to revenue growth, suggests improved operating leverage — meaning incremental revenue is converting to profit at a higher rate than in the prior-year quarter. The data provided does not break out the specific drivers of this margin expansion (e.g., pricing, cost control, mix shift), so the underlying cause cannot be characterized further from what's given.

**Guidance**

For full-year 2026, Caterpillar's revenue guidance was raised to "mid to high teens" percentage growth. However, the source data flags an unresolved inconsistency: other material in the same dataset describes 2026 revenue growth guidance as "low double-digit." The data does not clarify whether these reflect two different points in time (e.g., pre- and post-Q2 guidance updates) or a discrepancy between sources reporting on the same guidance. This should be treated as an open inconsistency rather than resolved in either direction.

Separately, the company's backlog reached a reported $72 billion, up $9 billion from the prior quarter and roughly 92% higher year-over-year, described as a record. A backlog of this size and growth rate — if sustained — would typically imply revenue visibility extending beyond the current quarter, though the data does not specify the backlog's expected conversion timeline or composition by segment.

**What this implies, and where the data falls short**

Taken together, the reported figures describe a quarter of accelerating revenue, margin expansion outpacing revenue growth, and a sharply larger backlog — a combination that, on its face, points to strengthening demand and improving operating efficiency. However, several gaps in the provided data limit how far this can be interpreted:

- The revenue guidance conflict ("mid to high teens" vs. "low double-digit") is unresolved in the source material and directly affects how the forward outlook should be read.
- It is not stated whether the $2.2–2.4 billion in tariff headwinds referenced in the data has already been incorporated into the raised guidance, which matters for assessing whether the guidance increase reflects genuine demand strength or is partly offset by cost pressure not yet reflected.
- The peer/industry comparison figures (11.8% revenue growth, 16.5% operating margin) referenced elsewhere in the dataset are not reconciled with Caterpillar's own reported Q2 figures (+24% revenue, 20.9–21.9% margin) — it's unclear whether these are measured on the same basis (e.g., same period, same margin definition), so no direct comparative conclusion can be drawn from them.
- Valuation-related figures in the data (market cap, forward P/E, price targets, technical signals) are internally inconsistent across sources and are, in any case, outside the scope of this earnings/fundamentals analysis.

Given these gaps — particularly the unresolved guidance discrepancy and the unclear tariff treatment — the guidance portion of this picture should be read with caution rather than taken at face value.
