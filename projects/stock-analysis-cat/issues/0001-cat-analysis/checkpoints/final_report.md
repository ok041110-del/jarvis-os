# Caterpillar Inc. (NYSE: CAT) — Research Report
**Data snapshot: web-collected sources, as of ~2026-08-17. Not verified against CAT's 10-Q or earnings release.**

## Fundamental
Q2 2026 revenue was $20.543B (+24% YoY), the first quarter above $20B. EPS rose to $7.77 ($4.62 prior year); adjusted EPS reached $8.17 (+73% YoY). Operating margin expanded to 20.9% (17.3% prior year); adjusted operating margin rose to 21.9% (+430bps). Growth was broad: Construction Industries +35%, Power & Energy +17%, Resource Industries +20% ($4.648B, segment profit +23% to $693M), Financial Products +10% ($1.145B, segment profit +32% to $328M) — profit outrunning revenue in two segments. Backlog reached $72B, +$9B sequentially, ~92% YoY, a record.

**Flagged inconsistency:** 2026 revenue guidance is reported as both "mid to high teens" and "low double-digit" growth in the same dataset, with no indication of which is current. It is also unclear whether the $2.2–2.4B tariff headwind is already reflected in guidance. Peer-comparison figures elsewhere (11.8% revenue growth, 16.5% margin) don't reconcile with these Q2 actuals — the measurement basis is unexplained.

## Technical
Signals are internally contradictory, not merely mixed. One source shows a "Neutral" moving-average read (6 buy/6 sell), a tighter daily 3/3 split, yet the same source separately calls the stock "strongly bearish" for trading below its 5/20/50-day EMAs — these cannot all be true of the same indicator set without an unexplained methodological difference. RSI(14) = 40.55, within the standard neutral band (30–70), but the source mislabels it "oversold." No support/resistance levels are provided. Readings may date to late July, adding staleness risk. **No directional trend conclusion is supportable from this data.**

## Industry
CAT competes with Deere, Komatsu, Terex, Oshkosh, Wabtec, XCMG, and Epiroc. Market cap is given as both $392.49B and $409B across sources (~$16B gap, unreconciled). Forward P/E is reported as 38.51x (vs. peer average 35.13x) in one source and 43.7x in another (vs. Deere's 33.1x) — direction (premium) agrees, magnitude doesn't. Komatsu (14.73x) and Terex (13.65x) trade far cheaper; CAT's ROE of 48.21% vastly exceeds Komatsu (10.83%) and Terex (13.43%), offered as the premium's justification, though no quantitative link (e.g., PEG, regression) is provided. Peer-comparison figures show CAT growth (11.8%) trailing Terex (17.0%) and margin (16.5%) trailing Deere (17.4%) — but again, this doesn't reconcile with CAT's own Q2 actuals (+24%/20.9–21.9%), and the data doesn't explain why. Tariffs are an industry-wide headwind, but CAT's estimated $2.2–2.4B for 2026 is roughly double Deere's $1.2B projection.

## News/Event
The $2.2–2.4B tariff estimate is treated as a recurring structural cost, not one-time. Cited demand drivers — U.S. infrastructure spending, energy-transition mining demand, automation, data center/sustainability investment — align plausibly with CAT's segments (data centers → Power & Energy; mining → Resource Industries), but the data gives no quantification of each driver's contribution. The central unresolved question: how a $2.2–2.4B tariff headwind coexists with raised guidance is never explained in the source material — possible explanations (already netted in, different metrics being compared, guidance and tariff estimates from different points in time) are all plausible but none is confirmed.

## Sentiment
S&P Global's 28-institution consensus: 15 buy / 11 hold / 2 sell → "Buy" rating, though buys are only just over half of coverage. A separate 16-institution consensus projects 2026 revenue of $78.9B and EPS growth of 13% to $26.71. **Target prices are the weakest data point in the entire set:** three sources give three different averages built on three different institution counts — $972.95 (28 institutions), $970.37 (26), $900.78 (34) — with no explanation for why the underlying institution counts themselves differ. One source's range is $575–$1,218, a ~2.1x spread, undermining the usefulness of any single average as a summary of consensus.

## Bull Case
- Profit is growing far faster than revenue (73% adjusted EPS growth vs. 24% revenue growth), with margin expansion embedded within segments (not just consolidated), indicating genuine operating leverage.
- Record $72B backlog (+92% YoY) signals durable forward demand.
- Guidance was raised even as a large, quantified tariff headwind was disclosed — consistent with demand strong enough to more than offset it.
- CAT's 48.21% ROE — several multiples above Komatsu and Terex — offers at least directional support for its valuation premium.
- Analyst ratings skew majority-buy, with no source showing a net-bearish distribution.
- The peer-lag figures (11.8%/16.5%) are unreconciled with CAT's own reported Q2 actuals and arguably shouldn't be trusted over the company's directly reported numbers.
- Contradictory technical signals cannot be resolved into a confirmed bearish trend, so they don't offset the fundamental strength.

## Bear Case
- The valuation premium (P/E 38.51x–43.7x vs. Komatsu 14.73x, Terex 13.65x) is justified only by narrative (ROE), with no quantitative link in the data connecting the size of the ROE gap to the size of the multiple gap.
- The same peer-comparison data the bull dismisses still shows CAT trailing Terex on growth and Deere on margin — it exists in the sourcing and can't simply be discarded because it's unfavorable.
- CAT's tariff exposure ($2.2–2.4B) is nearly double Deere's, and it is not established that this is already priced into guidance — a live risk to full-year margin.
- The guidance conflict itself is a red flag: if "low double-digit" is the operative figure, the growth story is materially weaker than the market may assume.
- The backlog's conversion timeline and segment composition are unstated, so it cannot be assumed to translate into near-term revenue at any particular pace.
- A "strongly bearish" EMA read is explicitly present in the source data (alongside sub-50 RSI) and shouldn't be waved away merely because other lines in the same source disagree.
- Nearly half of covering analysts (13 of 28) are not bullish, and the ~2.1x target-price spread suggests real disagreement on fair value hidden beneath the "Buy" label.

## Synthesis
Both cases agree on the same core Q2 print (revenue +24%, adjusted EPS +73%, margin +430bps, $72B backlog, $2.2–2.4B tariff estimate, 48.21% ROE, 15/11/2 rating split) — the dispute is about interpretation and how to treat the dataset's internal inconsistencies, not the underlying facts. Identical numbers support opposite framings (e.g., the 15/11/2 split is both "majority buy" and "nearly half non-bullish"). The most consequential open questions are: (1) which revenue guidance figure is current, (2) whether the tariff estimate is already netted into that guidance — this most directly determines whether Q2's margin expansion is sustainable, (3) why the peer-comparison growth/margin figures diverge from CAT's own Q2 actuals, (4) the backlog's conversion timeline, and (5) the true forward P/E and market cap. The dataset does not contain enough information to resolve any of these, and both bull and bear cases correctly flag them as gaps rather than resolving them.
