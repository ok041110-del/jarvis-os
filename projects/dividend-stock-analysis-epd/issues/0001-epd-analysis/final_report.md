## Bull Case
Real YoY operating momentum (DCF +21%, marine volumes +33%, pipeline +8%), an EPS beat, and 20.9% ROE; a 28-year distribution-growth streak with an (unverified) improving coverage trend to 1.9x plus $159M buybacks; cheap P/E vs. peers and mid-pack forward EV/EBITDA; a technically unambiguous uptrend (golden cross, 12/0 signals); a structural Mont Belvieu moat with brownfield-efficient growth; a low-drama, internally succeeded leadership transition; and a 0-Sell sentiment tally. The bull case acknowledges but treats as non-negating the EV/EBITDA, payout-ratio, and capex contradictions.

## Bear Case
Treats the unresolved contradictions themselves as the central risk, not noise. The 57%/80%/56% payout-ratio conflict is the crux — if 80% is accurate, distribution cushion is materially thinner than the "1.9x coverage" framing suggests, and the "improving coverage" sequence looks suspicious sitting beside it. The capex swing ($2.9–3.4B vs. $2.2–2.5B) could signal deliberate capital discipline or fewer attractive projects — either way, an unexplained guidance swing is itself a red flag. EV/EBITDA ambiguity removes the second core valuation pillar, leaving P/E as a lone support. Sentiment dispersion includes an active Underweight with a cut target, undercutting the clean "0 Sell" narrative. Price-target upside is unverifiable given the baseline-price conflict. Intraday overbought RSI is a live tactical risk with no support level to gauge downside. The EPS "beat" basis is unconfirmed, and Q1 2026 was reportedly an "EPS miss, revenue beat" quarter, undercutting a clean beat-streak narrative. Losing the strategy's formative architect (Teague) coincides with, rather than resolves, the capex ambiguity.

## Synthesis
Bull and bear draw from the same messy dataset rather than disputing individual numbers. Undisputed facts: the 28-year streak, the daily-timeframe technical trend, Mont Belvieu positioning, and headline Q2 volume/DCF growth (though lacking margin or multi-quarter context). Genuinely unresolved data conflicts, inherited by both sides: payout ratio (57%/56% vs. 80%), EV/EBITDA (11.31x vs. 9.82x), 2026 capex ($2.9–3.4B vs. $2.2–2.5B), price-target baseline ($38.6 vs. $36.57), and EPS-beat comparability. Same-facts-different-reading disputes: coverage-ratio trend credibility, how much weight a 0-Sell tally deserves given Morgan Stanley's Underweight, RSI timeframe emphasis, how much a single clean multiple (P/E) can carry when its companion (EV/EBITDA) is broken, and how "continuity" the leadership transition really is. The highest-leverage open question is which payout ratio is correct; next are the capex range, the true current EV/EBITDA, EPS-beat comparability, and the rationale behind Morgan Stanley's Underweight. **The data provided is not sufficient to adjudicate between the bull and bear narratives** — several of the numbers needed for a confident conclusion are simply unsettled in what was supplied and would require reconciliation against primary sources (10-Q, Q2 2026 earnings release).
