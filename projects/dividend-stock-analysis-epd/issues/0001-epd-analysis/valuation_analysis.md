# Valuation Analysis: Enterprise Products Partners L.P. (EPD)

**Note on data quality:** The dataset provided contains multiple internal contradictions on the same metrics from different sources, collected at a single point in time (August 16, 2026) via web search rather than primary filings. This analysis flags those contradictions explicitly rather than reconciling them, since no basis is given to determine which figure is correct.

## P/E Ratio

On a P/E basis, EPD is reported at approximately 13.5x–13.6x (two sources converge closely here, which is the one area of relative agreement in this dataset). This sits slightly above the cited sector average of 12.6x, but meaningfully below the peer-group average of roughly 19.6x–20.3x. Taken at face value, this would suggest EPD looks inexpensive relative to its direct peer group but roughly in line with (or marginally more expensive than) the broader sector. However, no peer composition is given, so it is unclear whether the "peer group" and "sector" comparisons are measuring against the same set of companies, which limits how much weight this comparison can bear.

## EV/EBITDA — Unresolved Contradiction

This is the most significant issue in the dataset. Two different EV/EBITDA figures are attributed to EPD within the same source set: **11.31x** and **9.82x**. The 9.82x figure appears in a sentence comparing EPD to Enbridge's 15.53x, which implies it is meant to represent EPD's multiple, but the surrounding text is described as ambiguous about which company the figure actually belongs to. No basis (TTM vs. forward, different calculation date, different data vendor) is given to reconcile these two numbers. Because of this, **no reliable point-in-time EV/EBITDA figure can be stated for EPD from this data**, and any comparison built on either number individually should be treated as unverified.

Separately, a forward EV/EBITDA range of 9.0x–9.5x is given, positioning EPD between Energy Transfer (~8.0x forward) and Enbridge (~11.5x forward) — i.e., roughly mid-pack among these three midstream peers. This forward figure does not by itself resolve which of the two TTM figures (11.31x or 9.82x) is more plausible, since neither is explicitly labeled as trailing or forward.

## Distribution Yield

EPD's distribution yield is given as 5.66%, above Enbridge (5.03%) and below Energy Transfer (7.21%). On this metric alone, EPD sits between the two peers, which is a directionally consistent, single-sourced data point in this set — though a higher yield is not inherently a sign of cheapness without knowing the sustainability of the distribution.

## Payout Ratio and Coverage — Contradictions That Undercut the Yield Read

The distribution yield figure cannot be evaluated in isolation because the data supporting the sustainability of that distribution is itself contradictory:

- **Distribution coverage ratio** is reported as 1.7x, 1.8x, 1.85x, and 1.9x across different sources, with no consistent explanation for the spread.
- **Payout ratio** is reported as 56%, 57%, and 80% — a spread wide enough that one source's framing explicitly notes a conflict between a management-reported figure (57%) and a separately calculated media figure (80%).

These are not minor rounding differences; an 80% payout ratio implies a materially less-cushioned distribution than a 56–57% payout ratio would. Given this, the 5.66% yield figure cannot be confidently characterized as "well-covered" or "cheap yield" versus peers without knowing which coverage/payout figure is accurate.

## Enterprise Value vs. Market Cap

The data cites an enterprise value of roughly $120B against a market cap of $84.1B, but the two figures are not explicitly reconciled (e.g., no stated net debt bridge), and the ~$120B figure is described as tied to "growth over a CEO's tenure" rather than a clean current-point EV calculation. This further weakens confidence in the EV/EBITDA multiples discussed above, since EV is a direct input to that ratio and its own basis is unclear.

## Other Contradictions Noted But Not Bearing Directly on Multiples

- 2026 growth capex is reported in two non-overlapping ranges ($2.9–3.4B vs. $2.2–2.5B).
- Price targets are built on different reference prices ($38.6 implied vs. $36.57), and the five analyst ratings cited use five different rating labels, making it unclear whether they represent genuine dispersion of opinion or inconsistent labeling conventions across sources.
- RSI readings differ (60s vs. 80) but this reflects different timeframes (daily vs. intraday), which is a different kind of inconsistency (measurement window) than the other contradictions (source disagreement), and is a technical/momentum indicator rather than a valuation multiple.

## Overall Assessment

Based strictly on the data provided: EPD's P/E multiple offers the most coherent (if still incomplete) valuation signal, placing it below its peer group average but slightly above a cited sector average. The EV/EBITDA data, by contrast, is internally contradictory to the point that **no single trailing multiple can be stated with confidence**, and the forward range (9.0x–9.5x) only places EPD in the middle of a three-company peer set without resolving the trailing-multiple conflict. The distribution yield of 5.66% is the one figure presented without competing values, but its interpretive value is undermined by sharply conflicting payout ratio (56–80%) and coverage ratio (1.7x–1.9x) figures from the same source set.

**Given these contradictions — most notably the dual EV/EBITDA figures (11.31x vs. 9.82x) and the payout ratio spread (57% vs. 80%) — the data as provided is insufficient to reach a clear, unqualified judgment on whether EPD is cheap or expensive.** The P/E comparison leans toward "reasonably valued to cheap relative to peers, roughly in line with sector," but this single data point is not enough to override the unresolved conflicts in the EV/EBITDA and distribution-sustainability metrics, which are core to a midstream MLP valuation. Resolving these would require going to primary sources (the 10-Q and Q2 2026 earnings release) rather than relying on this secondary, web-search-derived dataset.
