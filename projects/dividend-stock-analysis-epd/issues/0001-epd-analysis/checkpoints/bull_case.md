# Bull Case: Enterprise Products Partners L.P. (EPD)

## 1. Operating momentum is real and accelerating

Even without a multi-quarter revenue trend, the Q2 2026 operating metrics that *do* have YoY comparisons all point the same direction — up, and by wide margins:

- **Operational DCF of $2.3B, up 21% YoY** — a quarterly record.
- **Pipeline volumes of 14.7 MMBPD, up 8% YoY.**
- **Marine terminal volumes of 2.8 MMBPD, up 33% YoY** — a standout figure suggesting EPD's export/terminal infrastructure is seeing outsized demand growth.
- Reported adjusted EPS of $0.84 came in above the cited consensus of $0.77, and Q2 is independently described as beating consensus on both revenue and earnings.
- **ROE of 20.9%**, flagged in the data as an "excellent metric," supports the case that this cash-flow growth is being generated efficiently on the existing asset base.

Whatever the ambiguity around margins or multi-year trend lines, a partnership growing DCF 21% and marine volumes 33% year-over-year in the same quarter it beat consensus is showing clear underlying business momentum.

## 2. A 28-year distribution growth streak, now with rising coverage

EPD has raised its distribution for **28 consecutive years** — a track record few midstream or energy peers can match, and one that survived multiple commodity cycles. The most recent data point shows continued growth (2.8% YoY per-unit increase to an annualized $0.56/unit).

On coverage, the sequence of figures reported across the year — 1.7x (FY2025) → 1.8x/1.85x (Q1 2026) → **1.9x (Q2 2026 earnings release)** — is, taken at face value, a steadily improving trend. A coverage ratio near 1.9x is comfortably above 1.0x and implies a wide cushion between operating cash flow and the distribution, even before counting the **$159M in Q2 unit buybacks**, which represent an additional, discretionary return of capital layered on top of the distribution itself.

## 3. Attractively valued versus peers, with a superior yield-to-risk profile

- **P/E of ~13.5x–13.6x** sits well below the peer-group average of ~19.6x–20.3x, while only marginally above the broader sector average (12.6x) — the cleanest, most-agreed-upon valuation data point in the entire dataset.
- **Forward EV/EBITDA of 9.0x–9.5x** places EPD in the middle of its three-peer set — cheaper than Enbridge (~11.5x forward) while still commanding a premium to the more leveraged Energy Transfer (~8.0x forward), which reads as the market pricing EPD's lower-leverage, investment-grade-style profile fairly.
- **Distribution yield of 5.66%** is meaningfully above Enbridge's 5.03%, offering more income than a comparable C-corp peer, while sitting at a more moderate (arguably safer) level than Energy Transfer's 7.21% — consistent with EPD occupying a "quality plus yield" middle ground rather than a distressed high-yield outlier.
- Three independent analyst shops — **JPMorgan ($41→$42), Raymond James ($40→$42), and Scotiabank ($39→$40)** — all raised price targets around the same period, a directionally consistent signal even amid ratings-label dispersion.

## 4. Technical picture is unambiguously bullish

This is the one dimension of the dataset explicitly described as having **no internal disagreement among sources**:

- **12 buy signals, 0 sell signals** across tracked moving averages.
- A **"Strong Buy" summary classification.**
- A **recently occurred golden cross.**
- The stock **trading above all major moving averages.**
- Daily RSI(14) of 60.48, explicitly characterized as "healthy buying pressure, not overbought" — i.e., the trend has room to run on the timeframe institutional trend-followers typically use, even if shorter intraday windows show hotter momentum.

A confirmed golden cross combined with a clean sweep of moving-average buy signals is a textbook technical case for continuation of an uptrend.

## 5. A structurally advantaged, hard-to-replicate competitive position

EPD's position at the **Mont Belvieu pricing hub — influencing over 95% of U.S. NGL production pricing** — is close to a price-setting franchise rather than a mere pipeline asset, and is the kind of infrastructure moat that's very difficult for a competitor to replicate from scratch. Layered on top of this is **brownfield economics in LPG export expansion**, meaning EPD can grow capacity by leveraging existing infrastructure rather than building greenfield facilities — a structurally more capital-efficient growth path. With **$51.6B in annual revenue** and **50,000+ miles of pipeline** across four diversified business segments (NGLs, crude, natural gas, petrochemicals/refined products), EPD has scale and diversification that smaller single-segment midstream players lack.

## 6. Leadership transition is a non-event, and governance was reinforced, not disrupted

The CEO succession (Teague → Fowler) is about as low-risk as a leadership change can be structured:

- **Internal, sitting co-CEO successor** — no outside-hire strategy-reset risk.
- **~5-month announced runway** to the transition date, not an abrupt handover.
- The **Office of the Chairman was formally expanded** to include the CEO, chief commercial officer, and CFO specifically as a continuity mechanism.
- Outgoing CEO Teague's tenure is credited with growing the company from **$1.8B to almost $120B** in enterprise value and pioneering the "wellhead-to-water" NGL strategy that Fowler inherits intact — the strategic playbook that current growth (marine terminal volumes +33% YoY) is still executing on.

## 7. Sentiment is net-positive with zero outright Sell calls

Across 21 covering institutions, the aggregate rating is **Buy — 13 Buy, 6 Hold, 0 Sell.** No analyst in the tally has an outright bearish rating on the stock.

---

### A note on data quality, in the interest of a *good-faith* case

This bull case leans on the coherent, mutually-reinforcing data points above (volume/DCF growth, the 28-year streak with rising coverage, the peer-relative P/E discount, the unambiguous technical trend, the Mont Belvieu moat, the low-drama succession, and the no-Sell-rated sentiment tally). It should be acknowledged directly, not glossed over, that the underlying dataset contains real unresolved contradictions elsewhere — dual EV/EBITDA figures (11.31x vs. 9.82x), a payout ratio spread (56%/57% vs. 80%), conflicting 2026 capex guidance ($2.2–2.5B vs. $2.9–3.4B), and two analysts (TD Cowen, Morgan Stanley) that *cut* targets in the same window others raised them. None of these contradictions are resolved by the data provided, and a rigorous bull case doesn't pretend otherwise — but none of them negate the specific, better-sourced positive data points above, which stand on their own even after discounting the noisier metrics.
