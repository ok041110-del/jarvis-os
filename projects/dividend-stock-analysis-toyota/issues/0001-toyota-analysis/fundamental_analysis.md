## Toyota Motor Corporation (7203.T / TM) — Fundamental Analysis

**Revenue and volume trend**

For FY2026 (fiscal year ended March 2026), Toyota reported consolidated revenue of ¥50.68 trillion, up 5.5% year-over-year, alongside unit sales of 9.595 million vehicles, up 2.5% year-over-year. Revenue growth outpaced volume growth (5.5% vs. 2.5%), which is consistent with either higher average selling prices, a richer product/regional mix, or currency translation effects — but the provided data does not break out which of these factors drove the gap, so no specific attribution can be made.

**Earnings and margin trend**

Earnings moved in the opposite direction of revenue. Operating income fell to ¥3,766.216 billion, down 21.5% from ¥4,795.586 billion the prior year, and net income fell to ¥3,848.098 billion, down 19.2%, with basic EPS declining from ¥359.56 to ¥295.25. Since revenue rose while operating income fell sharply, this implies a material compression in operating margin for FY2026 — operating margin would have moved from roughly 9.9% to roughly 7.4% on the figures given (calculated from the revenue and operating income figures provided, not stated directly in the source data).

The stated drivers of this margin compression are: US tariff impact of approximately ¥1.38 trillion, higher R&D and personnel costs, and foreign-exchange effects, partially offset by marketing efforts and cost reduction. Separately, tariff costs are reported at up to $9 billion on a fiscal-year basis, described as the largest such cost among major automakers — though the data does not clarify whether this $9B figure is the same as, overlaps with, or is additional to the ¥1.38 trillion figure cited as the FY2026 tariff drag.

**A significant inconsistency in the data**

The data set also includes a separate report that Q1 (April–June) profit for FY2026 rose 76% year-over-year to ¥1.48 trillion. This is directionally opposite to the full-year operating income decline of 21.5%. The data explicitly notes this contradiction but does not explain it — it only observes that the two figures apparently refer to different periods (FY2026 full year vs. FY2026 Q1). Because no reconciling detail is provided (e.g., quarterly progression through the year, one-time items, or a restated comparison basis), it is not possible from this data alone to determine how a year that ended down 21.5% on operating income contained a quarter that was up 76% on profit. This should be treated as an open question rather than resolved.

**Guidance**

For FY2027 (April 2026–March 2027), Toyota raised its outlook: operating income guidance of ¥3.4 trillion (up from a prior forecast of ¥3.0 trillion), revenue guidance of ¥51.0 trillion, and net income guidance of ¥3.0 trillion. Taken at face value, this guidance implies:
- Revenue growth of roughly 0.6% over FY2026's ¥50.68 trillion — essentially flat.
- Operating income guidance of ¥3.4 trillion would still sit below FY2026's actual ¥3.766 trillion, implying further year-over-year operating income decline even after the upward revision from the original ¥3.0 trillion forecast.
- Net income guidance of ¥3.0 trillion would be below FY2026's actual ¥3.848 trillion, implying a further decline in net income.

So while the guidance was revised upward relative to Toyota's own prior forecast, the absolute guided figures for FY2027 remain below FY2026's actual results for both operating and net income, based strictly on the numbers provided. The data does not explain the gap between "raised guidance" framing and guided profit levels that are still below the prior year's actuals — it's possible the original FY2027 forecast (before the ¥3.0T→¥3.4T revision) was set very conservatively, but that is not stated.

**Additional gaps flagged in the data**

- EV-related guidance is internally inconsistent in the source material: a reported reduction in EV production targets (to 800,000 units) appears alongside a reported expansion in EV model count (from 5 to 15 models), with no explanation of how these two directions relate.
- No balance sheet, cash flow, free cash flow, capex, or segment-level data (e.g., regional or business-line breakdown) is included, so this analysis is limited to top-line revenue, operating/net income, EPS, and forward guidance only.
- The tariff and margin narrative is sourced from press summaries rather than the primary financial statements, per the data's own caveats.

**Summary of what the data supports**

FY2026 shows a company with modest top-line growth (revenue +5.5%, volume +2.5%) but substantial profitability compression (operating income -21.5%, net income -19.2%), attributed primarily to US tariffs, cost inflation, and FX effects. FY2027 guidance was raised versus Toyota's own prior forecast but, on the absolute figures given, still implies operating and net income below FY2026 actual levels — meaning the guidance, as stated, does not point to a full profit recovery to prior-year levels within the guided year. The Q1 profit figure (+76%) directly contradicts the full-year direction and cannot be reconciled from the data provided; this should be treated as an unresolved inconsistency rather than incorporated into the trend read.
