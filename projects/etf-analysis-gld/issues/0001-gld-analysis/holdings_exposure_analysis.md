## Holdings & Exposure Analysis — SPDR Gold Shares (GLD)

**Concentration is total and by design.** GLD is not a diversified portfolio in any conventional sense: 100% of the fund's holdings consist of allocated gold bullion held in vaults in London. There are no individual securities, no issuers, and no "positions" to speak of — the fund's entire economic exposure is to the price of a single physical commodity, gold. Framed against typical concentration metrics (top-10 holdings weight, issuer concentration, single-name risk), those metrics simply do not apply here, because there is no underlying basket of names to concentrate across. The fund's concentration, in effect, is 100% in one asset.

**Sector, credit-quality, and maturity breakdowns are not applicable to this fund.** These are analytical frameworks built for equity or fixed-income portfolios — sector classification presumes multiple issuers across industries, credit-quality distribution presumes rated debt obligations, and maturity distribution presumes instruments with a term structure. Gold bullion has none of these properties: it is not issued by any entity, carries no credit or counterparty risk in the way a bond does, and does not mature. Reporting "N/A" against these categories is not a data gap here — it reflects a genuine mismatch between the analytical framework (designed for something like an aggregate bond fund) and the nature of the asset itself.

**Geographic reference is custodial, not an exposure dimension.** The bullion's location in London vaults describes where the physical metal is stored, not a geographic allocation or diversification choice. It should not be read as the fund having "exposure to the UK" in any economic sense — the fund's value moves with the global gold price, not with any UK-specific economic factor.

**What the fund's exposure structure actually is:** a single-factor exposure to spot gold prices, with the fund's shares designed to track (before fees and expenses) the performance of that physical bullion holding. The only meaningful "exposure" question for this fund is its sensitivity to the price of gold itself — there is no sub-structure beneath that to analyze.
