## GLD (SPDR Gold Shares) — Cost & Tracking Analysis

**Cost structure.** GLD carries a total expense ratio of 0.40%, which is meaningfully higher than two direct competitors also tracking physical gold: GLDM at 0.10% and iShares Gold Trust (IAU) at 0.25%. On a pure cost basis, GLD is the most expensive of the three. The data provided attributes GLD's continued prominence not to cost but to liquidity — it is described as having the largest trading volume among the group. This is a real trade-off: a lower expense ratio compounds favorably over long holding periods, while higher liquidity/volume can matter more for investors who trade frequently or need tight bid-ask spreads for large orders. The data does not include actual bid-ask spread figures for any of the three funds, so the liquidity advantage is stated qualitatively, not quantified.

**How "tracking" is defined for this fund.** Unlike an index-tracking ETF, GLD's stated investment objective is to reflect the performance of spot gold minus the fund's expenses and liabilities. That means the relevant benchmark for tracking purposes is gold's spot price, not a published index. Framed this way, the primary driver of tracking difference is structural and largely predictable: the expense ratio itself. All else equal, a fund with this objective would be expected to lag spot gold by roughly its expense ratio over time, before accounting for any secondary factors (e.g., trading costs, cash drag, or timing differences in valuation) that are not detailed in the data provided.

**Missing tracking-error figure.** No explicit numeric tracking error (in basis points or any other unit) is present in the source data. This is a significant gap for a tracking assessment — it means there is no empirical measure here of how closely GLD has actually followed spot gold beyond the theoretical expectation implied by the expense ratio. I'm flagging this explicitly rather than estimating or substituting the expense ratio as a proxy for realized tracking error, since the two are conceptually related but not the same thing.

**Data inconsistency to flag.** The source data contains conflicting YTD return figures for GLD — one figure shows +2.17% (dividend-reinvested basis) and another shows -5.42%. These are not reconcilable from the information given (they may reflect different time windows, different total-return conventions, or a data error at the source), and I am not resolving or picking between them. Both are reported here as-is; any conclusion drawn from YTD performance should account for this unresolved discrepancy.

**Other limitations.** The data does not include volatility, beta, duration, or any other quantitative risk metrics, nor does it include actual historical tracking data versus spot gold. The figures were collected via web search as of 2026-08-16 and are not live/real-time data, so current expense ratios, volumes, or prices may have changed since collection.

**Summary of what can and cannot be said.** Based solely on the data provided: GLD is the highest-cost option among the three gold funds compared but reportedly has an offsetting liquidity advantage; its tracking objective is defined relative to spot gold with the expense ratio as the primary stated driver of expected divergence; and no actual tracking-error statistic is available to confirm how well it has performed against that objective in practice. This is not sufficient information to characterize the fund's realized tracking quality with precision, only its theoretical cost-driven expectation. This analysis is not a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold this fund.
