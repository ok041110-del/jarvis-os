## Fundamental Analysis: NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA)

**Basis of analysis:** This assessment is drawn solely from the Q1 FY2027 results (quarter ended April 26, 2026, reported May 20, 2026) and accompanying Q2 FY2027 guidance as summarized above. No balance sheet, cash flow, operating expense, or segment detail beyond Data Center revenue was provided, and no prior-quarter guidance was included for comparison. The analysis is bounded strictly by these figures.

**Revenue trend.** NVIDIA reported Q1 FY2027 revenue of $81.6 billion, up 20% sequentially and 85% year-over-year. Growth was overwhelmingly concentrated in the Data Center segment, which reached $75.2 billion — a record — up 21% quarter-over-quarter and 92% year-over-year. Data Center now represents roughly 92% of total company revenue, meaning the company's overall growth trajectory is, on this data, effectively a function of Data Center demand alone. No other segment (Gaming, Professional Visualization, Automotive, etc.) was broken out, so the extent to which those businesses are growing, flat, or contracting cannot be assessed from what's given.

**Earnings.** GAAP EPS was $2.39 versus Non-GAAP EPS of $1.87. This is worth flagging explicitly: it is atypical for GAAP EPS to exceed Non-GAAP EPS, since Non-GAAP figures normally exclude stock-based compensation and other charges that would otherwise depress the GAAP number. The provided data contains no explanation for this gap (e.g., a tax benefit, a one-time gain, or an equity/investment mark-to-market item), so the driver cannot be determined and should not be assumed. This is a data gap, not a resolved fact.

**Margin trend.** Gross margins were essentially identical on a GAAP and Non-GAAP basis — 74.9% and 75.0%, respectively — indicating limited distortion from non-GAAP adjustments at the gross-margin line specifically (even though the EPS lines diverge). No prior-quarter gross margin figure was provided, so whether margins are expanding, holding steady, or compressing sequentially cannot be established from this data alone; only the guidance (below) provides a forward reference point.

**Guidance and its implications.** Q2 FY2027 guidance calls for revenue of $91.0 billion (±2%), which at the midpoint implies roughly 11.5% sequential growth — a deceleration from the 20% sequential growth just reported in Q1, though still substantial in absolute dollar terms (roughly $9.4 billion of sequential revenue addition at the midpoint). Gross margin guidance of 74.9%/75.0% (GAAP/Non-GAAP, ±50bp) is essentially flat versus the just-reported quarter, implying management expects the current cost/pricing structure to hold rather than expand or compress materially in the near term.

The single most consequential disclosure in the guidance is that it **excludes any Data Center compute revenue from China**. Given that Data Center is ~92% of total revenue and has been the primary growth driver, this exclusion means two things simultaneously: (1) the $91.0 billion guide understates potential revenue if China sales were to resume or be permitted during the quarter, representing a source of guidance upside not currently priced into the number; and (2) it signals that China market access remains an unresolved, material variable for the business — the guidance is effectively built on an assumption of zero contribution from what has historically been a significant regional market, which points to ongoing export-control or geopolitical constraints rather than a demand issue. The data provided does not specify the dollar magnitude of China Data Center revenue in prior periods, so the size of this excluded opportunity cannot be quantified from what's given here.

**Summary of what the data does and doesn't support.** The figures support a picture of continued, Data-Center-driven high growth with stable, very high gross margins. They do not support any conclusion about profitability trends below the gross-margin line, expense trajectory, segment diversification, balance sheet health, or the cause of the GAAP/Non-GAAP EPS divergence — those would require additional data not present in this summary. The China exclusion in guidance is a explicitly disclosed known unknown, not an inferred one, and should be treated as a distinct, unresolved swing factor for the forecast period.
