## NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) — Analyst Sentiment Analysis
*Based on data collected 2026-08-08*

**Overall Tone**

The available analyst data points to a broadly positive consensus on NVDA. S&P Global's aggregation of 61 covering institutions characterizes the consensus as "Strong Buy," and a separate source covering a larger pool of 79 institutions shows a rating distribution skewed heavily toward bullishness: 60 analysts at Buy/Strong Buy versus 16 at Hold and only 3 at Sell/Strong Sell. On its face, this suggests the sell-side community is largely constructive on the stock, with dissenting views (Hold or Sell) representing a minority.

**Price Target Levels and Reliability**

Average price targets across the two sources provided are $302.83 (61-institution S&P Global aggregate) and $319.48 (79-institution separate source), both measured against a reference current price of $223.96 in the second source. These two averages differ by roughly $17, which itself is a modest but notable gap given they are meant to represent similar consensus figures — a reminder that "consensus" numbers can vary meaningfully depending on which analysts are included and when the data was pulled.

More significant for assessing reliability is the spread of individual price targets: the 1-year target range runs from a low of $180 (-19.63% from current price) to a high of $500 (+123.25%). This is an extremely wide dispersion — the high-end target is roughly 2.8 times the low-end target. A range this wide indicates substantial disagreement among analysts about NVDA's near-term prospects, whether driven by differing views on AI/semiconductor demand sustainability, competitive dynamics, valuation multiples, or other factors not specified in the data provided. When individual estimates diverge this much, the average target figure ($302.83 or $319.48) should be treated with caution as a single "expected" outcome — it masks a genuine split in analyst opinion rather than reflecting tight agreement around a central case.

**Inconsistency in Upside Percentage**

The data also contains an explicit inconsistency that should be flagged rather than reconciled: one source states the average 1-year target implies +42.7% upside from the current price, while another states the figure is +35.22%. These are not close enough to be rounding differences, and the source material does not explain the discrepancy — it may stem from different aggregation dates, different analyst pools, or different "current price" reference points, but none of that is confirmed in the data provided. This inconsistency itself is a data point about sentiment reliability: even the basic "how much upside does consensus imply" figure is not stable across sources at this snapshot in time.

**Summary Assessment**

Taken together, the data shows a sell-side community that is directionally positive on NVDA (majority Buy/Strong Buy ratings, positive average targets across both sources), but the reliability of any single summary statistic is undermined by two factors present in the data itself: (1) a very wide 1-year target range ($180–$500), indicating real disagreement among individual analysts rather than convergence around a shared estimate, and (2) conflicting upside percentages (+42.7% vs. +35.22%) reported by different sources for what should be a comparable figure. Given these internal inconsistencies, the data supports a characterization of "generally positive but not tightly aligned" sentiment — it does not support treating the average price target as a precise or highly reliable point estimate. No further data (e.g., time-series of target revisions, breakdown of which firms hold the extreme high/low targets, or the exact aggregation methodology) is provided that would allow resolution of these discrepancies or a finer-grained reliability judgment.
