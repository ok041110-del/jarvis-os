## Fundamental Analysis — Nestlé S.A. (NESN.SW / NSRGY), H1 2026

**Revenue and organic growth trend**

For the first half of 2026, Nestlé reported sales of CHF 43.1 billion with organic growth of +3.6%, made up of +1.5% real internal growth (RIG — volume/mix) and +2.1% from pricing. This growth accelerated slightly in the second quarter on a standalone basis (+3.7% organic, with RIG of +1.8% and pricing of +1.9%), suggesting the volume component strengthened somewhat quarter-over-quarter while pricing contribution moderated marginally. Growth was notably uneven by geography: emerging markets excluding China grew organically +7.1% (RIG +3.9%), more than three times the pace of developed markets, which grew +2.3% organically with RIG of just +0.6%. This implies that unit volume growth in developed markets was close to flat, with most of the reported growth there coming from price, while emerging markets (ex-China) delivered materially stronger volume-driven growth. China's contribution is not broken out in the data provided, so no conclusion can be drawn about that market specifically.

**Earnings trend**

The earnings picture is inconsistent depending on which metric is used, and the data provided does not resolve this. On a net profit (GAAP) basis, H1 2026 net income was CHF 3.5 billion, down 31.4% from CHF 5.1 billion in H1 2025 — a sharp decline. On an "underlying" (adjusted) basis, however, net profit was CHF 5.7 billion, down only 2.4% year-over-year, or actually up 3.4% at constant currency. These two figures move in opposite directions in growth terms (GAAP sharply down, adjusted roughly flat-to-up), and the source material does not specify what accounts for the roughly CHF 2.2 billion gap between reported and underlying net profit, nor does it identify any one-time items, impairments, or restructuring charges responsible for the GAAP decline. Without that detail, it is not possible from this data alone to determine whether the underlying earnings power of the business actually weakened, improved, or was masked by non-recurring items.

**Margin trend**

The data indicates management expects UTOP (underlying trading operating profit) margin to improve in 2026 versus 2025, per guidance. No specific H1 2026 UTOP margin figure (in percentage terms) is provided in the data, so the magnitude of any margin movement realized so far this half cannot be assessed — only the forward expectation is available.

**Cash flow and cost program**

Free cash flow improved to CHF 3.4 billion in H1 2026 from CHF 2.3 billion in H1 2025, a year-over-year improvement of roughly 48%. This cash flow improvement stands in contrast to the GAAP net income decline, which is consistent with the earnings drop being driven at least partly by non-cash or non-operating items — though again, the data does not confirm this explicitly. Separately, the company's "Fuel for Growth" cost-savings program has delivered CHF 1.7 billion cumulatively against a 2026 target of CHF 2 billion, indicating the program is close to (roughly 85% of) its full-year target with two quarters remaining.

**Guidance and what it implies**

Management reiterated and narrowed full-year 2026 organic growth guidance to a 3–4% range, consistent with the H1 print of +3.6% sitting roughly mid-range — implying management does not expect a significant acceleration or deceleration in the second half relative to H1 trends. Guidance also calls for UTOP margin expansion versus 2025 and free cash flow above CHF 9 billion for the full year; H1 FCF of CHF 3.4 billion would require H2 FCF of at least CHF 5.6 billion to meet that target, implying management expects a materially stronger cash generation in the second half, which is a typical seasonal pattern for Nestlé but is not independently confirmed by the data provided.

**Explicit data gaps**

Several things this data does not support a conclusion on:
- The cause of the 31.4% GAAP net profit decline (no one-time items or charges are identified).
- The actual H1 2026 UTOP margin figure or its year-over-year change (only forward guidance for full-year improvement is given).
- China's specific organic growth contribution.
- Any reconciliation between GAAP and underlying net profit beyond the two headline figures.

Given the divergence between GAAP and underlying earnings and the absence of an explanation for it, this data is insufficient to characterize overall earnings quality or trend with confidence — only the organic sales growth trend, the FCF improvement, and the guidance figures can be stated with the precision the source material provides.
