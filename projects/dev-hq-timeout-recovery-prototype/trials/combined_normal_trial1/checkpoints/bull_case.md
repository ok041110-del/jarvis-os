# Bull Case: Nestlé S.A. (NESN.SW / NSRGY)

## 1. Growth is real, balanced, and accelerating where it matters most

Nestlé's H1 2026 organic growth of +3.6% is not a pricing-driven mirage — it splits roughly evenly between +1.5% real internal growth (volume/mix) and +2.1% pricing, a healthier composition than a company simply pushing through price hikes while volumes erode. More encouraging: the standalone Q2 figure came in at +3.7% organic with RIG *rising* to +1.8% — momentum, if anything, built through the half rather than fading.

The real growth engine is emerging markets ex-China, which posted +7.1% organic growth with +3.9% RIG — genuine, volume-backed demand running at roughly three times the developed-market organic rate (+2.3%, RIG only +0.6%). For a bull, this is the crux of the growth story: Nestlé has a large, underappreciated demand engine in emerging markets that is growing on volume, not just price, giving the topline a structural growth lever beyond what mature Western markets can offer. Full-year guidance of 3–4% organic growth was maintained and narrowed — not cut — consistent with the H1 actual, indicating management has visibility and confidence in the trajectory.

## 2. The "underlying" earnings picture, not the GAAP headline, is the better guide to earnings power

Yes, GAAP net profit fell -31.4%. But underlying net profit — the metric that strips out one-off items — was down just -2.4% in reported currency and *up* +3.4% at constant currency. A bull reads this gap as exactly what it looks like: a company whose core earnings power held up or improved, dragged down on paper by non-recurring items (impairments, restructuring charges, disposals) rather than deteriorating operations. This reading is reinforced by the fact that free cash flow rose sharply in the same period — from CHF 2.3B to CHF 3.4B H1-over-H1 — which is very hard to reconcile with a genuine -31.4% collapse in earnings power. Cash doesn't lie the way accounting charges can distort a headline number; rising FCF alongside a GAAP net-income drop is a pattern consistent with one-off charges, not structural decay.

Management's own guidance backs this: UTOP margin is expected to *improve* versus 2025, and full-year FCF guidance sits at CHF 9B or higher. A company guiding to margin expansion and record-level cash generation is not behaving like one with a genuinely broken earnings base.

## 3. The cost program is ahead of pace and getting more ambitious, not less

The "Fuel for Growth" program has already banked CHF 1.7B against a CHF 2B full-year 2026 target — roughly 85% delivered before year-end. Separately, the broader restructuring savings target was raised from CHF 3.13B to CHF 3.76B, and the associated ~16,000-role cut spans both white-collar overhead and manufacturing/supply chain — a comprehensive efficiency push, not a token gesture. A bull can reasonably argue that raising the savings target after the initial announcement signals management conviction that there is more efficiency to capture, layering a structural margin tailwind on top of the topline growth story just discussed.

## 4. New leadership brings strategic focus, and the portfolio is being sharpened accordingly

The new CEO has articulated a clear five-year direction built around four core categories — coffee, pet care, nutrition, and food/snacks — and the market has already seen this in action via the Blue Bottle Coffee divestment to Centurium Capital. For a bull, a company voluntarily shedding non-core assets to concentrate capital and management attention on categories where it already holds leadership positions is the textbook playbook that has worked for large-cap consumer staples peers pursuing similar slimming strategies. Nestlé enters this repositioning from a position of scale strength: at ~$89.9–92B in annual revenue, it is the largest food & beverage company in the dataset, roughly $30B ahead of #2 Unilever (~$60B) — meaning even a modest execution win on focus and efficiency is being applied to the largest revenue base in the peer set.

## 5. Valuation is not a stretch, and multiple independent signals point toward undervaluation

Against the narrow, named peer set, Nestlé is mid-pack, not expensive — above Unilever's 12.93x trailing P/E but below Danone's 22.54x, with Danone's multiple described as in-line with its own 10-year median. That is a defensible, non-extreme valuation position among direct comparables.

More strikingly, both DCF estimates provided — despite disagreeing on the reference price — point in the same *direction*: undervaluation, with one source implying +46.1% upside and the other implying a similarly large upside on its own price basis. Two independently sourced DCF models converging on "undervalued," even if their absolute numbers don't reconcile, is a directional signal a bull is entitled to lean on. Layer on the trailing/forward P/E gap (28.53x vs. 17.62x) — consistent with the market pricing in a forward earnings recovery — and the valuation backdrop supports a re-rating case as underlying earnings (which, per point 2, look healthier than the GAAP headline) show through in reported numbers over coming quarters.

## 6. Sentiment has moved in the bull's favor, and the technical setup offers a reasonable entry

The analyst consensus is "Buy" (3 buy / 1 hold / 1 sell) with an average target price of $113.68, implying +18.91% upside from the reference price used in that dataset. Notably, this consensus reportedly improved from "Hold" a year earlier (2025-08) to "Buy" now (2026-08) — even accounting for uncertainty about whether that shift reflects genuine view changes or coverage composition, a move from Hold to Buy at minimum does not indicate deteriorating sentiment, and a bull can fairly characterize the directional trend as improving.

Technically, price (79.06) sits above both the 5-day (72.04) and 50-day (71.85) moving averages — a short/medium-term buy signal — and is also above the 200-day average (74.52) in absolute terms. RSI at 50.83 is neutral, meaning there's no overbought condition capping near-term upside, and price is trading just above a nearby support level (78.99), suggesting a favorable risk/reward entry with a defined downside reference point. A separate cited article projects a rebound to a $87 target (from a $71 base), which — if directionally credible — would support further upside from current levels.

## 7. Dividend track record signals a management culture oriented toward shareholder returns

Sixteen consecutive years of dividend increases, with the latest hike at +4.09%, is a long, uninterrupted record that most companies cannot match. Combined with a 3.32% headline yield, this gives income-oriented bulls a tangible reason to hold through the current restructuring noise, on the view that a board with a 16-year record of raising the dividend is unlikely to have let it become disconnected from underlying (as opposed to GAAP) earnings power — particularly given the underlying-profit and FCF improvement discussed above.

---

## Where this case is data-dependent, stated plainly

A fair bull case has to acknowledge, not paper over, the places where it leans on an inference the source material itself flags as unconfirmed:

- The core argument in Section 2 — that the GAAP -31.4% decline is a one-off and underlying earnings power is intact — is a **plausible inference from the FCF/underlying-profit pattern, not a fact the data confirms**. The source material is explicit that it does not identify what caused the CHF ~2.2B gap between reported and underlying profit.
- The DCF convergence in Section 5 is directional agreement from two sources whose baseline prices disagree by ~31%; it is suggestive, not a validated single fair-value estimate.
- The Hold→Buy sentiment shift in Section 6 could reflect coverage-panel composition changes rather than genuine view improvement — the data cannot distinguish the two.
- The technical picture is mixed in the underlying data (aggregate signal count skews sell-heavy even though the specific MAs and price-vs-support levels cited above are bullish); this case selects the more bullish-supporting indicators, which a bear would weight differently.

None of these gaps invalidate the bull thesis, but a rigorous reader should treat points 2, 5, and 6 above as reasoned bets on ambiguous data rather than settled facts.
