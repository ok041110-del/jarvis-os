# Nestlé S.A. (NESN.SW / NSRGY) — Final Research Report
*Based on web-search data collected as of 2026-08-16. Not live data.*

## Fundamental
H1 2026 sales reached CHF 43.1B, organic growth +3.6% (RIG +1.5%, pricing +2.1%) — a balanced mix, with Q2 alone slightly stronger (+3.7%, RIG +1.8%). Emerging markets ex-China grew +7.1% (RIG +3.9%), roughly 3x developed markets (+2.3%, RIG only +0.6%), meaning developed-market growth is almost entirely price-driven. **China is excluded from every EM figure with no standalone number given.** FCF improved to CHF 3.4B (from CHF 2.3B); FY guidance is CHF 9B+. The "Fuel for Growth" program has banked CHF 1.7B of its CHF 2B 2026 target. FY organic guidance (3–4%) was maintained. **Key inconsistency:** GAAP net profit fell -31.4% (CHF 5.1B→3.5B) while underlying net profit was roughly flat/+3.4% CC (CHF 5.7B) — a ~CHF 2.2B gap the data does not explain (no impairment/restructuring/disposal itemized). No historical margin data exists to confirm the guided UTOP improvement.

## Dividend Quality
16 consecutive years of dividend increases, latest hike +4.09%, headline yield 3.32%. **No FCF-based payout data is provided at all**, so cash coverage cannot be assessed. The earnings-based payout ratio is 86.9% (vs. JNJ's 46.19%), but it's unclear whether it's GAAP or adjusted, and its relationship to the -31.4% net-income drop is not established — either the ratio already reflects depressed earnings (thin cushion) or predates the drop (likely to worsen). Nestlé pays annually (vs. US peers' quarterly), limiting interim visibility. Swiss withholding tax is 35%, reducible to 15% for US residents via treaty claim (not automatic).

## Valuation
Forward P/E (17.6–17.7x) is 23% above the CPG sector median (14.32x) — rich on this comparison. Against named peers, Nestlé sits mid-pack: above Unilever (12.93x, historically cheap for Unilever) and below Danone (22.54x, in-line for Danone). Trailing P/E (28.53x) is ~62% above forward P/E, implying a priced-in earnings rebound, plausibly but not confirmed to be linked to the net-income decline. **DCF data is contradictory and unusable as presented:** one model gives fair value $113.62 vs. price $77.76 (+46.1% upside); another gives $179.01 vs. $102 — reference prices disagree by ~31%, with no stated cause (ADR vs. ordinary shares, FX, or date). **Market cap is also reported as both $258.56B and $385B (~1.5x discrepancy, unreconciled)**, and CHF/USD figures are mixed without an exchange rate throughout the dataset.

## Technical
Price: 79.06 (currency mixed CHF/USD per source). 5-day (72.04) and 50-day (71.85) MAs signal "buy"; the 200-day (74.52) is labeled "sell" despite price sitting above it — an unexplained internal contradiction. RSI(14) = 50.83, neutral. Support 78.99 / resistance 81.65 — price sits almost exactly on support. Aggregate signal count is 8 sell vs. 4 buy, yet a separate cited article projects a rebound to an $87 target (+23% from $71) — directly contradicting the sell-weighted picture, with no reconciliation offered. Currency ambiguity and lack of source verification further limit confidence.

## Industry
Nestlé is the largest food & beverage company by revenue (~$89.9–92B), roughly $30B ahead of #2 Unilever (~$60B); Danone is much smaller (€27.3B revenue, $53B market cap). The ~1.5x market-cap discrepancy noted above limits confident cross-peer valuation comparison. New CEO strategy focuses on four core categories (coffee, pet care, nutrition, food/snacks), evidenced by the Blue Bottle Coffee sale to Centurium Capital (price undisclosed). A reported "AI-driven premium nutrition" entrant with 12% share in an unspecified premium segment lacks brand name, segment definition, or source verification — flagged as unconfirmed.

## News/Event
Former CEO Laurent Freixe was dismissed for cause after an internal investigation (substance undisclosed); Chairman Paul Bulcke also departed early; Philipp Navratil is the new CEO — a concentrated governance disruption. Restructuring was expanded: ~16,000 job cuts (12,000 white-collar, 4,000 manufacturing/supply chain) and savings target raised from CHF 3.13B to CHF 3.76B. The restructuring was announced October 2025, predating the H1-2026 earnings report (July 2026), but **no causal link between the leadership change, restructuring, and the -31.4% net-income decline is established in the data.**

## Sentiment
Consensus across 5 institutions: 3 Buy / 1 Hold / 1 Sell = "Buy," average target $113.68 (+18.91% implied upside). Target range ($97–$127.40) is wide relative to the average. The same coverage reportedly showed "Hold" in 2025-08 vs. "Buy" now — the data cannot distinguish genuine sentiment improvement from a change in the institutional panel composition. Target prices (USD) cannot be reconciled against technical levels (apparently CHF) absent an FX rate.

## Bull Case
Growth is volume-and-price balanced with accelerating EM momentum; FCF growth alongside GAAP profit decline suggests the earnings hit is one-off, not structural; cost programs are ahead of pace and being expanded; new leadership is sharpening portfolio focus from a scale-leading revenue base; valuation is mid-pack vs. named peers with two (non-reconciled but directionally aligned) DCF models suggesting undervaluation; sentiment has moved Hold→Buy; technicals show price above short/medium-term MAs near support with neutral RSI. Bulls concede several of these are inferences from ambiguous data, not confirmed facts.

## Bear Case
GAAP profit fell 31.4% with the reconciling detail undisclosed — the "it's just one-off items" defense is unproven. Developed-market growth is almost entirely price-driven (RIG +0.6%), and China's exclusion from EM figures could be masking weakness. The 86.9% payout ratio is high in isolation and its relationship to the profit drop is unresolved. Forward P/E is rich vs. the sector median, and the DCF "cheap" argument is explicitly unusable given contradictory baselines. Technicals are sell-weighted (8:4) with price sitting on support. Governance disruption (for-cause CEO exit, early chairman departure) coincides with an escalating restructuring that could reflect cost pressure rather than confidence. The Buy sentiment consensus may be a small-sample/coverage artifact rather than genuine improvement.

## Synthesis
Bull and bear draw on the same source material; disagreement is mostly interpretive (how to weight ambiguous figures), not factual. The load-bearing unknowns that would most change the conclusion: (1) what makes up the ~CHF 2.2B GAAP/underlying profit gap; (2) China's standalone growth figure; (3) whether the 86.9% payout ratio predates or reflects the profit decline, and the FCF-based payout ratio; (4) reconciliation of the ~31% DCF baseline gap; (5) the substance behind the CEO's dismissal; (6) whether the Hold→Buy shift is genuine or a sampling artifact. The dataset also contains unresolved internal inconsistencies independent of interpretation: a ~1.5x market-cap discrepancy, a ~31% DCF baseline mismatch, mixed CHF/USD reporting without FX rates, and a 200-day MA labeled "sell" despite price trading above it. These gaps mean neither a clearly bullish nor bearish verdict is well-supported by the data as given.
