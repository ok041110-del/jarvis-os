## Valuation Analysis: Nestlé S.A. (NESN.SW / NSRGY)

**On a forward earnings basis, Nestlé screens as moderately expensive relative to its immediate sector.** The provided forward P/E of 17.62–17.72 sits 23.1% above the cited Consumer Packaged Goods sector median forward P/E of 14.32. Within its direct peer set, however, Nestlé's forward multiple falls in the middle: above Unilever's trailing P/E of 12.93 (itself described as 28% below Unilever's own 10-year median — a real outlier to the downside) but below Danone's trailing P/E of 22.54 (in line with Danone's own historical median). Because the Unilever and Danone figures given are trailing P/E while Nestlé's is forward P/E, this three-way comparison is not on a fully consistent basis, which weakens how much can be concluded from Nestlé's "middle of the pack" position — it may look more or less expensive than peers depending on which multiple convention is applied to each.

**A separate and more troubling data point is the gap within Nestlé's own multiples**: trailing P/E of 28.53 versus forward P/E of 17.62–17.72 implies the market (or estimate providers) expect a substantial increase in forward earnings relative to trailing earnings. The data supplied does not explain this gap — specifically, it does not clarify whether the depressed trailing multiple reflects the previously-noted 31.4% net income decline, and if so, whether that decline is viewed as one-off or structural. Without that context, the forward multiple's apparent cheapness relative to the trailing multiple cannot be assessed as either a genuine re-rating opportunity or an artifact of a temporarily depressed earnings base.

**The DCF evidence in this data set is internally contradictory and should not be relied upon as presented.** One source puts fair value at $113.62 against a stated current price of $77.76 (implying 46.1% upside); another puts fair value at $179.01 against a stated current price of $102 (implying 43% undervaluation). Beyond the fair-value estimates themselves differing by roughly 58%, the two sources do not even agree on what the *current* price is ($77.76 vs. $102) — a gap of roughly 31%. This is large enough that it plausibly reflects an ADR-versus-ordinary-share (NSRGY vs. NESN.SW) mismatch, or the two estimates being pulled at different points in time, or some combination — but the data provided does not state which, and no currency or timestamp information is given to resolve it. As supplied, these two DCF figures cannot be reconciled or averaged into a single "intrinsic value" conclusion.

**Compounding this, market capitalization is reported at both $258.56B and $385B across sources — a roughly 1.5x discrepancy with no stated cause.** Given that market cap discrepancies of this magnitude are consistent with a currency or share-class confusion similar to the DCF issue above, this reinforces rather than resolves the concern that valuation figures in this data set may be mixing CHF-denominated ordinary-share pricing with USD-denominated ADR pricing without adjustment. No FX rate is provided anywhere in the data to test or correct for this.

**Bottom line on what the data supports:** On forward P/E alone, Nestlé appears moderately expensive versus the broader Consumer Packaged Goods sector (+23.1%) and roughly mid-pack versus its two named peers, though that peer comparison mixes trailing and forward multiples. The DCF-based "cheap vs. expensive" question cannot be answered from this data — the two DCF sources disagree not only on fair value but on the current price used as the baseline, a discrepancy large enough (and paired with a similarly unexplained ~1.5x market-cap discrepancy) to suggest an unresolved currency or share-class mismatch rather than a genuine analytical disagreement. I would treat the DCF figures as unusable until that mismatch is identified, and would flag the trailing/forward P/E gap as unexplained rather than assume it signals either cheapness or an earnings-quality problem. Additional information needed to move past these gaps: a stated currency and as-of date for each price/multiple figure, and clarification of what is driving the expected earnings recovery embedded in the forward P/E.
