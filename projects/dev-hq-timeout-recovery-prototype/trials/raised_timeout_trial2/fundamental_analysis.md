## Nestlé S.A. — Fundamental Analysis (based on H1 2026 results, reported 2026-07-23)

**Revenue and top-line growth**

Nestlé reported H1 2026 sales of CHF 43.1 billion, with organic growth of +3.6%, split between real internal growth (RIG) of +1.5% and pricing of +2.1%. This indicates that just under two-thirds of organic growth came from price increases rather than volume/mix, meaning the company is still leaning more heavily on pricing power than on selling more units. Q2-26 alone showed organic growth of +3.7% (RIG +1.8%, price +1.9%), a very similar composition to the half-year figure, suggesting the growth mix has been broadly stable quarter-to-quarter rather than shifting decisively toward volume-led growth.

Growth was notably uneven by geography: emerging markets excluding China grew organically +7.1% (RIG +3.9%), more than triple the RIG rate of developed markets (organic +2.3%, RIG +0.6%). This implies developed-market demand is growing mostly through price/mix rather than volume, while emerging markets (ex-China) are contributing both stronger pricing and materially stronger unit growth. The data explicitly carves out China from the emerging-markets figure without providing China's own number, so no conclusion can be drawn about Nestlé's performance in China specifically — this is a gap in the provided data.

**Earnings and profitability**

The earnings picture is the most striking — and most ambiguous — element of this dataset. Reported (GAAP) net profit fell to CHF 3.5 billion from CHF 5.1 billion a year earlier, a decline of -31.4%. However, underlying (adjusted) net profit was CHF 5.7 billion, down only -2.4% in reported currency and actually up +3.4% on a constant-currency basis. This is a substantial divergence: the GAAP and adjusted metrics move in different directions once currency is stripped out (GAAP sharply down, adjusted underlying figure up on a constant-currency basis). The data provided does not disclose what accounts for the gap between reported and underlying net profit (e.g., impairments, restructuring charges, disposals, or other one-off items), so the cause of the -31.4% GAAP decline cannot be identified from this material. Any assessment of earnings quality is therefore constrained: the underlying trend looks resilient to modestly improving, but the GAAP trend looks materially worse, and reconciling the two would require information not present in this dataset.

**Margins**

The data does not provide an actual H1 2026 UTOP (underlying trading operating profit) margin figure or its year-over-year change — only forward guidance that UTOP margin is expected to improve versus full-year 2025. Without a reported current-period margin number, no margin *trend* can be established from this data; only a directional expectation for the full year has been stated by the company.

**Cash flow and cost program**

Free cash flow improved to CHF 3.4 billion in H1 2026 from CHF 2.3 billion in H1 2025, a year-over-year improvement that stands in contrast to the GAAP net income decline — implying that cash generation held up (or improved) even as reported earnings fell, which is consistent with the possibility of non-cash items depressing GAAP net profit, though this is inference beyond what the data confirms. The "Fuel for Growth" cost-savings program has delivered CHF 1.7 billion cumulatively against a stated 2026 target of CHF 2 billion, indicating the program is close to its target with the data's collection point (2026-08-16) still several months before year-end.

**Guidance**

Management maintained full-year 2026 organic growth guidance of 3–4%, described as a narrowed range, alongside expectations for UTOP margin improvement versus 2025 and free cash flow of CHF 9 billion or more. Guidance being reaffirmed (with a tighter range) alongside an improving underlying-profit and cash-flow narrative suggests management's own framing of the business is more consistent with the adjusted/underlying figures than with the steep GAAP net income decline — but again, the data does not explain the GAAP/underlying gap, so this should be read as management's stated outlook rather than a resolved reconciliation.

**What the data does not allow me to conclude**

Several important gaps limit the strength of any conclusions:
- The specific driver(s) of the 31.4% GAAP net profit decline are not disclosed in this material.
- No actual reported UTOP margin or margin change figure is given — only forward guidance.
- China's individual organic growth contribution is not broken out.
- The data notes unresolved inconsistencies in market cap ($258.56B vs. $385B), DCF fair-value estimates ($113.62 vs. $179.01), and reference share prices ($77.76 vs. $102) across sources, with currency (CHF vs. USD ADR) not reconciled — none of these affect the operating/earnings analysis above directly, but they mean any valuation-adjacent inference should be treated as unverified.
- The relationship between the 86.9% dividend payout ratio and which net income figure (GAAP or underlying) it is calculated against is not specified.

**Summary of what the data implies**

Taken together, the data describes a company with continued mid-single-digit organic growth driven more by pricing than volume, materially stronger volume growth in emerging markets (ex-China) than in developed markets, improving free cash flow, a cost-savings program nearing its annual target, and reaffirmed (tightened) full-year guidance — set against a sharp reported GAAP net profit decline that the underlying/adjusted metrics do not corroborate and that this dataset does not explain. This divergence between GAAP and underlying earnings is the central open question in the data and would need additional disclosure (e.g., the full half-year report's income statement detail) to resolve.
