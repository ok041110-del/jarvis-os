## Nestlé S.A. (NESN.SW / NSRGY) — Fundamental Analysis (H1 2026)

**Revenue and earnings trend**

Nestlé reported H1 2026 sales of CHF 43.1 billion, with organic growth of +3.6%, split between real internal growth (RIG) of +1.5% and a pricing contribution of +2.1%. This indicates that roughly three-fifths of organic growth came from price increases rather than volume/mix, a pattern that continued into the second quarter alone (Q2-26 organic growth +3.7%, RIG +1.8%, pricing +1.9% — a slightly more balanced split than the half-year average). Growth was notably uneven by geography: emerging markets excluding China grew organically +7.1% (RIG +3.9%), more than triple the RIG rate of developed markets, where organic growth was +2.3% (RIG only +0.6%). This suggests developed-market growth is currently driven almost entirely by pricing rather than underlying volume demand, while emerging markets (ex-China) are contributing both stronger pricing and stronger volume.

On earnings, the data shows a sharp divergence between two reported profit measures. Net profit (GAAP, presumably) fell to CHF 3.5 billion from CHF 5.1 billion a year earlier, a decline of -31.4%. However, underlying (adjusted) net profit was CHF 5.7 billion, down only -2.4% year-on-year, and actually up +3.4% on a constant-currency basis. The provided data does not explain what drove the gap between the two measures (e.g., one-off items, impairments, currency effects) — this is an explicit gap in the source material. As a result, the earnings trend cannot be characterized as simply "improving" or "deteriorating"; it depends entirely on which profit measure is used, and the underlying cause of the GAAP decline is unknown from what's provided.

**Margin trend**

The data does not include specific H1 2026 margin figures (e.g., no UTOP margin percentage or gross margin figures are given for the current or prior period). What is provided is forward-looking: guidance that the UTOP (underlying trading operating profit) margin is expected to improve versus full-year 2025. Without a stated current-period margin, this guidance cannot be benchmarked against an actual reported trend within this dataset.

**Guidance and cash flow**

Management maintained full-year 2026 organic growth guidance at 3–4%, described as a "narrowed" range within that band, alongside the UTOP margin improvement guidance noted above and free cash flow guidance of CHF 9 billion or more for the full year. Interim results are consistent with progress toward that FCF target: H1 free cash flow was CHF 3.4 billion, up from CHF 2.3 billion in the prior-year period. The company's "Fuel for Growth" cost-savings program has delivered CHF 1.7 billion cumulatively against a stated 2026 target of CHF 2 billion, implying the program is largely on track to meet its annual goal based on H1 progress alone.

**What this implies, and where the data falls short**

Taken together, the data points to a business generating steady mid-single-digit organic growth that is price-driven more than volume-driven, with meaningfully stronger volume momentum in emerging markets (ex-China) than in developed markets. Cash generation and cost-cutting progress both appear favorable relative to the company's own prior-year and full-year targets. However, the profitability picture is genuinely unclear from this data: the 31.4% GAAP net income decline versus a much smaller underlying decline (or slight underlying constant-currency gain) is an unreconciled and unexplained discrepancy in the source material, and no current-period margin figures are provided to substantiate the forward margin-improvement guidance. Given these gaps — plus separately flagged inconsistencies in market-cap figures ($258.56B vs $385B) and DCF fair-value estimates ($113.62 vs $179.01) across sources, and unresolved CHF/USD currency mixing — this dataset is insufficient to draw firm conclusions about overall earnings quality or valuation, and any such conclusion should be treated as provisional pending reconciliation with primary source filings (e.g., the full Nestlé half-year report).
