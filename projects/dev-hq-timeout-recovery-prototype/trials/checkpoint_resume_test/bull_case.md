# Bull Case: Nestlé S.A. (NESN.SW / NSRGY)

## 1. Underlying business momentum is intact, and the "profit collapse" headline likely overstates the real story

Organic growth of +3.6% in H1 2026 (accelerating slightly to +3.7% in Q2) sits within management's guided 3-4% range for the full year — the company is executing to plan, not missing it. More importantly, the **underlying (adjusted) net profit** — the measure that strips out one-off noise — was down only 2.4% YoY, and was actually **up 3.4% on a constant-currency basis**. That is the cleaner read of core operating performance. The eye-catching -31.4% GAAP net profit decline is very likely an artifact of one-off items (restructuring charges tied to the announced 16,000-role reduction, impairments, or currency effects) rather than a deterioration in the operating business — the fundamental analysis explicitly notes the reported figure and the adjusted figure "point in opposite directions," and the news analysis separately notes the restructuring announcement predates the profit print, making a one-off charge a plausible, data-consistent explanation even though it isn't proven. Free cash flow actually improved to CHF 3.4B in H1 2026 from CHF 2.3B in H1 2025 — real cash generation moving in the right direction, which corroborates the "underlying strength" narrative rather than the "genuine earnings decline" narrative.

## 2. Emerging-market growth engine is firing

Emerging markets ex-China grew organic sales +7.1% (RIG +3.9%) versus +2.3% in developed markets. This is a real, volume-backed (not just price-led) growth pocket that gives Nestlé a structural growth lever most developed-market staples peers can't point to as clearly.

## 3. Management is actively self-correcting, and cost targets are on track

The "Fuel for Growth" program has already delivered CHF 1.7B of a CHF 2B full-year 2026 target — 85% achieved with time still left in the year. Separately, the cost-savings target tied to the 16,000-role restructuring was raised roughly 20% (CHF 3.13B → 3.76B), a sign management sees more efficiency upside than originally scoped. Full-year guidance (3-4% organic growth, FCF ≥ CHF 9B, UTOP margin improvement vs. 2025) was maintained, not cut, despite the leadership upheaval — indicating the new CEO's team has enough visibility into the business to stand behind the prior outlook.

## 4. Portfolio is being sharpened, not just shrunk

The new CEO's move to focus on four core categories (Coffee, Petcare, Nutrition, Food/Snacks) and the divestiture of Blue Bottle Coffee to Centurium Capital both point toward capital being concentrated in Nestlé's strongest categories rather than spread thin. Regardless of the undisclosed sale price, exiting a subscale specialty-retail business to refocus on core B2B/mass channels (Nespresso, Nescafé) is a logical capital-allocation move consistent with a "sharper" post-crisis strategy.

## 5. Dividend track record remains a genuine, uncontested strength

16 consecutive years of dividend increases is a fact confirmed in the data, with the most recent increase (+4.09%) continuing that streak even through the leadership crisis and reported profit volatility — the board did not cut or freeze the dividend in the period covered. A ~3.32% yield on top of that growth streak is a real, data-supported income argument.

## 6. Sell-side consensus, for what it's worth, leans bullish with real upside

Aggregated analyst ratings show 3 Buy / 1 Hold / 1 Sell with a "Buy" consensus and an average target price of $113.68, implying +18.91% upside from the reference ADR price used in that dataset. Even the low end of the analyst range ($97) does not reflect a bearish view among covering institutions — the spread reflects differing conviction on magnitude, not direction (all five ratings are Buy/Hold, none below Hold).

## 7. Valuation is not obviously stretched versus direct peers

While Nestlé's forward P/E (~17.6-17.7x) is above the broader Consumer Packaged Goods sector median (14.32x), it sits squarely between its two closest named peers: above Unilever's TTM P/E of 12.93x but well below Danone's TTM P/E of 22.54x. On a peer-relative basis (acknowledging the trailing/forward mismatch noted in the valuation report), Nestlé is not priced as an outlier — it's mid-pack among the world's largest packaged food/beverage companies, all while holding the #1 revenue position globally (~$89.9-92B vs. Unilever's ~$60B), a scale advantage the smaller peers don't have.

## 8. Technical picture is not bearish on the measures closest to price action

The current price (79.06) sits above the 5-day, 50-day, and 200-day moving averages referenced in the data, and is trading just 0.09% above its stated support level (78.99) — i.e., holding a key technical floor rather than breaking it. RSI at 50.83 is neutral, showing no overbought exhaustion or oversold distress — room to move in either direction without a stretched momentum signal working against the stock. A separately cited forecast in the data points to ~23% upside from a $71 base toward a $87 target, directionally consistent with the analyst target-price upside noted above, even though the technical report itself flags this forecast's methodology as undisclosed.

## Explicit caveats this bull case must acknowledge (per the data provided)

This case cannot respond to every bear point cleanly, and I want to be direct about that rather than paper over it:

- **The GAAP net profit decline (-31.4%) is not actually explained by any report** — I'm arguing it's *likely* a one-off/restructuring effect because the timing and the underlying-profit divergence are consistent with that story, but no report confirms this mechanism. This is an inference, not a documented fact.
- **The dividend payout ratio (86.9%)** is high in absolute terms, and it is unknown whether it's calculated on GAAP or adjusted earnings — if GAAP, coverage could be genuinely tight going forward.
- **Market cap ($258.56B vs. $385B), DCF fair values ($113.62 vs. $179.01), and reference prices ($77.76 vs. $102)** all show large, unreconciled discrepancies in the source data — likely currency/instrument (CHF ordinary vs. USD ADR) mixing, but this isn't confirmed. The bull case above leans on the ratio-based comparisons (P/E vs. peers, technical levels vs. price) precisely because those are more internally consistent than the absolute-dollar figures.
- **The composite technical signal (4 buy vs. 8 sell, moving-average subgroup "Sell")** and the **200-day moving average "sell" label** are genuine countervailing signals this bull case has to concede exist, even while noting price is currently above all three moving averages cited.
- **The same research firm's rating flipped Hold → Buy over 12 months** with no stated reason (methodology or coverage-panel change) — this reduces, but does not eliminate, the reliability of the current "Buy" consensus as a trend signal.

**Bottom line of the bull case:** a global category leader with a still-intact growth and cash-flow story, an active self-correction underway (cost cuts, portfolio focus, leadership reset), a 16-year unbroken dividend growth streak, mid-pack peer valuation, and a majority-Buy analyst consensus with double-digit implied upside — where the single scariest headline number (the GAAP profit decline) is most plausibly a one-off/restructuring effect rather than a sign of deteriorating core economics, based on the adjusted-profit and free-cash-flow figures moving in the opposite direction.
