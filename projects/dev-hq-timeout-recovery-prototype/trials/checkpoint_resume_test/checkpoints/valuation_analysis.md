## Nestlé S.A. (NESN.SW / NSRGY) — Valuation Read, as of the Data Provided (2026-08-16 snapshot)

**On relative multiples, the picture is mixed and partly resolves toward "somewhat expensive," but not cleanly.** Nestlé's Forward P/E of 17.62–17.72 sits 23.1% above the Consumer Packaged Goods sector median Forward P/E of 14.32, which on its face reads as a premium valuation relative to the broader industry. However, against the two named direct peers, Nestlé is not an outlier: Unilever trades at a TTM P/E of 12.93 (itself 28% below its own 10-year median — i.e., Unilever looks cheap even relative to its own history), while Danone trades at a TTM P/E of 22.54 (in line with its own 10-year median). Nestlé's forward multiple falls between these two. The comparison is weakened by a methodological mismatch the data does not address: Unilever and Danone are quoted here on a trailing (TTM) basis while Nestlé's peer-relative figure is forward-looking, so the "middle of the pack" positioning is only indicative, not apples-to-apples.

**A separate, unexplained internal contradiction sits within Nestlé's own multiples.** Trailing P/E of 28.53 versus Forward P/E of 17.62–17.72 is a large gap — the forward multiple is roughly 38% lower than trailing. The data flags that this may relate to a reported -31.4% net income decline but does not confirm the mechanism (e.g., whether trailing EPS is depressed by a one-off or impairment that forward estimates exclude). Without that confirmation, the trailing and forward P/Es are pointing in different directions on the cheap-vs-expensive question and should not be reconciled by assumption.

**The DCF evidence is internally inconsistent to the point of being unusable for a valuation call.** One source implies +46.1% upside (fair value $113.62 vs. a stated current price of $77.76); another implies the stock is 43% undervalued (fair value $179.01 vs. a stated current price of $102). These are not just different fair-value estimates — the two sources also disagree on what the *current* price is ($77.76 vs. $102), a roughly 31% discrepancy in the reference point itself. The data explicitly does not explain whether this stems from ADR-vs-ordinary-share (NSRGY vs NESN.SW) mixing, currency (USD vs CHF) mixing, or differing collection dates. Because the "cheap" and "undervalued" conclusions from these two DCFs rest on two different, unreconciled current-price bases, neither can be treated as a reliable independent confirmation of the other, and averaging or picking one would not be supported by the data as given.

**Currency and scale inconsistencies compound the problem.** The dataset mixes CHF-denominated ordinary-share pricing with USD-denominated ADR pricing without providing an FX rate, and separately reports market capitalization figures of $258.56B and $385B (a ~1.5x gap) with no stated cause. Any of the price-based comparisons above (multiples, DCF upside/downside, or a check between the two) could be materially affected by this unresolved currency/instrument mixing, and the data does not supply enough information to correct for it.

**Other data points cannot be interpreted with what's given.** An 86.9% dividend payout ratio is reported alongside a 31.4% net income decline, but whether the payout ratio is calculated on a GAAP or adjusted earnings basis — which would materially change how sustainable that payout looks — is not specified.

**Overall assessment:** On the single cleanest comparison available — Forward P/E vs. sector median — Nestlé screens as moderately expensive (+23.1%). Against its two closest named peers on a forward basis, it looks roughly mid-range rather than expensive. The trailing P/E and the two DCF-based fair-value estimates cannot currently be used to support or contradict that reading, because they are either internally inconsistent (trailing vs. forward P/E gap unexplained) or mutually contradictory at the level of basic inputs (the two DCFs disagree on both fair value and the current price used to compute upside). Given the unresolved currency mixing, the market-cap discrepancy, and the conflicting DCF base prices, **the data as provided is insufficient to support a clean cheap-or-expensive judgment beyond the sector-relative Forward P/E observation**, and any conclusion drawn from the DCF or trailing-P/E figures would not be well-supported by this dataset alone.
