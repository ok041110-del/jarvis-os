## Nestlé S.A. (NESN.SW / NSRGY) — Fundamental Analysis (H1 2026, reported 2026-07-23)

**Revenue and growth trend**

H1 2026 sales reached CHF 43.1B, with organic growth of +3.6%, composed of real internal growth (RIG) of +1.5% and pricing of +2.1%. This means roughly three-fifths of top-line growth in the period came from price rather than volume/mix — a pattern that continued into Q2 2026 in isolation, where organic growth ticked up slightly to +3.7% (RIG +1.8%, pricing +1.9%), showing a modest shift toward more volume-driven growth in the most recent quarter versus the first-half average.

Growth is geographically uneven: emerging markets excluding China grew organically +7.1% (RIG +3.9%), more than triple the developed-market pace of +2.3% organic growth (RIG +0.6%). Developed markets are therefore growing almost entirely through pricing rather than volume, while emerging markets (ex-China) are delivering both stronger pricing power and materially higher volume growth. The data provided does not include a China-specific figure, so no conclusion can be drawn about that market's contribution or drag.

**Earnings trend**

This is the area with the most significant data conflict in the source material. Reported (GAAP) net profit was CHF 3.5B, down -31.4% from CHF 5.1B in the prior-year period — a large decline. However, underlying (adjusted) net profit was CHF 5.7B, down only -2.4% on a reported basis and actually up +3.4% at constant currency. In other words, the GAAP and adjusted metrics point in opposite directions in terms of magnitude of change, and the underlying trend (modest decline or even growth at constant currency) is far less negative than the headline GAAP figure suggests. The provided data explicitly does not identify what drove the gap between GAAP and underlying net income (e.g., impairments, restructuring charges, disposals, or other one-off items) — this is a material gap that limits how far one can interpret the earnings decline. It should be treated as an unexplained item rather than assumed to reflect operating deterioration.

**Margin trend**

The specific H1 2026 UTOP (underlying trading operating profit) margin figure is not given in the data. What is provided is forward-looking: management guidance calls for UTOP margin improvement in 2026 versus 2025. Without the actual H1 margin number or the 2025 comparative baseline, it is not possible to assess whether this guided improvement is already tracking on plan or represents a second-half inflection.

**Guidance**

For full-year 2026, Nestlé maintained organic growth guidance of 3–4%, with the source noting the range was "narrowed" (implying increased confidence within that band, though the prior/wider range is not specified in the data). Management also guided to UTOP margin improvement versus 2025 and free cash flow (FCF) of CHF 9B or more for the year.

**Cash flow and cost program**

H1 FCF was CHF 3.4B, up from CHF 2.3B in the prior-year period — a clear improvement and one of the more unambiguous positive data points in this set. The "Fuel for Growth" cost-savings program has delivered CHF 1.7B cumulatively against a 2026 target of CHF 2B, suggesting the program is close to its annual target with roughly CHF 0.3B still to be realized, though the data doesn't specify over what remaining period that gap is expected to close.

**What this implies, and where the data falls short**

Taken together, the data describes a company with continued but price-led top-line growth, an emerging-market growth advantage over developed markets, improving free cash flow, and management reiterating (with a narrowed range) full-year growth and margin guidance. However, the headline net income figure fell sharply while the underlying/adjusted figure held roughly flat to slightly positive — and the source material does not explain this divergence. Any interpretation of "earnings trend" therefore has to acknowledge this as an open question rather than a resolved fact: readers relying on the GAAP figure alone would see a business in steep profit decline, while readers relying on the adjusted figure would see a business with roughly stable underlying profitability. The provided data does not contain enough detail (e.g., a breakdown of one-off/non-underlying items) to reconcile these two readings, and I have not filled that gap. Additionally, no H1 2026 or 2025 UTOP margin figures are given, so the guided margin improvement cannot be checked against actual first-half progress from this data alone.
