## Nestlé S.A. (NESN.SW / NSRGY) — Fundamental Analysis: H1 2026

**Revenue and earnings trend**

Nestlé reported H1 2026 sales of CHF 43.1B with organic growth of +3.6%, composed of +1.5% real internal growth (volume/mix) and +2.1% pricing. This split shows growth is being driven more by price than by volume — pricing contributed roughly 58% of the organic growth figure. Q2 2026 alone showed organic growth accelerating slightly to +3.7%, with RIG improving to +1.8% (a healthier volume contribution) while pricing moderated to +1.9%. This sequential shift — rising volume, easing price contribution — could indicate improving underlying demand, though only two data points (H1 and Q2) are given, which is not enough to confirm a durable trend.

Growth was geographically uneven: emerging markets excluding China grew organically +7.1% (RIG +3.9%), more than triple the developed-markets rate of +2.3% organic growth (RIG +0.6%). Developed markets are therefore growing almost entirely on price, with minimal volume growth. Notably, the data explicitly excludes China from the emerging-markets figure and gives no standalone China number, so no conclusion can be drawn about that market specifically.

On the bottom line, the picture is inconsistent depending on which metric is used. Net profit (presumably IFRS/GAAP, though the label "net profit" is not explicitly qualified as such in the data) fell sharply to CHF 3.5B from CHF 5.1B a year earlier, a -31.4% decline. But underlying (adjusted) net profit was CHF 5.7B, down only -2.4% in reported terms and actually up +3.4% at constant currency. The direction of these two metrics diverges — one shows a steep decline, the other shows near-stability or mild growth — and **the data provided does not explain what caused the gap** (e.g., one-off items, impairments, restructuring charges, disposals). This is a material gap: without knowing the source of the ~CHF 1.6B difference between reported and underlying net profit, it is not possible to assess earnings quality or whether the GAAP decline reflects a genuine deterioration in the business versus a non-recurring accounting item.

**Margin trend**

The data does not provide a specific reported UTOP (underlying trading operating profit) margin figure for H1 2026 or the prior-year comparison — only forward guidance that full-year UTOP margin is expected to improve versus 2025. Without an actual H1 margin data point, it's not possible to independently verify whether margin is already trending upward or whether improvement is purely a management projection for the second half. The cost-savings program (Fuel for Growth) has delivered CHF 1.7B cumulative savings against a 2026 target of CHF 2B, which is a plausible mechanical support for margin improvement, but the data doesn't connect these savings quantitatively to a specific margin delta.

**Cash flow**

Free cash flow improved to CHF 3.4B in H1 2026 from CHF 2.3B in H1 2025, a notable increase (~48%). Full-year FCF guidance is for more than CHF 9B. Since H1 delivered CHF 3.4B, the full-year target implies H2 FCF generation of roughly CHF 5.6B or more — consistent with Nestlé's typical seasonal skew toward second-half cash generation, though the data doesn't confirm that seasonal pattern explicitly.

**Guidance and its implications**

Management maintained but narrowed full-year organic growth guidance to 3–4%, which is consistent with the H1 actual of 3.6% — i.e., guidance implies little acceleration or deceleration expected in H2 versus H1. Combined with the UTOP margin improvement and >CHF 9B FCF guidance, management's public framing points to operational stability and improving efficiency. However, this guidance is stated in terms of organic growth and underlying profitability — not in terms of the reported/GAAP net profit that fell -31.4%. The data gives no guidance on reported net profit for the full year, so it's unclear whether management expects the GAAP decline to reverse, persist, or be a one-time H1 event.

**Explicit data limitations affecting this analysis**

- The cause of the -31.4% GAAP net profit decline is not stated anywhere in the provided data; this is the single largest unresolved item for interpreting earnings trend.
- No absolute UTOP margin figures (H1 2026 or H1 2025) are provided, only directional full-year guidance — margin "trend" here is guidance, not an observed data point.
- Currency basis is mixed across the broader dataset (CHF for operating results vs. USD for some market/valuation figures elsewhere), and the earnings figures here are in CHF without a stated FX rate to compare against USD-denominated figures — no cross-currency comparison should be inferred from this section alone.
- No standalone China performance figure is given, despite China being explicitly excluded from the emerging-markets growth number.
- No segment-level (product category) breakdown is provided, so it's not possible to say which parts of the business are driving the RIG/pricing mix.

Given these gaps — particularly the unexplained divergence between GAAP and underlying net profit — this data supports a description of top-line and cash-flow trends but is insufficient to characterize overall earnings quality or margin trajectory with confidence.
