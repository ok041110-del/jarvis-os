## Nestlé S.A. (NSRGY / NESN.SW) — Dividend Quality Analysis

**Growth track record.** The data indicates 16 consecutive years of dividend increases, with the most recent increase at +4.09% year-over-year, bringing the annualized dividend to $3.29 per ADR share (implied yield 3.32%). A 16-year streak of increases is a meaningful indicator of management's commitment to progressive dividend policy, and the latest increase is in the low-to-mid single digits — consistent with a mature, slow-growth capital return rather than an aggressive one. Beyond the single most recent growth figure and the length of the streak, however, the data provided does not include a multi-year series of dividend amounts or growth rates, so I cannot characterize the trend's consistency (e.g., whether increases have been accelerating, decelerating, or steady) beyond this one data point.

**Coverage.** The reported payout ratio is approximately 86.9%, which the data explicitly flags as high relative to a peer comparison (JNJ at 46.19%). This is the single most important caution in the dataset: a payout ratio near 87% leaves comparatively little earnings buffer, and payout ratios in that range are generally considered to signal reduced flexibility for continued increases if earnings falter. The data explicitly states that the accounting basis for this 86.9% figure — whether it is calculated on a GAAP or adjusted/underlying earnings basis — is not specified in the source material, which materially limits how much can be concluded from the number alone (GAAP and adjusted payout ratios can differ substantially for a company with one-off items). Compounding this, the data references a separate fact — a -31.4% decline in net income — without connecting it analytically to the payout ratio calculation or to the period the 86.9% figure covers. I cannot determine whether the 86.9% ratio already reflects that earnings decline, predates it, or is unrelated to it, and I am not able to bridge that gap with the information given.

Notably, although the task framing references free-cash-flow coverage, **no free-cash-flow figures, FCF payout ratio, or cash-flow-based coverage metric are present in the data provided.** This means I cannot assess whether operating or free cash flow supports the current dividend independent of the reported (and ambiguous) earnings-based payout ratio — this is a data gap, not a judgment I can infer from what's given.

**Structural/non-U.S. market characteristics.** The dividend is paid annually rather than quarterly, which is standard for Swiss issuers but differs structurally from U.S. peers cited in the data (JNJ, KO, PG), and it means dividend cash flow to shareholders is lumpier and any single annual increase carries more weight in year-over-year comparisons than a quarterly cadence would. The data also notes that Swiss dividends are subject to a 35% withholding tax at source for non-resident shareholders, reducible to a 15% treaty rate for U.S. residents only through a non-automatic reclaim process via the ADR depositary and Swiss tax authorities — the data does not specify how automatic or lengthy this process is. This affects the *net* yield realized by U.S. holders relative to the headline yield, though it does not itself bear on the company's ability to sustain or grow the gross dividend. Separately, the data notes that Swiss-listed company dividends are generally subject to AGM shareholder approval as a market practice, but no Nestlé-specific detail on this process is provided, so this can only be noted as a general structural feature rather than analyzed for this company.

However, the coverage picture is materially incomplete and internally inconsistent: the payout ratio's accounting basis is unstated, its relationship to a reported -31.4% net income decline is unexplained in the source data, and no free-cash-flow coverage metric is provided at all. Additionally, the broader dataset carries flagged inconsistencies (market cap figures differing by roughly 1.5x between sources, DCF fair-value estimates differing by roughly 1.5-1.6x, and unreconciled CHF/USD currency mixing) that were not resolved by the source material itself.
