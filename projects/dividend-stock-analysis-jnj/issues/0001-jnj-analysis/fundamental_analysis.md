## Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) — Fundamental Analysis: Q2 2026

**Revenue and earnings trend**

JNJ reported Q2 2026 revenue of $25.3B, up 6.6% year-over-year, with operational growth of 5.6% and adjusted operational growth of 5.7% — all ahead of the $25.05B consensus estimate. On the bottom line, GAAP EPS came in at $2.27 while adjusted EPS was $2.90, also beating the $2.85 consensus. The gap between GAAP EPS ($2.27) and adjusted EPS ($2.90) is notable — a roughly 22% difference — implying meaningful one-time or non-operating items were excluded from the adjusted figure, though the data provided does not specify what those items are (e.g., legal charges, restructuring, amortization).

Growth was led by the Innovative Medicine (pharmaceutical) segment, which posted $16.38B in revenue, up 7.8% year-over-year and above the $16.1B estimate — the company's primary growth engine this quarter. MedTech contributed $8.93B, up a more modest 4.5%. This divergence suggests the pharmaceutical business is currently outpacing medical devices as a growth driver, though only one quarter of segment data is provided, which is insufficient to establish a durable trend.

**Margin trend**

The data here is thin and somewhat contradictory. Revenue and adjusted EPS both beat expectations, which on its face suggests operating performance was solid. However, a separately noted headline states that "Q2 net margin is under pressure," implying GAAP profitability concerns even as adjusted metrics looked healthy. The provided data does not specify the cause of this margin pressure (e.g., litigation reserves, one-time charges, cost of goods sold, tax items) or give actual margin percentages (gross, operating, or net margin) for the quarter or comparable prior periods. Without margin figures or a stated cause, I cannot characterize the direction or magnitude of the margin trend with confidence — this is a gap in the data as provided, not a conclusion I can fill in.

**Guidance**

JNJ raised full-year 2026 guidance: revenue guidance midpoint moved to $101.1B (+7.3%), and adjusted EPS guidance was raised by $0.13 to $11.68 (+8.2%). An upward revision to both revenue and adjusted EPS guidance, following a quarter that beat on both metrics, is consistent with management expressing confidence in continued operational momentum for the remainder of the year. That said, the data does not indicate what assumptions underlie the raised guidance (e.g., pipeline contributions, MedTech recovery, macro factors) or how the previously reported $5.5B talc litigation settlement factors into these projections — the data explicitly notes this linkage is not specified in the source material.

**What this implies, and what remains unresolved**

Taken together, the data points to a quarter where top-line and adjusted profitability metrics outperformed expectations, led by the pharmaceutical segment, and management responded by raising full-year revenue and adjusted EPS guidance. This is consistent with a narrative of operational strength on an adjusted basis.

However, several elements prevent a fuller picture:
- The GAAP-vs-adjusted EPS gap and the reported "net margin pressure" headline are not reconciled by any figures or explanations in the provided data — it's unclear whether this reflects a one-off item or a more structural cost issue.
- No actual margin percentages (gross, operating, or net) are given for this quarter or prior periods, so a margin *trend* cannot be established — only that a margin-pressure headline exists alongside beat-and-raise topline/EPS results.
- The effect of the $5.5B talc settlement on guidance or margins is explicitly absent from the data.

Given these gaps, I'd characterize the data as showing positive momentum on revenue and adjusted earnings with raised guidance, but with an unresolved and unquantified margin/GAAP profitability question that the provided data flags but does not explain. Any conclusion about overall margin health would require the underlying margin percentages and clarification of what's driving the GAAP/adjusted EPS divergence — neither of which is present here.
