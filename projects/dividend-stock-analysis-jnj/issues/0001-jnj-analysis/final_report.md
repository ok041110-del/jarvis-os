# Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) — Final Research Report

**Data basis:** This report is compiled entirely from seven pre-collected web-search analyses (Fundamental, Dividend Quality, Valuation, Technical, Industry, News/Event, Sentiment), snapshotted around 2026-08-13, with underlying data points ranging from Q2 2026 results through an 2026-08-07 price close. No live data was accessed and no figures beyond what appears in those source materials are used below. Where the source material is internally inconsistent or incomplete, that is stated explicitly rather than resolved by assumption.

---

## 1. Fundamental

JNJ reported Q2 2026 revenue of **$25.3B**, up 6.6% YoY (5.6% operational, 5.7% adjusted operational), ahead of the $25.05B consensus. GAAP EPS was **$2.27**; adjusted EPS was **$2.90**, beating the $2.85 consensus. The ~22% gap between GAAP and adjusted EPS is unexplained in the data — no breakdown of the excluded items (e.g., legal charges, restructuring, amortization) is provided.

Growth was led by **Innovative Medicine** ($16.38B, +7.8% YoY, above estimate), with **MedTech** contributing $8.93B (+4.5% YoY) — a single quarter of segment data, insufficient to call a durable trend.

A separate headline states "Q2 net margin is under pressure," but no actual margin percentages (gross/operating/net) are given for this or prior periods, and no cause is specified. This sits uneasily next to the top- and bottom-line beats and cannot be reconciled from the data provided.

Management **raised FY2026 guidance**: revenue midpoint to $101.1B (+7.3%), adjusted EPS to $11.68 (+8.2%, a $0.13 raise). The data does not specify what assumptions underlie the raise, nor how the $5.5B talc settlement (see News/Event) factors into it.

**Net read:** A beat-and-raise quarter on revenue and adjusted EPS, pharma-led, but with an unresolved and unquantified margin/GAAP-earnings-quality question flagged by the source data itself.

## 2. Dividend Quality

JNJ extended its streak to **64 consecutive years** of dividend increases (Dividend King status), most recently raising the quarterly dividend 3% to an annualized **$5.36/share** (next ex-dividend date 2026-08-25). No comparative data is given on whether 3% is faster or slower than JNJ's historical increase cadence.

The **payout ratio** reportedly fell sharply from **84% (year-ago) to 46.19%**, described as reflecting "stronger cash generation." However, the data does not specify whether this is computed on GAAP or adjusted EPS, nor what drove the swing (e.g., a prior-year one-off distorting the comparison) — this is an unexplained gap, not a confirmed structural improvement.

Management is reported to tie dividend increases to a **~$21.00B FCF outlook**, a positive framing (cash-based rather than accounting-earnings-based policy), but share count and total annual dividend outlay are not given, so this FCF-coverage claim **cannot be independently verified** from the data — it is a cited management statement, not a computed figure.

Dividend yield is reported inconsistently: **2.06% vs. 2.49%** across sources, with no stated reason for the discrepancy.

**Net read:** A genuinely exceptional long-term track record and directionally positive coverage/FCF signals, but the magnitude of the payout-ratio improvement and the FCF-coverage claim are not verifiable from the data as given.

## 3. Valuation

The data cannot support a confident over/undervaluation call. **P/E (TTM) is reported at three different values**: 27.61x, 29.7x, and 16.9x, with no explanation for the discrepancy (possibly GAAP vs. non-GAAP EPS, or differing calculation dates — unconfirmed). The 16.9x figure is the basis for a claim that JNJ is undervalued vs. a 21.2x industry average; the other two suggest the opposite.

Industry/peer comparisons are also **internally contradictory**: one source says JNJ trades above the pharma industry average P/E (~17.1x, i.e., expensive); another says JNJ trades below a peer-group average of 47.2x (i.e., cheap). These likely reference different comparison sets, but the data doesn't confirm this. Named-competitor comparisons (cheaper than Eli Lilly/Merck, pricier than Novartis/Abbott) are qualitative only, with no multiples given.

Most notably, the source data explicitly frames a **"tug of war"** between DCF-based intrinsic value (suggesting undervaluation) and earnings-multiple methods (suggesting overvaluation) — a structural, not incidental, disagreement between valuation approaches.

**Net read:** Three layers of internal inconsistency (P/E figures, industry benchmark definitions, DCF vs. multiples) make a single valuation verdict unsupportable from this data set. Resolving it would require checking calculation bases against primary filings.

## 4. Technical

As of the 2026-08-07 close of **$259.24**, the stock traded above its EMA20, EMA50, and EMA200 — a textbook bullish trend structure — reinforced by a separate moving-average summary of 12 buy / 0 sell signals ("Strong Buy"). However, that same source's actual MA price levels (5-day $240.24, 50-day $234.98, 200-day $229.10) sit **$19–30 below** the $259.24 close — an unusually large, unexplained gap that is flagged as a data-quality issue, not resolved.

Momentum signals conflict: **RSI(14) is reported as both 64.566** (buy signal) **and 55.45** (mildly positive, not strong) by different sources. Separately, the **daily MACD histogram is negative even as price rises** — a possible early bearish momentum divergence against the bullish trend picture.

No support/resistance levels, chart patterns, or volume data are provided.

**Net read:** Trend-following signals read bullish; momentum signals are mixed-to-diverging; the underlying moving-average data itself is internally inconsistent with the quoted close. This should be treated as a data-quality caveat, not a resolved market signal.

## 5. Industry

JNJ is described as the **second-largest company by market cap in global biopharma** (>$560B) and is projected to **cross $100B in annual revenue for the first time in 2026** (from ~$94B in 2025). No breakdown of organic vs. acquisition-driven growth, or segment-level revenue/margin splits, is provided.

The dual **Pharmaceutical/MedTech structure** is cited qualitatively as risk-diversifying, though its actual internal weighting cannot be assessed from the data.

**Pharma competitors** named: Eli Lilly, Novartis, Roche, Amgen (broad immunology/oncology), Bristol Myers Squibb (oncology/immunology-focused). **AbbVie is specifically called a "strong competitor" in immunology** — the one instance of a competitor named as strong against JNJ specifically. In oncology, JNJ is described only as "pursuing a leading position" — vague, unsubstantiated positioning language per the source itself.

**MedTech competitors** named: Abbott, Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Stryker, Roche — with no sub-segment, share, or trend detail.

Cited strengths (diversified portfolio, R&D scale, global footprint) are qualitative only — no R&D spend, R&D/revenue ratio, or geographic mix figures are given.

**Net read:** Data supports a general picture of a large, diversified competitor facing real pressure in immunology (AbbVie) and unproven positioning in oncology, but is insufficient to assess actual market share or competitive trajectory in any specific franchise.

## 6. News/Event (Talc Litigation)

The dominant development is a reported **~$5.5B settlement proposal (July 2026)** aimed at resolving ~70,000 pending talc lawsuits in the federal MDL, with **$3B scheduled to begin payout in 2027**. Plaintiff counts diverge across sources (68,914 vs. "over 63,000"), itself a sign of inconsistent tracking.

A structurally significant fact: JNJ's **third attempt to resolve talc claims via subsidiary bankruptcy was rejected by the court, and the company is not appealing** — a shift away from the bankruptcy-channel strategy (pursued since 2021) toward settlement plus continued case-by-case tort litigation.

Individual tail risk remains live: an Illinois appellate court **affirmed a $45M award** to a mesothelioma victim's family — illustrative of per-case severity, not aggregate exposure.

**Critically, the data does not show how the $5.5B settlement (or the $3B 2027 tranche) is reflected in reserves, guidance, or cash flow.** Sourcing is primarily legal/plaintiff-adjacent sites (motleyrice.com, drugwatch.com, torhoermanlaw.com, lawsuit-information-center.com) rather than primary financial filings, and no SEC filings were cross-checked.

**Net read:** A large share of pending claims may be moving toward a bounded, quantified resolution, but the bankruptcy-path rejection reopens exposure to individual verdicts, and the settlement's financial/accounting impact on JNJ cannot be assessed from this data.

## 7. Sentiment

Across **32 institutions**, the composite rating is "Buy" (7.7/10): **13 Buy, 11 Hold, 1 Sell**. With Hold nearly as large a bloc as Buy, this is more accurately described as a "mild positive bias" than a strong buy consensus.

Analyst **target price range is $155.00–$240.00** — an $85 spread (~55% between low and high), itself indicating wide disagreement. Representative values also disagree by source: **median $210.02** (32 institutions) vs. **mean $238.2** (20 institutions), a ~$28 gap.

Most notably: the **2026-08-07 close ($259.24) exceeds every disclosed target figure**, including the top of the full range ($240.00), the median, and the mean. One source simultaneously claims "+6.05% upside vs. average target" — a figure that is **mathematically inconsistent** with the $238.2 average reported alongside it. The data does not explain this contradiction (possibly a timing mismatch between when targets were set and the current price).

**Net read:** Sentiment is directionally positive but internally weak — wide target dispersion, conflicting representative values, and a price that has already surpassed every disclosed target undermine confidence in reading this as a strong bullish signal.

## 8. Bull Case

1. **Clean beat-and-raise quarter**: Revenue and adjusted EPS both beat, led by pharma; guidance raised on both lines — a credibility-backed signal of durable momentum.
2. **Dividend King with improved coverage**: 64 years of increases; payout ratio improved 84%→46.19%; dividend increases explicitly tied to a ~$21B FCF outlook.
3. **DCF signals undervaluation**, and JNJ's P/E screens cheap against some benchmarks (16.9x vs. 21.2x industry average; below peer-group average of 47.2x and named competitors Lilly/Merck).
4. **Unambiguously bullish technical trend**: price above EMA20/50/200, 12 buy/0 sell MA signals, one RSI reading (64.566) in bullish-but-not-overbought territory.
5. **Scale and diversification**: second-largest global biopharma by market cap, first-ever $100B revenue year projected, dual-segment structure spreading competitive risk.
6. **Talc overhang moving toward resolution**: a bounded $5.5B settlement with defined timing replaces open-ended litigation uncertainty; the abandoned bankruptcy strategy can be read as pivoting toward an actually-converging resolution.
7. **Net-positive sell-side sentiment**: only 1 of 25 rated institutions is a Sell.

**Bull-side caveats acknowledged:** the P/E and target-price data used above are contested by other figures in the same set; the current price already exceeds even the highest disclosed analyst target; the GAAP/adjusted EPS gap and margin-pressure headline are unexplained; technical momentum is not unanimous (second RSI reading, negative MACD histogram); the settlement's accounting treatment is undisclosed.

## 9. Bear Case

1. **Price is already above the entire disclosed analyst target range** ($259.24 vs. a $155.00–$240.00 range, $210.02 median, $238.2 mean) — the single most quantitatively solid bearish data point in the set. Ratings themselves show Hold (11) nearly matching Buy (13).
2. **Margin pressure is flagged but never explained**, alongside an unexplained ~22% GAAP/adjusted EPS gap; the raised guidance rests on the adjusted metric, not the one where pressure was flagged.
3. **Valuation signals are contradictory, but the contradiction cuts against paying up**: the more mutually consistent, higher P/E readings (27.61x, 29.7x) sit well above the 17.1x sector average cited elsewhere; the cheap 16.9x reading is an uncorroborated outlier.
4. **Litigation risk has structurally shifted, not resolved**: rejection of the bankruptcy-channel strategy (three attempts since 2021) pushes exposure back toward less predictable individual litigation, evidenced by the affirmed $45M Illinois verdict; the $5.5B settlement has no disclosed link to reserves/guidance/cash flow; plaintiff counts are inconsistent across sources.
5. **Competitive positioning may be softer than "diversified giant" framing suggests**: AbbVie explicitly called a "strong competitor" in immunology; JNJ only "pursuing" oncology leadership (no share data); MedTech (a third of Q2 revenue) grew at little more than half Pharma's rate in the one quarter reported.
6. **Technical momentum is diverging from price**, and the MA dataset itself is internally broken (unexplained $19–30 gap vs. close); MACD histogram negative while price rises; RSI readings disagree.
7. **The FCF-coverage/dividend-safety claim is uncorroborated** — share count and total dividend outlay aren't given, so ~$21B FCF vs. total dividends can't be checked; the unexplained payout-ratio swing raises the possibility of a distorted year-over-year comparison.

**Bear-side caveats acknowledged:** margin deterioration cannot be quantified (only a headline exists); the talc/earnings linkage is inferred as risk, not demonstrated as fact; the 16.9x P/E reading is real data, not invented; segment growth divergence is one quarter and not a trend; no competitor share figures exist to size the AbbVie/oncology concerns.

## 10. Synthesis

**Undisputed facts across both cases:** Q2 2026 revenue/EPS beat with raised guidance; the unexplained GAAP/adjusted EPS gap and margin-pressure headline; the dividend raise and unexplained payout-ratio swing; the price sitting above the entire disclosed analyst target range; the 13/11/1 rating split; the conflicting technical/RSI/MA data; the P/E range (16.9x–29.7x) with two different sector benchmarks; the talc settlement terms and the bankruptcy-channel rejection; and MedTech's slower growth vs. Pharma in the one reported quarter.

**Where interpretation diverges on the same facts:** whether the guidance raise reflects durable strength (bull) or is built on the specific metric where margin pressure was flagged (bear); whether the payout-ratio improvement reflects structural strength (bull) or an unexplained, possibly distorted comparison (bear); whether 13 Buy/11 Hold reads as "almost no bears" (bull) or "barely more Buy than Hold" (bear); whether the end of the bankruptcy strategy is a pivot toward resolution (bull) or a loss of the preferred capped-liability mechanism (bear); and whether the diversified pharma/MedTech structure is a strength (bull) or currently unbalanced (bear).

**Where the source data itself is unreconciled, independent of framing:** the three P/E figures and two sector benchmarks; the two technical/MA datasets; the two RSI readings; and the two plaintiff-count figures. These are data-quality gaps in the source material, not a substantive bull/bear disagreement.

**The one clean, undisputed data point that matters most:** the stock's price ($259.24) sits above every analyst target figure disclosed in this data set — the $240.00 ceiling, the $210.02 median, and the $238.2 mean. This is acknowledged, not contested, by both cases.

**Open questions that would most change the picture** (in descending order of impact, per the source data's own flags): (1) what explains the ~22% GAAP/adjusted EPS gap and the margin-pressure headline; (2) whether the $5.5B talc settlement is already reserved or represents an incremental future charge; (3) what caused the payout ratio to fall from 84% to 46.19%; (4) which P/E and sector benchmark is actually correct; (5) why the two technical/RSI datasets disagree so widely; (6) what the actual current plaintiff count and remaining talc exposure are.

**Overall:** the data set supports a **constructive-but-unresolved** picture — a genuinely strong quarter and raised guidance, an exceptional dividend track record, and a talc settlement that may be narrowing overhang — sitting alongside a price that has run above every disclosed valuation anchor, an unexplained earnings-quality/margin question, an unresolved litigation-accounting linkage, and materially inconsistent valuation and technical data. The dataset does not support a confident directional call either way.
