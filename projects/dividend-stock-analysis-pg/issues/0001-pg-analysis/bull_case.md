# BULL CASE: Procter & Gamble (PG)

## 1. A dividend track record that is effectively unmatched in corporate America

PG has paid a dividend continuously for 136 years (since 1890) and raised it for 70 consecutive years (since 1956) — a "Dividend King" pedigree held by only a handful of companies. The 10-year dividend CAGR of 5% shows this isn't a frozen legacy payout; it's grown at a pace that has historically outstripped inflation. The current payout ratio of 63.77% sits in a moderate middle range — comfortably below Coca-Cola's 77–80% — indicating the company has historically retained a meaningful share of earnings beyond the dividend rather than stretching to cover it. For an income-oriented holding, this is one of the most durable capital-return records available in the market, and nothing in the data contradicts the qualitative strength of that 70-year streak.

## 2. Analyst consensus is directionally positive, with real upside embedded

Across multiple independent sources, the aggregated analyst rating is "Buy," and the average 1-year price target implies positive expected upside (+11.67%). This directional read — Buy-leaning sentiment with positive expected return — is the one point in the sentiment data that holds up consistently across sources, even though the specific dollar target varies. Even the low end of the reported target dispersion is only modestly negative (-0.85%), while the high end reaches +27.19%, meaning the balance of professional opinion, taken as a whole, skews toward upside rather than downside.

## 3. Management is proactively defending margins on two fronts

The company isn't sitting still against cost pressure. It is running a two-year restructuring program cutting ~15% (~7,000 people) of non-manufacturing headcount, and execution is ahead of schedule — over 3,500 positions (roughly half the target) were already eliminated after just one year. That's a management team executing decisively on cost structure rather than dragging its feet.

Simultaneously, PG is using its brand pricing power directly: mid-single-digit price increases on 25% of U.S. products began in August 2026, a direct, confirmed (not disputed in any source) mechanism to offset tariff and commodity cost headwinds. A company with the market power to raise prices on a quarter of its U.S. portfolio without the data indicating any negotiated resistance is demonstrating exactly the kind of pricing power bulls look for in a consumer staples leader — and it's paired with a new CEO (Shailesh Jejurikar, installed January 2026) whose tenure now visibly includes both the restructuring execution and the pricing action, suggesting a coherent, active playbook rather than passive drift.

## 4. Category leadership and structurally strong gross margins

PG is reported as the #1 company by market cap (~$357B) in the household products sector, with a 51.2% gross margin — a level indicative of real brand premium and cost-structure advantage over the category. Operating margin, even at the lower of the two reported figures (24%), is described as substantially above Unilever's ~17%, the one competitor for which a direct comparison is given. That gap, even accounting for the source discrepancy (24% vs. 26.3%), is large enough in either case to support a story of structural profitability advantage versus at least one major global peer.

## 5. Full-year results still show growth, and guidance keeps the growth trajectory intact

FY2026 net sales grew 3% (or organic +1%) and full-year GAAP EPS grew 2%, with Core EPS up 1% — modest, but positive, top- and bottom-line growth in a year that also absorbed cost headwinds and restructuring charges. For FY2027, management guided to continued net sales growth of +1% to +3% and Core EPS growth of 0% to 3%, meaning even the guidance floor calls for flat, not declining, full-year earnings — despite explicitly flagging a rough Q1. A company guiding to a growth range (even a modest one) while mid-restructuring and mid-tariff-absorption is guiding through the trough, not away from growth altogether.

## 6. Technical picture is neutral, not deteriorating — a reasonable entry backdrop

The technical setup is not a headwind to the bull case: RSI at 42.4 is neutral (nowhere near overbought, leaving room to run), and the composite technical signal is explicitly Neutral (two buy vs. two sell signals) rather than bearish. Price at $144.49 sat within a defined range between $138 support and $150 resistance — a level bulls can point to as a floor with defined risk, not a stock in technical breakdown.

## 7. Where the case is genuinely inconclusive rather than negative

In fairness to the data: Q4 GAAP EPS fell 15% and Core EPS fell 3% even as sales rose, Forward P/E is reported inconsistently (21.1x vs. 25.4x) making relative valuation hard to pin down, and Price-to-FCF (29.0x vs. Unilever's 16.9x) is the one valuation metric that consistently points to a premium. No FCF-based dividend coverage ratio or DCF intrinsic value exists in the data to confirm cash-flow support for the dividend. These are legitimate open questions the bear case will press — but none of them are confirmed negatives; they are unresolved data gaps, and a bull can reasonably argue that in the absence of contradicting cash-flow evidence, a 70-year raise streak and a moderate earnings-based payout ratio are the best available signal, and both favor sustainability.

## Bottom line

The strongest, data-grounded bull thesis for PG rests on: (1) a nearly unrivaled 136-year/70-year dividend record with moderate payout coverage, (2) consistent Buy-leaning analyst sentiment with positive average expected upside, (3) decisive, ahead-of-schedule management action on both cost structure (restructuring) and pricing power (mid-single-digit increases) to defend margins against cost headwinds, (4) category-leading scale and gross margins with a plausible operating-margin edge over at least one major peer, (5) continued (if modest) top- and bottom-line growth with guidance for further growth in FY2027, and (6) a neutral-not-bearish technical setup offering a defined-range entry. The valuation and Q4 EPS questions are real and should be tracked, but the data provided does not confirm they outweigh this combination of durable capital return, active margin defense, and directionally positive analyst sentiment.
