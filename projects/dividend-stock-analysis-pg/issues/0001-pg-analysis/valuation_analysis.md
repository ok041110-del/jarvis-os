## Valuation Analysis: The Procter & Gamble Company (PG)

**Trailing earnings multiple: internally inconsistent, though the internal spread is modest.** The data provides two different TTM P/E readings for PG — 20.67x as of 2026-07-31 and 22.03x as of 2026-07-15, drawn from different sources. This is a genuine data-quality problem: a stock's trailing P/E should not move by roughly 1.4 turns (6-7%) over a two-week window absent a sharp price move or an earnings restatement, and no such event is described in the data provided. I flag this as an unresolved discrepancy rather than resolving it in either direction. For context, the data notes this gap is smaller than a reported 3-way inconsistency in JNJ's P/E figures (16.9x–29.7x), which suggests these cross-source snapshot mismatches may be a broader pattern in how the underlying sources are sampling or timing their data, not unique to PG.

**Forward P/E comparison to sector produces a contradiction that the data itself cannot resolve.** PG's forward P/E is given as 21.08x, reported to be 44.6% above the Consumer Packaged Goods sector median of 14.58x — on its face, a meaningful premium suggesting PG looks expensive relative to its broader sector. However, the data also contains a second, seemingly incompatible claim: that PG's P/E is *lower* than a peer-group average of 22.95x, implying PG looks cheap relative to its direct peers. These two statements — "expensive vs. sector median" and "cheap vs. peer-group average" — cannot both describe the same relationship in the same direction, and the data does not clarify whether "sector median" and "peer-group average" are constructed from different constituent sets, different time points, or different P/E definitions (trailing vs. forward). This is explicitly flagged in the source material as an unresolved issue, comparable to a similar definitional ambiguity noted for JNJ. I am not able to determine which comparison, if either, is more reliable, and I would treat any conclusion drawn from either sector or peer-group comparison alone as unsupported until this is reconciled.

**Named-competitor comparison is directional only, with no way to verify magnitude.** The data states PG trades at a lower multiple than JNJ and Colgate-Palmolive (CL), and at a higher multiple than Kimberly-Clark (KMB) and Clorox (CLX). No actual multiples for JNJ, CL, KMB, or CLX are provided, so this ordering can be reported but not independently checked or sized — it tells us PG sits in the middle of this five-name group, but not by how much in either direction.

**Two independent signals point toward undervaluation, though both carry source-specific caveats.** GuruFocus's proprietary intrinsic value estimate (GF Value) is $167.26 against a stated current price of $148.01, implying roughly 11.5% undervaluation. This is a single-source, model-derived estimate — GF Value is GuruFocus's own proprietary methodology, not a market-consensus figure, so it should be weighted as one firm's model output rather than a market-validated fact. Separately, the data states that PG's current P/E of 20.67x sits 26% below its own historical average, which is a self-referential (time-series) valuation signal rather than a peer or sector comparison, and is directionally consistent with the "cheap vs. historical norm" and "cheap vs. GF Value" readings — but not with the "44.6% premium to sector median" figure discussed above.

**Net picture.** The data does not support a single, clean verdict of "cheap" or "expensive." On a historical-self-comparison basis and per one proprietary intrinsic-value model, PG screens as moderately undervalued. On a forward-P/E-vs-sector-median basis, it screens as notably overvalued. On a P/E-vs-peer-group-average basis, it screens as modestly undervalued. These are not reconcilable from the data provided, and the source material itself acknowledges the sector-median/peer-group-average distinction is not defined. Given this, along with the unexplained ~$10 discrepancy in moving-average figures between sources (noted in the data's own limitations section) and the two divergent TTM P/E readings, I would characterize the valuation picture presented here as mixed and internally inconsistent rather than as supporting a confident conclusion in either direction. Any judgment beyond "the data conflicts across sources and comparison bases" would require reconciling which sector/peer definitions and which snapshot dates the higher-confidence figures actually rest on — information not present in what was provided.
