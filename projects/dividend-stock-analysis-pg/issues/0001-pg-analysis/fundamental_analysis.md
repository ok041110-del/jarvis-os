## Fundamental Analysis: The Procter & Gamble Company (PG)

**Revenue and earnings trend**

For fiscal year 2026 (ended June 30, 2026), P&G reported net sales of $87.0B, up 3% year-over-year, with organic sales growth of just 1% — a notably slower pace than the headline all-in growth figure, implying that acquisitions, divestitures, or currency effects accounted for a meaningful portion of the reported top-line gain. GAAP diluted EPS for the year came in at $6.62 (+2%), and Core EPS was $6.89 (+1%), both showing modest full-year growth.

However, the fourth quarter tells a different story: Q4 FY2026 net sales grew 2% to $21.2B, but GAAP diluted EPS fell sharply to $1.26, down 15% year-over-year, while Core EPS declined 3% to $1.43. The data explicitly notes that this Q4 GAAP decline and the full-year GAAP gain move in opposite directions, and the source material does not reconcile or explain this divergence — it simply reports both figures side by side. As a result, it is not possible from the data provided to determine whether the Q4 GAAP EPS drop reflects a one-time or non-recurring item, a genuine deterioration in underlying profitability late in the year, or some other factor. This should be treated as an open question rather than resolved one way or the other.

**Margin trend**

The data provided does not include explicit gross margin or operating margin figures for either the full year or Q4. What can be inferred indirectly is that Core EPS growth (+1% for FY2026) trailed net sales growth (+3%), and that in Q4 specifically, Core EPS declined (-3%) even as net sales rose (+2%) — a combination consistent with margin compression in the quarter, though the underlying drivers (cost inputs, mix, pricing, promotional spend) are not specified in the data. The guidance commentary reinforces this margin-pressure narrative by citing an expected ~$1B after-tax headwind from commodities, energy, and transportation costs in FY2027, but no historical margin percentages are given to quantify the trend precisely.

**Guidance and its implications**

For FY2027, P&G has guided to net sales growth (both all-in and organic) of +1% to +3%, and Core EPS growth of 0% to 3% off the FY2026 base of $6.89, implying a Core EPS range of roughly $6.89 to $7.11. The company has flagged approximately $1B in after-tax cost headwinds from commodities, energy, and transportation for the coming fiscal year, and has explicitly stated that Q1 FY2027 EPS could decline by 5% or more year-over-year.

Taken together, this guidance implies a company expecting continued but modest top-line growth alongside real near-term profitability pressure, particularly in the first quarter of the new fiscal year. The low end of the Core EPS guidance range (0% growth) suggests management is allowing for the possibility that cost headwinds fully offset any sales-driven earnings gains over the full year. The data does not specify whether the ~$1B cost headwind and any restructuring/tariff-related costs are already fully reflected in this guidance range or represent a risk on top of it — this is noted in the source material as an unresolved gap.

**Data limitations affecting this analysis**

Several inconsistencies in the underlying data should be flagged explicitly rather than smoothed over:

- Net sales for FY2026 are reported as $87.0B in the fundamentals section but $85.26B elsewhere in the source material, with no stated reconciliation (possibly a fiscal-year basis difference, but this is not confirmed in the data).
- Tariff/cost impact estimates vary between $600M and $1B depending on the source cited.
- No gross margin, operating margin, or segment-level margin data was provided, limiting the ability to characterize the margin trend beyond what can be inferred from the EPS/sales growth gap.
- No free-cash-flow figures or dividend coverage ratios are included, so cash-generation trends and dividend sustainability cannot be assessed from this data.
- No discounted-cash-flow or intrinsic-value estimate is present in the data.
- The data spans reporting dates from late July into mid-August 2026, with as much as an 11-day lag between some figures, and should not be treated as a single point-in-time snapshot.

Given these gaps — particularly the unreconciled net sales discrepancy and the absence of any margin percentage data — this analysis should be read as directionally indicative rather than precise, and any conclusions about margin trajectory in particular carry meaningful uncertainty.
