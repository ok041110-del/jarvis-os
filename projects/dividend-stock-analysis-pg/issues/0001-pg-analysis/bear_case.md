# BEAR CASE: Procter & Gamble (PG)

## 1. Earnings momentum is deteriorating, not accelerating

The headline full-year numbers mask a weakening trend. FY2026 organic sales growth was just **1%**, far below the 3% all-in growth figure — meaning the "real" underlying business is barely growing, and most of the reported top-line gain came from something other than organic demand. More alarming is the trajectory into year-end: Q4 FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS **fell 15% YoY** and Core EPS **fell 3%**, even as sales rose 2%. A company whose earnings decline while sales grow in the same quarter is a company with margin problems, cost problems, or both — and management's own guidance confirms this isn't a one-quarter blip: **Q1 FY2027 EPS is explicitly guided to decline 5% or more YoY**. This is deceleration flagged by the company itself, not a bear's inference.

## 2. Margin compression is coming, and it's not small

FY2027 guidance embeds a **~$1B after-tax headwind** from commodities, energy, and transportation. Combined with the fact that Core EPS growth (+1%) already trailed sales growth (+3%) in FY2026, and Core EPS outright *declined* in Q4 despite sales growth, the pattern is consistent: costs are rising faster than the company can pass through or absorb. Guidance for FY2027 Core EPS growth of **0%–3%** — with the low end at literally flat — tells the market that management itself sees a real scenario where a full year of cost headwinds completely erases any earnings benefit from sales growth.

## 3. The one clean valuation signal available says PG is expensive

Amid the cross-source noise in P/E multiples, **Price-to-FCF is the one metric where the analyses agree**: PG trades at **29.0x** versus Unilever's **16.9x** — nearly double. That's a premium of the kind normally reserved for a company with superior growth, not one guiding to flat-to-low-single-digit EPS growth and mid-teens organic sales growth. The bull argument for this premium rests entirely on a qualitative claim about superior operating margins (~24–26%) — but no analysis in the data provided ever translates that margin advantage into a *justified* multiple. A premium asserted but never quantified is a premium the bear case is entitled to discount.

## 4. Technicals confirm near-term weakness

As of the most recent data (Aug 3), PG traded at **$144.49, below both its 20-day ($148.06) and 50-day ($147.33) moving averages** — a textbook bearish structural signal. RSI at 42.4 sits below the neutral midpoint of 50, and price sat closer to the middle of its $138–$150 range with no bullish tilt. Even the sentiment data's own **low-end analyst target of $145 implies essentially zero upside (-0.85%)** from the last technical close — meaning at least one covering analyst sees no further room to run at all, directly undercutting the "Buy consensus" headline.

## 5. Dividend coverage cannot currently be verified — and the one number available isn't reassuring

The 70-year increase streak is a real qualitative strength, but current coverage quality is genuinely unclear from this data, and what's visible skews cautious: the **63.77% payout ratio coincides with the same period in which quarterly GAAP EPS fell 15%**, and the data cannot confirm whether that payout ratio is based on trailing annual earnings or the weaker recent quarter. There is **no free-cash-flow coverage ratio anywhere in the data** — the more reliable dividend-safety test — which means the durability of the 5% historical dividend growth rate against a backdrop of guided flat-to-low EPS growth cannot be confirmed. Absence of reassuring data is not proof of a problem, but it removes a pillar the bull case would need.

## 6. Restructuring and tariff costs are real, sizeable, and possibly not fully priced into guidance

P&G is cutting **~15% of non-manufacturing headcount (~7,000 roles)** with pre-tax non-recurring charges of **$1.0B–$1.6B**. Separately, tariff-related pre-tax cost estimates range from **$600M to $1B**. Critically, **the data cannot confirm whether either of these cost items is already reflected in the FY2027 Core EPS guidance range of $6.89–$7.11** — which means there is a real possibility that actual FY2027 earnings come in below even the low end of guidance if these charges land on top of, rather than inside, the guided range. Management's price increases (mid-single-digit hikes on 25% of U.S. products from August 2026) are a direct tax on the consumer to defend margins, and the data provides **no evidence on how volume will respond** — a pure cost pass-through with unknown demand elasticity is a risk, not a solved problem.

## 7. Consensus bullishness is less solid than it appears

The "Buy" rating and average +11.67% upside are real data points, but the dispersion beneath them is wide enough to question their reliability: average target prices range from **$163.3 to $178.63 — a >9% spread while citing the identical 25-institution sample**, and the full target range spans **-0.85% to +27.19%**. That kind of disagreement among analysts looking at the same company suggests the "consensus" is fragile and built on inconsistent underlying assumptions (the same Forward P/E and cost-headwind figures that are already unreconciled elsewhere in the data).

---

## Bear thesis, summarized

Even setting aside every cross-source inconsistency the data flags, the signals that *are* internally coherent all point the same direction: **Q4 earnings already declined while sales grew; guidance's own low end is flat EPS growth; the cleanest valuation metric (P/FCF) shows a near-2x premium to a key peer; the stock trades below its short-term moving averages; a payout ratio is anchored to a period of falling EPS with no FCF coverage check available; and $1.6–2.6B combined in restructuring and tariff costs may not be fully absorbed in forward guidance.** This is a mature, single-digit-growth consumer staple carrying a premium multiple while showing genuine near-term earnings deterioration and cost pressure — a combination that, at minimum, does not support the premium valuation being paid, and at worst sets up guidance risk to the downside in FY2027.

**What would break this thesis:** confirmation that the $1B+ in tariff/restructuring costs are already embedded in guidance (removing the downside surprise risk), a reconciled Forward P/E showing PG closer to peer average (21.1x) rather than at a premium (25.4x), and FCF-based dividend coverage data showing comfortable headroom. None of these are available in the data provided, and that absence itself is a reason for caution, not comfort.
