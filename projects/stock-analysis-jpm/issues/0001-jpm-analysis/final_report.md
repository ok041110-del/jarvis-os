# JPMorgan Chase & Co. (JPM) — Research Report

**Note on scope and method:** This report is compiled entirely from data explicitly supplied by prior research sections (fundamental, technical, industry, news/event, and sentiment analyses), described as web-search summaries collected around 2026-08-10 to 2026-08-13. It is a single-quarter, single-day-snapshot dataset, not verified against primary filings (10-Q, earnings call transcript) and not live market data. No figures, dates, or facts beyond what was provided have been added.

## 1. Fundamental

Q2 2026 revenue was $57.3B (+27.7% YoY), beating consensus ($50.61B) by 13.3%; on a managed basis, revenue was $58.0B (+27% YoY), with record revenue reported across every business segment and Equities standing out at +86% YoY. Net income reached $21.2B, a quarterly record, with EPS of $7.70 beating consensus ($5.55) by 38.7%.

However, roughly $5.6B of that net income (a $4.6B Visa-related one-time gain plus $1.0B in other equity investment gains) was non-recurring. Adjusted net income was $16.9B (adjusted EPS $6.14) — about 20% below the headline figure — though this adjusted figure still beat consensus on both revenue and earnings.

No efficiency ratio, net margin time series, or prior-quarter/prior-year ROTCE was provided — only a single-quarter adjusted ROTCE of 23%, with nothing to compare it against. Full-year NII guidance was raised to $105.5B, but expense guidance was simultaneously raised to $107.5B, attributed only vaguely to "increased activity." The data does not permit a determination of whether this represents improving operating leverage or costs simply scaling with revenue. The quarterly dividend was raised to $1.65/share, though no payout ratio or capital-ratio (e.g., CET1) data was supplied to assess sustainability.

**Data limitation:** This is a single-quarter snapshot with YoY comparisons only — no QoQ or multi-quarter trend data is available, so persistence of this performance cannot be assessed.

## 2. Technical

As of 2026-08-10, JPM closed at $357.52, above its 20-day SMA ($349.40) and 50-day SMA ($333.00) — a structure consistent with an established uptrend. The 20-day SMA rose 1.51% over the trailing five days. Price sits well above the 3-month low ($293.67) but below the 3-month high ($363.00).

RSI(14) reads 63.0 — elevated but below the conventional overbought threshold of 70; the source itself describes this as "near the upper end, just short of overbought" and separately flags a "possible pause" in the advance despite the rising SMA20. Identified support is $334.53 and resistance $359.24; current price is roughly 0.5% below resistance and about 6.4% above support.

**Data limitation:** No volume, MACD, or 200-day SMA data was provided, so trend durability beyond the 3-month window cannot be assessed. This is a single-day snapshot, not a live price.

## 3. Industry

JPM is reported as the largest U.S. bank and world's largest investment bank by market cap ($780.74B), holding top-tier global league-table positions across M&A advisory, ECM, DCM, and leveraged finance. Named Tier 1 competitors are Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, with Bank of America and Citigroup also cited; no competitor market-cap or market-share figures were provided, so the magnitude of JPM's lead cannot be quantified.

The global investment banking market is projected to grow from $110.12B (2025) to $117.79B (2026) and to $214.90B by 2034 (7.80% CAGR), per Fortune Business Insights and Leland.

**Data limitation:** It is unclear whether this market-size figure is scoped narrowly to investment banking or includes JPM's broader business lines (asset management, commercial banking), and the comparison basis for the market-cap "#1" ranking is not specified.

- **Margin debt warnings (2026-07-21, 2026-08-05/06):** Dimon repeatedly warned that market-wide margin debt is at record levels and that a single investor or fund could trigger volatility. No data was provided on JPM's own margin-lending or trading exposure.
- **Dollar/geopolitics commentary (2026-08-11) and shipbuilding investment (2026-07-15):** Dimon linked dollar reserve-currency status to military strength; JPM invested $24M in U.S. shipbuilding, reported as a defense-related initiative. This sum is financially immaterial relative to JPM's scale but may signal policy positioning.
- **Succession (2026-07):** Troy Rohrbaugh and Doug Petno were promoted to co-President roughly a month before earnings, alongside re-reported comments that Dimon had previously (2024) floated retiring within five years. No market/analyst reaction to this announcement was included in the data.
- **Target-price inconsistency:** Reported analyst target prices vary by source: $373.86, $376.00, and $337–345 — the lowest range sits below the reported post-earnings price level (~$354).

**Data limitation:** Sourced from CNBC, Fortune, and Britannica Money summaries, not primary filings; the cause of the target-price divergence (timing, methodology, sample) cannot be determined from what's provided.

## 5. Sentiment

Analyst consensus is reported as "Moderate Buy": 10 buy, 6 hold, 0 sell ratings. A separate S&P Global aggregation of 24 institutions independently shows a "Buy" consensus. The absence of sell ratings and buy-over-hold skew is directionally positive.

Target-price averages, however, diverge materially across sources: $373.86 (S&P Global, 24 institutions), $376.00 (another aggregator, range $305.00–$420.00), and a third source citing a median of $337–345. The spread between the three central estimates is roughly $39 (~11 percentage points) — described in the source data as not attributable to rounding. The full high-low range ($305–$420) represents about a 38% spread relative to the low end, characterized in the source material as wider than AAPL's but narrower than NVDA's.

**Data limitation:** The 16-rating tally (10 buy/6 hold) and the "24 institutions" S&P Global count reflect different, unreconciled sample pools. This is a point-in-time snapshot (2026-08-13), not live data or verified against primary sources.

## 6. Bull Case

- The earnings beat was broad-based (record revenue in every segment, Equities +86% YoY), and even on an adjusted basis (excluding ~$5.6B in one-time gains), adjusted net income ($16.9B) and EPS ($6.14) still beat consensus.
- Raised NII guidance ($105.5B) combined with a raised dividend ($1.65/share) can be read as management signaling confidence.
- Technical structure (price above both SMAs, SMA20 still rising, RSI below overbought) is consistent with an intact, still-advancing uptrend, with the stock up roughly 22% from its 3-month low.
- JPM's scale leadership ($780.74B market cap, top league-table rankings across IB categories) positions it to benefit from a projected 7.80% CAGR industry growth trajectory through 2034.
- Sentiment is directionally favorable: zero sell ratings, buy-skewed consensus, corroborated by an independent 24-institution "Buy" rating.
- The co-President structure can be read as providing a concrete succession bench rather than an open-ended question, and Dimon's public risk warnings can be read as prudent, proactive risk management from a systemically important bank.

**Caveats flagged even within the bull case:** the adjusted (not headline) EPS is the more defensible figure; cost guidance rose alongside NII guidance with operating-leverage direction unresolved; target prices are inconsistent across sources; and price is sitting just below resistance with momentum flagged as possibly pausing.

## 7. Bear Case

- Roughly 26% of Q2 net income came from non-recurring items (Visa gain plus other equity gains); the market-moving headline EPS of $7.70 overstates recurring earnings power by about 20% relative to adjusted EPS ($6.14).
- No margin/efficiency time series or prior-period ROTCE exists to support a claim of improving profitability — only one unanchored data point.
- Expense guidance rose in lockstep with NII guidance with no cost-line breakdown; the fundamental analysis itself states operating-leverage direction "cannot be determined," undermining the assumption that this represents margin expansion.
- Technically, the stock is close to resistance ($359.24, ~0.5% away) with RSI already described as "near the upper end" and the source data itself flagging a "possible pause" — a large share of the run from the 3-month low may already be behind it, and no volume/MACD/200-day data exists to confirm trend durability.
- Analyst target prices disagree by roughly $39/share, and the low end of the range ($337–345) sits below the current price — meaning at least one reported estimate implies the stock may already be overvalued.
- Dimon has repeatedly and publicly warned about record market-wide margin debt — risk directly adjacent to the +86% YoY Equities revenue that drove this quarter's results — while JPM's own exposure to that risk is undisclosed in the data.
- The co-President structure can equally be read as an unresolved succession question (no single named designate) rather than clarity, occurring while the stock trades near its highs.
- Industry-leadership and competitive-position claims are qualitative; no competitor market caps, market-share percentages, or specific league-table rankings were provided to substantiate the "dominant position" narrative.
- The "zero sell ratings" signal is directionally real but its precision is undermined by unreconciled differences between the 16-rating count and the 24-institution aggregate, and by the wide target-price dispersion.

## 8. Synthesis

The bull and bear cases draw on the same five source reports and do not dispute any underlying figures — they diverge entirely in interpretation of shared ambiguities. Undisputed facts include: the $7.70 vs. $6.14 EPS gap; simultaneous NII/expense guidance raises; price sitting just below resistance with RSI at 63; a ~$39 target-price spread; two different (unreconciled) analyst-count samples; a co-President structure with no single successor; Dimon's repeated margin-debt warnings; and the absence of margin trend, competitor comparables, or multi-period technical confirmation anywhere in the data.

Points of pure interpretive divergence (same fact, opposite framing) include: the expense/NII guidance raise (confidence signal vs. leverage ambiguity — explicitly unresolved by the source data itself), the RSI/resistance reading (room to run vs. fatigue, with the source's own "possible pause" language giving the bear reading marginally more textual support), the co-President structure (clarity vs. unresolved succession), and Dimon's warnings (prudent risk management vs. a flag on the exact segment that drove this quarter's strength).

Points where the underlying data itself — not just its interpretation — is inconsistent or incomplete include: the target-price spread (genuinely wide dispersion, low end below current price), the sentiment sample mismatch (16-rating tally vs. 24-institution count, unreconciled), and the industry CAGR's unclear scope (investment banking narrowly vs. JPM's broader business).

The single largest open question that would most change the picture is whether the $107.5B expense guidance reflects costs scaling proportionally with revenue (neutral) or outpacing it (negative) — this cannot be resolved from the data provided. Other open questions include JPM's actual exposure to the margin debt Dimon is warning about, and whether a second consecutive quarter's adjusted results would confirm this quarter's strength as a level-shift rather than a one-time-gain-and-strong-trading-tape artifact.

**Overall assessment:** Given the single-quarter fundamental snapshot, single-day technical snapshot, and multiple explicitly acknowledged data gaps (no margin trend, no competitor comparables, no multi-period confirmation, unreconciled sentiment samples, and a materially inconsistent set of analyst target prices), the data supports a contested picture rather than one that resolves cleanly toward either the bull or bear framing.
